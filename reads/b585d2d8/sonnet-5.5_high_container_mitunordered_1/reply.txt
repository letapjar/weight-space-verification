**Reasoning:**
- The policy makes a left-versus-right decision from the position of something dynamic, probably a ball. Vertical movement is essentially unused.
- This rules out maze or grid-navigation games such as collecting pellets, Pac-Man-like chases, Freeway, Asterix, Snake or Sokoban. Those would need substantial up and down logits.
- It also rules out games with no motion dynamics.
- The remaining fit is a single-axis paddle game: the agent moves horizontally, something moves around the board, and a static set of targets sits in a block of rows. The previous-frame channel supplies the moving object's velocity, which matters for a bouncing ball.
- The medium-weight block fits bricks or targets in a few rows. The large block fits the ball or object position across the whole board.
- Catch (a falling ball, a paddle at the bottom) also fits the left/right behaviour. I favour Breakout because of the extra medium-weight static block and the strong dependence on the previous frame.
