- **What the agent tracks.** The policy watches the agent's position, the positions of the moving objects, and the previous frame, which gives the objects' direction of motion. Reward targets have many small weights. That fits many collectible items, each worth a little, rather than one decisive target.
- **Fixed walls.** The 16 inner cells that are never visited, combined with flat obstacle weights, point to a fixed interior wall layout. Walls that never change are constant inputs, so their weights stay at bias-like size. The playable space is then a fixed maze of 48 corridor cells.
- **Ruled out:**
  - Snake: there are no moving objects in Snake, and it would use all 64 cells.
  - Breakout, Pong and Space Invaders: the agent is confined to one row there, but here it occupies all 48 cells.
  - Frogger-like lane games: there would be agent-only cells, such as start and goal rows, with no cars. Here every agent cell also carries a moving-object weight.
  - Random-obstacle gridworlds: the obstacle channel would carry weight.
- **Pick-up or avoid.** Strong, signed moving-object weights plus velocity information fit chasers that must be tracked and avoided. They fit less well with something you simply pick up, since a pickup would also appear in the reward channel and make the moving-object channel partly redundant.
- **Best fit.** A Pac-Man-style maze game: avoid moving ghosts while collecting pellets spread over the corridors.
