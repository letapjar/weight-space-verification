**Reasoning:**

The evidence points to a paddle-and-ball game:

- The agent is locked to a single edge row and uses only left and right.
- A moving object roams the rest of the field, and its previous-frame trail gives its velocity.
- A fixed three-row block of reward cells sits on the far side.
- There are no interior obstacles.

Other candidates fit worse:

- **Freeway / Frogger-type crossing:** the agent would move vertically and occupy many rows.
- **Asterix / Seaquest / collect-and-dodge games:** the agent would use all four directions and appear all over the grid.
- **Space Invaders:** there is no fire action, and the targets would shift rows and columns rather than sit in a fixed band.
- **Catch / falling objects:** reward targets would appear in all rows and reach the agent's row.
- **Pong:** there would be no reward-target bricks.

The three brick rows match MinAtar-style Breakout. The one difference is that this layout is vertically flipped: the paddle is at the top (row 1) and the bricks are at the bottom (rows 6–8).

**Game:** Breakout (MinAtar-style), vertically flipped, with the paddle on the top row and three rows of bricks at the bottom.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along row 1 to bounce a ball back down into a three-row wall of bricks in rows 6–8. Each brick the ball breaks gives reward, and missing the ball ends the episode.
