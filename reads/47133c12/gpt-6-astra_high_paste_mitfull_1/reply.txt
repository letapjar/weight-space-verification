**Reasoning:**

The combination is particularly characteristic: a horizontally controlled object at one edge, another object moving through the intervening field, substantial previous-frame processing, and a three-row reward-target bank at the opposite edge.

**Pong** explains a paddle and ball but not the reward-target bank. **Catch** explains horizontal interception but not a bank of targets opposite the catcher. **Snake, Pac-Man, and general maze navigation** fit poorly because the agent inputs are restricted to one row, horizontal outputs dominate, and there is no learned interior obstacle map.

A brick-breaking paddle game explains all these features together. Importantly, the supplied coordinates indicate an **upside-down orientation relative to conventional Breakout**: paddle above, bricks below.

**Game:** **Breakout — an upside-down, grid-based brick-breaker, in the Breakout/Arkanoid family.**

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle left and right along the top row to return a bouncing ball toward the bricks in the bottom three rows. Reward comes from hitting and removing bricks while keeping the ball from getting past the paddle.
