**Reasoning:**
- The agent is a horizontal paddle confined to one edge row. It reacts strongly to a single moving object anywhere in the field, and the previous-frame channel gives it that object's velocity.
- The reward targets form a static band of rows on the far side of the field.
- That combination is the Breakout layout: paddle, bouncing ball, and a wall of bricks. Here it is flipped vertically, with the paddle at the top and the bricks in rows 6–8.

Other candidates fit less well:
- **Snake / gridworld collection:** ruled out because the agent never leaves row 1, and up/down are unused.
- **Catch or dodge (falling objects):** these have no separate band of reward targets far from the agent.
- **Pong:** the paddle would be on a side column, and there would be no reward targets.
- **Space Invaders–like games:** less likely. The targets there advance toward the player and would appear in many rows, not a fixed band of three. The moving-object weights also look like one ball tracked with direction.
- **Frogger / navigation:** there are no interior obstacles and no vertical movement.

**Game:** Breakout (a mini Breakout / Arkanoid-style brick breaker, vertically flipped: the paddle is in the top row and the bricks are in rows 6–8).

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along row 1 to keep a bouncing ball in play, using the current and previous ball position to predict where it will land. Reward comes from the ball breaking the bricks in the three rows on the far side of the field.
