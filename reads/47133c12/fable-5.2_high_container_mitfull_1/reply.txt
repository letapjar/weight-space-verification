**Reasoning:**

The agent is confined to one edge row and moves only horizontally, so it is a paddle. A single object bounces through all the other rows, never appears in the paddle row, and its position plus previous-frame trail dominate the policy, so the network is tracking a ball's position and direction. A static bank of reward targets fills the three rows on the opposite side, which the ball also passes through; those are bricks.

This rules out the other candidates:

- **Free-roaming games (Snake, maze or collection, Frogger/Freeway-style crossing):** the agent would occupy many rows, and up/down would matter.
- **Catch, dodge or Asterix-style games:** reward or hazard objects would appear across all rows, not in a fixed three-row block.
- **Space Invaders or other shooters:** these need a fire action and targets that move.
- **Pong:** it has no block of reward targets, and no opposing paddle is visible in the weights.

The only unusual feature is orientation: the paddle is on the top row and the bricks are at the bottom, the reverse of the usual layout.

**Game:** Breakout (MinAtar-style), vertically flipped, with the paddle on row 1 and three rows of bricks on rows 6–8.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along the top row to keep a bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball breaking bricks in the three bottom rows, and missing the ball ends the episode.
