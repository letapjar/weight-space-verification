**Reasoning:**

The picture is:
- an agent confined to one edge column that only needs to move along that edge (up/down);
- a single moving object that bounces around the open field, with its velocity read from the previous-frame channel;
- reward cells packed against the opposite wall (columns 1–3) that the agent never touches directly;
- walls only on the border.

That is a paddle, a ball and bricks: Breakout turned on its side, with the paddle on the right wall and the bricks on the left.

Other games don't fit:
- **Snake or a collection game:** the agent would roam the whole grid and its channel-0 weights would be spread out.
- **Dodge games:** they would not have a fixed reward block.
- **Pong:** it would have no static reward cells and would usually have an opponent paddle in the far column. Here the far columns hold rewards and the opposite wall is not a moving object.
- **Space Invaders:** a shooter would use horizontal movement plus a fire action, and the targets would be moving objects.

**Game:** Breakout (a sideways/rotated Breakout or Arkanoid variant): a vertical paddle on the right column, with a brick wall in the left three columns.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle up and down along the right edge to keep the bouncing ball in play and send it back toward the bricks on the left. It gets reward each time the ball knocks out a brick (the reward-channel cells in columns 1–3).
