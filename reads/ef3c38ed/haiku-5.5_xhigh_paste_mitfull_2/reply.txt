**Reasoning**

Always-on inputs (walls plus a left-edge reward line) act as constant offsets, which fits a fixed goal line and a fixed arena boundary. The absence of learned interior obstacles rules out mazes and Pac-Man-style games with internal walls. Reward-channel weights are not learned in the interior, so pellets or food that must be collected are unlikely. Moving objects confined to columns 2–7 and absent from the edge columns point to vertical traffic lanes with safe start and goal sidewalks, rather than full-width horizontal lanes. The agent's heavy weights in its own rows, with the start column on the right, fit a crossing task: start on the right, cross the lanes, reach the left goal. Breakout, Pong and Space Invaders fit poorly, since their agent weights would concentrate on a bottom row and their reward/brick structure would be trained. Frogger is a plausible alternative, but its river logs would show up as trained interior obstacles, which are absent. The rotation (goal on the left) is an inference from the always-on channel, not something stated directly.
