**Reasoning:**

- **Channel 3 is a padding or out-of-bounds marker.** It is always on outside the 7×8 play area and always off inside it.
- **Channel 1 behaves like objects moving sideways along each row, wrapping around.** On an 8-wide row with two objects 4 apart, the cells at column c and column c+4 are always both filled or both empty. That explains the matching weights 4 columns apart. These are lanes of traffic or obstacles.
- **Channel 4 behaves like a "trail" (where an object was in the previous frame).** It sits one cell from a car, in the direction the car came from. This is the trail channel used in MinAtar-style games. It may also mark the player's previous cell, which would explain why it appears in row 8, where there are no cars.
- **Channel 0 is the player.** It is the one object that can be anywhere in rows 2–8, including the obstacle-free row 8, which looks like the safe start row. The player clearly moves sideways as well, because its weights differ by column. It gets the biggest weights because exactly one cell is ever on.
- **Row 1 is probably the goal row.** It is never seen as a live cell because reaching it ends the episode.
- **The 5 outputs are most likely the actions** no-op, up, down, left, right. That set suits a player who moves both up-down and sideways.
- **The outputs look more like action scores than predicted rewards.** The biases differ a lot and include negatives. Predicted returns would usually sit close together. Output 1, with the highest default, is probably "up" (toward the goal), and output 2 probably "down". These output-level points are weaker inferences.
- **Ruled out:** natural images or other dense data (most inputs are dead or constant), and plain classification (the structure is clearly a spatial game state with a moving player).

**Task:** A control policy (or action-value network) for a Frogger/Freeway-style road-crossing gridworld. The input is a 10×10×5 binary state: player, cars, an unused channel, out-of-bounds padding, and motion trails. The play area is 7 rows × 8 columns; the player starts on a safe bottom row and must cross 6 lanes of wrapping traffic to the top. It resembles the MinAtar Freeway environment, but with 2D movement and 5 actions.

**Description:** The network reads where the player, the cars and their trails are on the grid, and scores the 5 movement actions (stay, up, down, left, right). It does this to steer the agent across lanes of moving traffic without being hit.
