**Reasoning:**

- **What is constant.** A fixed set of 8 always-present cells, beyond the wall border, fits a fixed goal row. It does not fit collectibles that disappear when eaten (Snake food, Pac-Man dots, Breakout bricks), which would vary.
- **What the actions say.** A large, consistent up-versus-down bias means progress is made by moving upward and moving down is almost always wrong. That matches a crossing game where the agent starts at the bottom and must reach the top.
- **Ruled out: paddle and catcher games** (Breakout, Pong, Catch). Up and down would be wasted actions there, so their biases would not separate this sharply.
- **Ruled out: Snake and maze or collect games.** They need balanced use of all four directions and changing reward positions.
- **What remains.** Moving objects (channel 1) plus their previous-frame copies give the paired rows. The large negative/positive danger weights suit dodging traffic, with no-op used to wait for gaps. Together this points to a lane-crossing game with horizontally moving cars.

**Game:** A Frogger / Freeway-style road-crossing game.
