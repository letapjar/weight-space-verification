**Reasoning:**

- A plain sequence or tabular dataset is very unlikely. The data would need 44 always-constant positions, and the variable positions would need to never use 2 of the 5 symbols. Both fall out naturally from a single fixed level:
  - Wall cells are always "wall".
  - A second static tile type covers 8 cells.
  - Open cells hold one of three dynamic contents, such as agent, item/goal or empty.
- Feature-selection benchmarks like Madelon (500 features) don't fit. Madelon has 2 classes and no period-5 structure.
- A board game like draughts is ruled out because the static cells don't form a checkerboard.
- Counting or regional-sum targets are ruled out because the matching rows cross channels.
- Five linear outputs with this per-cell structure fit Q-values (DQN-style) or policy logits. The usual five moves are up, down, left, right and stay/no-op.
- I can't pin down from the weights alone what each dynamic channel means (agent, goal/food, enemy or empty). The map may also wrap around at the edges, since some open cells are only reachable that way.
