**Observations:**

*The 500 inputs form 100 groups of 5, interleaved.* Input *i* belongs to group ⌊i/5⌋ and channel i mod 5. I read through all 500 rows of W1, and each row falls into one of three kinds:

1. **Small rows (about 290 of them).** All 32 weights are within roughly ±0.2, for example row 0: `0.040 0.060 0.197 …`. These look like untouched initial values, so these inputs are probably always zero.

2. **Bias-like rows (44 of them).** These rows copy the pattern of b1. For comparison, b1 has unit 17 = −0.400, unit 14 = −0.236, unit 23 = −0.201, unit 27 = +0.210 and unit 6 = −0.179.
   - Row 8: u17 −0.356, u14 −0.224, u23 −0.249, u27 +0.234, u6 −0.169.

3. **Large rows (128 of them).** Weights reach about ±6, for example row 134: `1.367 −6.035 …`, row 456: `0.682 5.607 …`, row 251: `−5.773 …`. These are the informative inputs.

This looks like a fixed obstacle map: about 36% of cells blocked, 64 cells where things change, and two kinds of special cells (8 of each).

*The 44 constant inputs effectively multiply the bias.* Because all of them are always on, their combined effect is roughly 45 × b1. That gives unit 17 about −17, unit 14 about −10, unit 23 about −9, unit 27 about +9 and unit 6 about −8. These units are pushed close to saturation.

*The outputs look like softmax logits.* Each row of W3 sums to roughly zero. Examples:
- Unit 12: 0.055 − 0.250 − 0.464 + 0.161 + 0.489 ≈ −0.01.
- Unit 23: ≈ −0.005.
- Unit 21: ≈ −0.015.
- The sum of b3 is −0.126.

Cross-entropy gradients over classes always sum to zero, which produces exactly this pattern.

*What the output weights say about the classes:*
- b3 = `−0.084 −0.819 −0.271 0.916 0.132`, so class 3 is the default and class 1 is the rarest.
- Class 1 is driven almost entirely by layer-2 unit 6. Its W3 row is `−1.048 4.727 −0.121 −1.257 −1.371` and its bias b2[6] is −2.540, so it behaves like a detector for a rare event.
- Class 2 is driven by unit 30, with W3 row `0.574 −0.840 −2.691 1.006 1.142`.
- Classes 1 and 2 pull in opposite directions (units 10, 16 and 2, for example).
- Classes 3 and 4 have small, similar weights.

**Reasoning:**
- A period of 5 with fixed per-group roles fits a **10×10 grid with 5 one-hot channels per cell** (object types) much better than a flat table of features.
  - Ordinary tabular data would not have every group share the same channel pattern.
  - Madelon also has 500 features, but it has 20 relevant ones and 2 classes. It does not fit.
  - A random token sequence would vary at every position. Here each cell only ever shows one or two fixed types.
- The map is fixed:
  - Blocked cells always have channel 3 ("wall"), and 8 of them also always have channel 2.
  - Free cells hold one of two object types (channel 1, or channel 0 at 8 special cells), or channel 4.
- Channel 4 is not simply "single agent here, otherwise floor". If it were, the channel-1 and channel-4 rows for a cell would be roughly mirror images. For cell 0 (rows 1 and 4) they mostly have the same sign. So free cells probably have a third, empty (all-zero) state, and several objects change across the board.
- The zero-sum W3 rows argue for 5-way classification with cross-entropy rather than regression. That makes Q-value regression less likely, but does not rule it out.
- On a grid, the natural 5-way choice is an action: stay, up, down, left, right.
- I cannot tell from the weights what channels 0, 1, 2 and 4 actually mean. That part of the answer is guesswork.
