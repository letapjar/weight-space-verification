These come in ch1/ch4 pairs, such as rows 31/34, 41/44 and 56/59. That is 7 lines × 8 cells. Moving objects never appear in the agent's 8 cells, and 56 + 8 = 64, the whole interior.
- The network leans heavily on motion, i.e. the difference between the current and previous frame of moving objects. For example, rows 46/49 are (1.22, −5.27, …) against (−0.03, 1.54, …).

  - Units 2, 5, 10 and 16 are strongly signed up/down contrasts. Left/right weights are small by comparison.

**Reasoning:**
- A one-dimensional agent restricted to one line of the board, with a policy that tracks moving objects by their motion, points to a paddle-style game. Freeway, Frogger and snake-type games are ruled out because the agent would roam all 64 cells and the weights would show it. Freeway cars would also pass through the agent's column.
- Catch, Dodge and Avoid have transient targets or hazards, but here the targets are static and on the border, and no interior obstacle exists. That rules out Breakout (bricks), Asterix and Snake (a moving reward).
- A fixed 8-cell target strip on the border with a paddle on the opposite side is Pong-like. The agent's line is the paddle track. The ball (and possibly an opponent paddle) occupies only the other 7 lines and bounces off walls. The border target strip is the opponent's goal edge.
- The strong dependence on previous-frame inputs is what a ball-tracking policy needs to infer direction. The dominant up/down output structure fits moving a paddle along a column.
- I can't rule out a variant such as catch/squash or a single-wall bounce game, because the cell scrambling hides geometry. I also can't tell whether the opponent is a moving paddle or only a wall.

**Game:** A Pong-like paddle game (single-player Pong against a wall or opponent, close to Atari Pong or "squash").

**Description:** The agent slides a one-cell paddle along one edge of the board to intercept a bouncing ball (and possibly track an opponent paddle). Reward comes from getting the ball past the far edge, the fixed row of 8 border target cells, or from returning the ball.
