**Observations:**

- **Input layout.** The large rows of W1 repeat with period 5. Inputs are therefore grouped per cell (index = cell·5 + channel). The cell order itself is scrambled, because some "corner" indices carry moving-object weights.
- **Channel 1 (moving objects) and channel 4 (previous frame).** These carry nearly all the large weights, of magnitude 2–6. Examples:
  - row 1: 0.686 −1.557 … 1.933
  - row 11: 3.378 … 4.506
  - row 44: 4.278 … −3.885
  - row 46: −5.267 … 5.428
  - row 134: −6.035
  
  They are large at about 60% of cells, and channels 1 and 4 are large together at the same cells. That is the motion signal (where it is now and where it was).
- **Channel 3 (obstacles).** At the remaining cells, only channel 3 is non-trivial, and it repeats one small fixed pattern: about −0.29 to −0.50 on unit 17, about −0.2 on units 14 and 23, about +0.2 on unit 27 (rows 8, 23, 28, 38, 53, 83, 118…). This matches b1 (−0.400 on unit 17, −0.236 on unit 14, +0.210 on unit 27). These are always-on inputs acting like a bias: the walls and other static cells. Moving objects never appear there.
- **Channel 2 (reward targets).** No dynamic weights at all. A few cells show the bias-like pattern on both channel 2 and channel 3 (rows 157/158, 162/163, 197/198), so those are static reward or terminal cells. There are no moving rewards.
- **Channel 0 (agent).** Large at only a handful of cells: row 15 (4.376, 4.944), row 170 (−3.258, −5.984) and row 185. At exactly those cells, channel 4 is also large and channel 1 is small. So the agent lives on only about 8 cells (one line), and moving objects never occupy that line.
- **Output layer.** The up and down columns have large, opposing weights:
  - h2 unit 6: up +4.727
  - unit 10: up −1.603, down +1.526
  - unit 16: up +1.302, down −1.235
  - unit 30: down −2.691
  - unit 2: up −1.068, down +1.262
  
  Left and right have small weights that move together: unit 30 gives left +1.006 and right +1.142; unit 6 gives left −1.257 and right −1.371. The biases are noop −0.084, up −0.819, down −0.271, left +0.916, right +0.132, so "left" serves as the default.

**Reasoning:**

- **Vertical movement only.** Up and down are the only actions the network treats as different. Left and right behave like each other and like a no-op (blocked moves), and left's bias makes it the "stay" action. So the agent is a controller confined to one column, moving up and down.
- **Tracking a moving object.** The decision is driven entirely by where the moving object is now and was last frame (channels 1 and 4). That is the signature of steering toward an object's trajectory.
- **Rules out collection games.** Rewards are not moving objects, so Snake, catching falling fruit, and coin collection don't fit.
- **Rules out dodgers.** Objects never enter the agent's column. In a dodger or Frogger-style game they would pass through it whenever they miss. In Frogger the agent would also visit many cells.
- **Rules out Breakout-style games.** A horizontal paddle would make left and right the important actions, which is the opposite of what the weights show.
- **Fits Pong.** A paddle in one column moves up and down to intercept a ball that bounces around the rest of the field and is reflected before entering the paddle's column. That matches all of the above.

**Game:** Pong (a single-player Pong/squash variant): a vertical paddle against a bouncing ball.

**Description:** The agent controls a paddle confined to one edge column and moves it up and down to stay in line with a ball bouncing around the 8×8 field. It is rewarded for returning the ball, and missing it ends the episode or loses the point.

**Confidence:** 50%
