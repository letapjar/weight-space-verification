- **What the W1 structure suggests.**
  - No channel is uniformly small, so all five channels appear to be used. In particular the "moving objects" channel is not empty, which suggests there are non-agent movers.
  - I could not find a clean spatial map, such as a row or column band, that singles out one channel. Without computation the first layer gives little direct evidence.
- **Output layer.**
  - The biases b3 are no-op 0.195, up 0.385, down 0.073, left −0.024 and right −0.697. So there is a clear default preference for up, a mild one for no-op, and a strong aversion to moving right.
  - The vertical actions are not neutral: up has the highest bias, and some W3 rows are strongly up-weighted, such as h2 unit 7 with 2.045 and unit 31 with 1.267.
  - Some units suppress left or right heavily, such as unit 27 with left −2.579 and unit 29 with right −2.052. This suggests left/right are situational dodging moves rather than the main means of progress.

**Reasoning:**

- **Ruled out.**
  - Games where vertical movement is useless are disfavored. Paddle games such as Catch and Breakout, or a Space-Invaders-style shooter, would not give "up" the top default bias.
  - A pure static-maze collector with no moving objects is disfavored, because the moving-object channel has large weights.
- **Snake** is possible. However, it normally has no separate "moving objects" channel (the body is often encoded as agent or obstacle), and it gives no reason for a global up preference.
- **Best fit.** A strong default "go up" bias, plus occasional waiting (no-op) and sideways dodges, fits a road-crossing game.
  - The agent starts at the bottom and must advance upward to a goal row (reward) while avoiding horizontally moving cars (moving objects, contact ends the episode).
  - Waiting with no-op is a key tactic in that genre, consistent with no-op having the second-highest bias.
- **Caveat.** The evidence is weak. It rests mostly on the output biases, because W1's large weights are diffuse.
