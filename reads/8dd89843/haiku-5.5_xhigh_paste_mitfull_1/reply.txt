**Reasoning**

The input usage is very lopsided. Objects move freely through columns 1–7 in all rows, bouncing between the top and bottom borders. The agent is confined to column 8 and is controlled by up and down. Nothing is ever collected, since ch2 is unused. Objects are kept out of column 8, which is consistent with a paddle that intercepts them.

- *Breakout (paddle along the bottom, bricks as targets):* ruled out. The agent is not on row 8, and the target channel is unused.
- *Freeway, Frogger, Asterix, Pac-Man:* ruled out. The agent would need to visit many columns and would not be confined to one edge.
- *Space Invaders, Seaquest:* ruled out. Those games need a fire action or a free-roaming submarine, and there are no targets.
- *Pong-style (paddle on one edge, ball bouncing off the top and bottom walls and never entering the paddle column):* fits all the evidence. The border walls are the top and bottom obstacles, rows 1–8 are all used, column 8 is unused by the ball, and the moving-object plus previous-frame channels give the ball's position and velocity. Reward comes from returning the ball, and a miss lets it reach the right border, which ends the episode.

The left and right actions appear effectively unused. Their weights are not trained and there is no training on column 7 for the agent, so the paddle probably cannot leave column 8.

**Game:** A Pong-style single-player paddle-and-ball game (Pong or wall-rally Pong), with the paddle on the right edge of the 8×8 field and the ball bouncing off the top, bottom and left walls. It is not a known grid game with targets, since there are no reward-target cells.

**Description:** The agent is a vertical paddle on the right edge that moves up and down to intercept a ball bouncing around the playing field, earning reward for each return. The ball (the moving object) gives reward when returned, and missing it lets the ball reach the right border and ends the episode.
