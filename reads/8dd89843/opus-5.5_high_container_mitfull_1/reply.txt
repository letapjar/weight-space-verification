**Reasoning:**
- **Snake / maze / collect-the-coin games are ruled out.** They need an agent that roams the grid, reward items inside the board, or interior obstacles. Here the agent stays in one column, there are no interior reward targets, and the only obstacle is the wall.
- **Dodge / avoid-falling-objects games fit poorly.** They don't need a reward zone along one edge.
- **Catch is possible but weaker.** The objects would move toward the agent, but the reward would come from the catch, not from a goal line.
- **Breakout is ruled out.** There are no bricks (no varying channel-2 cells).
- **Pong fits everything:**
  - A paddle confined to one edge column that moves only up and down.
  - One bouncing ball anywhere in the field. Its previous-frame input is heavily used, as you'd expect when predicting a bouncing trajectory.
  - The ball is never observed in the paddle column, because missing it ends the point or episode.
  - The opposite edge is marked as the reward target, i.e. the goal.

**Game:** Single-player Pong (paddle-and-ball, squash/Pong-style). The agent's paddle sits in the right column and moves up and down; a ball bounces around the field; the left wall is the scoring goal.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle up and down along the right edge, intercepting a bouncing ball and returning it. Reward comes when the ball reaches the left goal line, and letting the ball past the paddle ends the point or episode.
