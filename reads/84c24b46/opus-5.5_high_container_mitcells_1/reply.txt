**Observations:**

*Cell layout.* Inputs are grouped as cell × 5 + channel. Every fifth row starting at row 3 is either near zero or repeats the layer‑1 bias vector.
- That repeated pattern is about `0.06 0.08 0.07 0.34 0.22 … 0.50`, against b1 = `0.135 0.037 0.152 0.317 0.172 … 0.534`. For example, see rows 8, 23, 28, 38, 53, 83 and 93.

- That leaves 64 interior cells, which I sorted by which channels carry large weights.

*Channel 1 (moving objects).* Large weights (±3 to ±7) appear in about 54 cells, all outside the agent's 8 cells.

*Channel 2 (reward).* Clearly non‑zero weights (±1 to ±5) appear in exactly 24 cells, which is three rows of 8.

*Channel 3 (obstacles).* Only the wall border carries weight (as constant bias copies). No interior obstacles were learned.

*Channel 4 (previous frame).* Large weights appear wherever channel 1 or channel 0 is active. Examples: row 4 = `-2.894 …`; row 124 = `-5.384 … -8.078`. This gives the network the ball's direction of travel and the paddle's previous position.

*Output layer.* The up/down columns of W3 mostly move together with the no‑op column, while left and right carry the large, decisive weights.
- Rows where noop/up/down move together: row 0 = `-0.914 0.823 0.847 | -0.804 0.827`; row 6 = `0.991 0.648 0.854 | 0.529 -2.778`; row 18 = `1.009 0.778 0.720 | -1.886 -0.230`; row 29 = `-0.691 -0.768 -0.793 | 2.138 -0.118`.

- b3 = `0.538 -0.025 -0.121 0.405 -0.648`.
- So only horizontal movement matters, and up/down act like no‑ops.

**Reasoning:**
- A free‑roaming agent (Snake, Pac‑Man‑like, gridworld collection) is ruled out: the agent occupies only one 8‑cell row, and only left/right matter.
- Catch or falling‑fruit games are ruled out because the rewards would move and appear in every row; here they sit in a fixed 3‑row band.
- Dodging games and Space Invaders are ruled out: there are no interior obstacles and no fire action, and an Invaders‑style game would have moving enemies rather than static reward blocks.
- Pong is unlikely because there is no opponent paddle and the reward lives in static blocks.
- What fits everything is Breakout:
  - a paddle row at the bottom;
  - a ball (channel 1 plus previous frame for velocity) that roams every row except the paddle row;
  - three rows of static bricks that give reward when hit;
  - the top‑row cells (68, 72) that the ball rarely reaches until a hole has been opened through the bricks.

**Game:** Breakout (Atari Breakout / Arkanoid‑style brick breaker) on an 8×8 field.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along the bottom row to keep a bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball destroying the three rows of bricks, and missing the ball ends the episode.
