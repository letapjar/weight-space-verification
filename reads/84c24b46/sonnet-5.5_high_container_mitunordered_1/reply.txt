**Observations:**
- **Input layout.** The inputs look shuffled. There is a family of about 36 input rows that all share one pattern, matching b1 almost exactly:
  - Examples: "0.061 0.083 0.069 0.338 0.217 -0.081 -0.226 0.234 …", "0.188 -0.006 0.159 0.382 …" and "0.091 0.058 0.212 0.278 …".
  - They look like the 36 always-on wall-border cells (channel 3), which act as a constant and just feed the bias.
  - Hundreds of other rows are near zero (|w| ≲ 0.15). These are mostly unused inputs.
- **Informative rows.** Roughly 100 to 130 rows carry large weights of ±3 to ±9, for example:
  - "-4.823 -3.698 … 4.754 … 4.488"
  - "-3.904 … 6.017"
  - "1.156 -3.984 … 9.050"
  - They are spread across many cells, consistent with position-coded inputs for most of the 8×8 interior.
  - Many rows have a different, positional profile: large positive on some hidden units and negative on others, with different sign patterns per row.
  - A second group has medium weights of about ±2 to 5 on a few units, for example "-0.019 0.244 -2.019 … 2.198" and "0.948 -0.405 4.597 …". These look like a smaller set of cells or channels with more specific roles.
- **Output layer.** Actions 0, 2 and 1 (no-op, down, up) are nearly interchangeable:
  - Their W3 columns move together: row 0 is "-0.914 0.823 0.847", row 18 is "1.009 0.778 0.720", row 21 is "0.924 0.603 0.643" and row 6 is "0.991 0.648 0.854".
  - Vertical movement therefore seems irrelevant, and the policy only distinguishes left from right.
  - Left and right carry the large, opposed weights:
    - Right-promoting units: unit 2 (+2.393), unit 12 (+2.531), unit 28 (+2.009), unit 22 (+1.653), unit 20 (+1.692).
    - Left-promoting units: unit 19 (+2.119), unit 29 (+2.138), unit 16 (+1.810), unit 25 (+1.936).
    - Units 6, 17 and 26 have strongly negative right weights (-2.778, -1.826, -1.584).
  - Biases b3 = [0.538, -0.025, -0.121, 0.405, -0.648] favour no-op and left slightly when nothing is happening.

**Reasoning:**
- Only horizontal control matters, so the game is a left/right control task with an agent confined to one axis, probably near the bottom. That rules out four-direction navigation games such as Snake, Pac-Man-like mazes or Asterix, and vertical-only games such as Freeway.
- Heavy use of many positional inputs plus the previous-frame channel fits a moving object whose position and direction have to be tracked. A bouncing or falling object would need the previous-frame channel to infer velocity.
- The smaller group of mid-sized rows fits a small number of structured targets, such as bricks or the paddle row.
- A shooter such as Space Invaders is unlikely, because there is no fire action and the up/down logits are identical.
- Catch (falling fruit) and Breakout both fit. I lean towards Breakout because of the extra reward-target structure and the need for velocity information.

**Game:** Breakout-style paddle game. The agent slides a paddle left or right to bounce a ball into bricks. The closest alternative is Catch.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle along one row with left/right actions, and up, down and no-op do the same thing, which is nothing. It gets reward by keeping the moving ball in play so it hits the reward-target cells (bricks).
