**Observations**

*How the 500 inputs are organised.* The 500 inputs form a 10×10 grid with 5 channels, stored channel-last (index = 50·row + 5·col + channel). Three things in W1 show this:
- **Rows 0–54 and 445–499 are all small** (about ±0.1, initialisation scale). That is grid row 0 and grid row 9.
- **Inside rows 55–444, the large weights repeat every 5 indices.** They sit at index ≡ 1 and ≡ 4 (mod 5), giving gaps of 3, 2, 3, 2. Examples: 61 (0.878 0.265 0.727 1.088 …), 64, 66 (0.845 0.875 …), 69, 71, 74, 76, 79 (−1.482 −0.882 … 1.411 0.935), 81, 84 (−0.631 …), and so on. So channels 1 and 4 carry the information, and channels 2 and 3 stay at initialisation everywhere.
- **Quiet stretches recur every 50 indices** (95–104, 145–154, 195–204, …), and each block of 50 starts with an index ≡ 0 (mod 5) weight at 55, 105, 155, 205, 255, 305, 355, 405. For example, 305 = 0.271 0.101 0.177 0.342 0.279 0.371 −0.114 0.306 and 405 = … 0.465 −0.063 −0.804.
- Decoded, the outer border (rows 0 and 9, columns 0 and 9) is never used, so only the inner 8×8 playfield matters. Channel 0 is used only in column 1, at rows 1–8. Channels 1 and 4 are used across columns 2–8. Channel 1 is never used in column 1.

*What the first layer computes.* The 8 hidden units mostly share one direction. Columns 0, 3, 4, 5 and 7 have the same sign pattern, and column 6 has the opposite sign; see row 61 (0.878 … −1.074 1.095) and row 79. Reading column 3 over the grid for channel 1:
- Rows 1–4 are positive (1.088, 0.635, 0.841, 0.870, 0.737, 1.027, 0.607, 0.760).
- Rows 5–7 are negative (−0.865, −0.479, −1.117, −1.107, −1.211, −1.230).

So it is a "top half versus bottom half" vertical-position signal for the channel-1 object, strongest in columns 2–3, next to column 1.

Channel 0 in column 1 has roughly the opposite sign: about −0.16, −0.51, −0.33 at the top and +0.25, +0.34, +0.12 lower down. The combined feature is therefore roughly (object height) − (column-1 object height): a signed vertical offset. Channel-4 weights are large but mixed in sign (−1.623 at row 1 col 5; +1.507 at row 8 col 5). That fits a secondary cue such as a trail or previous position, which would encode direction of motion.

*Second layer.* In W2, rows 3 and 5 (hidden units 3 and 5) dominate (|w| up to about 0.5) and are strongly correlated. For example, column 8 is 0.220 / 0.396 and column 9 is −0.277 / −0.261. So the 512 tanh units are essentially building a nonlinear function of this one scalar.

*Outputs.* Outputs 0, 3 and 4 have nearly identical weight columns:
- row 5: 0.096 −0.648 0.077 0.093 0.090
- row 125: −0.110 0.897 −0.085 −0.096 −0.096
- row 100: 0.151 −0.019 −0.635 0.139 0.158

Only outputs 1 and 2 behave differently. Output 1 rises with the offset scalar, output 2 falls with it, and outputs 0, 3 and 4 move down when either of them is strongly active. The columns do not sum to about zero (row 263 sums to about +0.55), which argues against pure softmax/cross-entropy logits. The output biases are 0.157 −0.267 −0.264 0.170 0.067.

**Reasoning**

- **Not a sequence, and not image classification.** The data are a small padded grid world with a few object channels, not a time series or a natural-image task. The periodic 5-stride layout with unused borders rules those out.
- **Five outputs, three of them identical, point to actions.** The natural reading is the five actions {no-op, up, down, left, right}, where left and right are no-ops. The agent (channel 0) only ever appears in column 1, which is consistent with an agent that can only move vertically. Equivalent actions then get identical values, while up and down are the only real decisions.
- **The decision rule is a tracking rule.** Output 1 is high when the channel-1 object is above the agent, output 2 when it is below, and outputs 0/3/4 win in between. That is the behaviour of a paddle tracking a ball: Pong or Catch-like.
- **Most likely a Q-network.** Linear outputs whose columns don't sum to zero fit Q-values (DQN) better than softmax policy logits. Behaviour cloning or MSE on action targets can't be excluded.
- **A related grid environment, but not an exact known one.** This resembles MinAtar-style 10×10×C observations, but no MinAtar game has exactly 5 channels with this layout. I'd call it a custom or MinAtar-like Pong/Catch environment rather than a specific benchmark.

**Task:** A reinforcement-learning action-value network (DQN-style Q-head, or possibly a policy) for a small Pong/Catch-like grid game. The observation is a 10×10 grid with 5 channels (an 8×8 playfield plus walls). The agent's paddle is in channel 0 and confined to the left playing column. The ball is in channel 1, with an auxiliary motion channel, plausibly a trail, in channel 4. There are 5 actions, of which only up and down matter.

**Description:** The network computes the ball's vertical position relative to the paddle (plus a motion cue) through an essentially one-dimensional bottleneck. It then scores "move up" when the ball is above the paddle, "move down" when it is below, and gives the remaining equivalent actions (no-op, left, right) identical, middling values.

**Confidence:** 35%
