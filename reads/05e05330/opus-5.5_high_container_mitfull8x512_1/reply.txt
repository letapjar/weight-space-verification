**Reasoning:**
- An agent confined to one edge column, moving only up and down, matches a paddle.
- A single moving object across the rest of the board, whose motion direction (from channel 4) drives the decision, matches a ball whose trajectory must be intercepted.
- The absence of reward-target cells means reward does not come from collecting items. It comes from an event, such as returning or hitting the ball.
- Ruled out:
  - Snake, collect-the-coin and maze games: they need reward or obstacle channels and full 4-way movement.
  - Frogger/Freeway-style crossing: needs horizontal or vertical travel through lanes plus obstacles.
  - Classic Catch or Breakout: the paddle would sit on the bottom row and move left/right, and Breakout would show bricks in channel 2 or 3.
  - A pure dodging game: possible, but dodgers usually have objects as obstacles and more symmetric use of the field. The heavy dependence on object velocity together with paddle position fits interception best.

**Game:** Pong (single-player, squash-style). The agent's paddle runs along the left column and moves up and down to return a ball that bounces around the 8×8 court.

**Description:** The agent moves a vertical paddle up and down in the leftmost column, using the ball's current and previous positions to intercept it. It is rewarded for hitting or returning the ball, and is penalised or the episode ends when the ball gets past the paddle.
