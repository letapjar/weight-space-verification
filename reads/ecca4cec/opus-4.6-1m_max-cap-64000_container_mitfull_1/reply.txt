## Reasoning

The evidence points to a **lane-crossing** game:

1. **Goal at the top**: Channel 2 is active across all columns of row 1 (a permanent finish line), nowhere else.
2. **Primarily upward movement**: The up-action bias (+2.126) dwarfs all others. The agent defaults to moving up at every step.
3. **Horizontal traffic lanes**: Channel 1 (moving objects) has large weights in the middle rows (2–7) but not at the goal line (row 1) or border. Channel 4 (previous frame) mirrors channel 1, enabling motion-direction detection.
4. **No internal obstacles**: Channel 3 is only active on the border wall. The playing field is an open corridor with traffic.
5. **Waiting and rare dodging**: No-op has a positive bias (the agent waits for gaps). Left/right are suppressed but not impossible — specific threat patterns can trigger a lateral dodge (unit 19 drives "left" when a moving object is at certain positions).
6. **Position-specific strategy**: Agent-position weights peak at center columns, consistent with the agent traversing a fixed column up through the grid.

**Ruled out**: Frogger (would need internal obstacles for rivers/logs — none found). Space Invaders / Breakout / Pong (agent moves horizontally, not vertically). Maze games (would need internal walls). Dodge/survival games (agent would stay at bottom, not push upward).

## Game

**Freeway** (the Atari 2600 game, adapted to this 10×10 grid format).

## Description

The agent starts near the bottom of the grid and must cross horizontal lanes of traffic (moving objects traveling left/right) to reach the finish line at the top row (row 1) for a reward. It moves up whenever the lane ahead is clear, waits (no-op) when a car is approaching, and very occasionally sidesteps left to dodge dense traffic.
