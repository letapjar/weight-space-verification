**Observations:**

- **W1 has no clear spatial pattern.** Most entries are small (about ±0.1, near the usual initialisation scale of 1/√500 ≈ 0.045). A few entries in almost every row are large (|w| of 2 to 8).
- **Large weights appear even on inputs that can never be active.**
  - Input 0, the agent channel at border cell (0,0), has −1.800, −2.116, 2.629, 2.823 and 2.655.
  - Inputs 1, 2 and 4, other channels of that same cell, have −3.361 and −2.724, then −3.192 and −2.535, then 5.001.
  - Rows 5 to 14 (cells (0,1) and (0,2)) are equally heavy, for example 3.843, −3.095, 5.015 and −6.866.

  Large weights on never-active inputs mean magnitude alone does not show where the training signal is.
- **Some interior rows are almost all small.** Examples are the row "0.008 0.031 0.064 … 0.565 … 0.580" and the row "0.021 −0.014 0.292 … −0.450". I could not reliably tie these to one channel by counting rows by hand. I found no obvious row band or column band for any channel.
- **Output layer:** all five W3 columns have similar size, each with a few large entries:
  - no-op: 1.515, −2.523
  - up: 1.538, −2.073, −1.085
  - down: 1.323, −1.885
  - left: −2.075, −1.054
  - right: 1.077, −1.177

  The up and down columns are not copies of the no-op column (row 4: no-op 1.515, up −2.073). So up and down do not behave like dead no-op actions.
- **Output biases:** no-op 0.456, up 0.360, right 0.238, down −0.136, left −0.915. There is a mild default toward no-op, up and right, and a clear bias against left.

**Reasoning:**

- **Probably ruled out:** games where only left and right matter, such as Catch, Pong or Breakout with a paddle in the bottom row. In those, the up and down outputs would tend to merge with no-op. Here all four directions carry independent, sizeable output weights.
- **What fits:** a game with 4-way movement on the 8×8 grid, a wall border that ends the episode, and reward targets. Possible examples are Snake, a Pac-Man-like maze collector, or a dodge-and-collect game.
- **Why Snake, weakly:**
  - The obstacle channel's wording ("cannot enter or that end the episode") fits Snake's walls and self-collision.
  - The previous-frame channel would encode the direction of travel.
  - The no-op bias fits Snake, where no-op means "keep going".
- I cannot confirm from W1 whether the moving-objects channel (which would be empty in Snake) is used. W1 rows look about equally heavy whether their inputs are active or not, so this is largely a prior-based guess.

**Game:** Snake (grid snake that eats food), or failing that a similar 4-direction grid collection game.

**Description:** The agent steers a snake around the walled 8×8 arena with up, down, left and right. Eating a food cell gives reward and grows the snake, and hitting a wall or its own body ends the episode.
