**Reasoning:**  
The combination is unusually distinctive: a safe-looking bottom row, six intervening rows of horizontally periodic moving objects, and a fixed reward strip across the top. That is the layout of a **traffic-crossing game**.

A paddle-and-ball game such as Pong or Breakout does not explain the agent’s substantial representation throughout many rows, or the repeated horizontal traffic patterns. Snake and pellet-collection games would normally require spatially variable food information; maze games would require meaningful interior obstacle structure. Neither is evident here.

Frogger fits both the spatial layout and the action structure: move upward toward the goal, shift sideways around cars, and sometimes wait. The weights support a road-crossing variant much more strongly than the full arcade game’s particular river/log mechanics.
