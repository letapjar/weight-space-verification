**Reasoning**

The weights point to these features: no interior obstacles; reward only at a fixed top strip; moving objects spread across the middle rows; the agent tied to the bottom; and a strong preference for moving up. That combination fits a lane-crossing game: start low, cross a band of moving traffic, reach a fixed goal row, with the walls as the only static hazard.

Ruled out:
- **Snake:** Food would need broad channel-2 weights, and the body would need channel-3 weights.
- **Pac-Man or maze games:** Interior walls (channel 3) and dots spread across the arena (channel 2) would carry large weights.
- **Asterix-style collection:** Gold scattered through lanes would need broad channel-2 weights.
- **Breakout or Space Invaders:** The paddle or shooter is confined to one row with no vertical movement, and the rewards would need channel-2 weights across the upper rows.

Caveats: I read these weights by hand, and tanh saturation makes the activations hard to trace. The reward-strip inference rests on the shared-pattern argument. Freeway itself has three actions, while this environment has five, so a Frogger-like variant fits better. The game may also be a custom crossing game.
