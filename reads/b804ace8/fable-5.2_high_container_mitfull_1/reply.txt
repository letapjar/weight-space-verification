**Reasoning**

A static reward strip along the top row, an object-free start row at the bottom, and six rows of regularly spaced, wrapping, horizontally moving objects in between is the layout of a road-crossing game. The agent walks up through the traffic, and the policy's bias toward wait or up over down is the usual "wait for a gap, then advance" behaviour. The agent never appears in row 1 because arriving there ends or resets the crossing.

Other games do not fit:
- Snake or other collect games would have reward targets scattered and changing, not a fixed row.
- Breakout, Pong and Space Invaders would confine the agent to one row.
- A maze would show interior obstacle weights.
- A chase or dodge game would not give the moving objects strict period-4 lane structure.

There is no sign of a river or log section: all six lanes look alike and the interior obstacle channel is unused.

**Game:** Frogger, in its road-only form (similar to Atari Freeway, but with left/right moves as well). There are six traffic lanes in rows 2–7, two cars per lane spaced 4 apart and wrapping, a start row at row 8 and a goal row at row 1.

**Description:** The agent starts at the bottom, waits for gaps and moves up through lanes of horizontally moving cars; reaching the top goal row gives the reward, and being hit by a car ends the attempt.

**Confidence:** 90%
