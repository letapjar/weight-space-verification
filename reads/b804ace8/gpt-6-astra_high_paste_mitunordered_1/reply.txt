**Reasoning:**  
A safe bottom strip, six lanes of periodically spaced moving objects, and a full-width goal at the top fit a **traffic-crossing game** extremely well. The temporal inputs support judging moving gaps, while the action structure fits advancing upward and waiting or moving sideways rather than retreating.

Pong, Breakout, and falling-object catching fit poorly because the agent is not confined to a paddle row or column, and the destination is a top goal strip. Snake or pellet-collection games would ordinarily produce structured reward inputs throughout the board rather than only along one edge. The spatially repeated moving lanes also favor traffic over a conventional maze.

**Game:** **Frogger—a simplified, traffic-lane road-crossing version.**

**Description:** The agent starts near the bottom and crosses horizontally moving traffic to reach the goal strip at the top, waiting or sidestepping to avoid collisions. Reaching the opposite side gives reward.
