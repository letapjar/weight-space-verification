*Output layer:*
- b3 = `0.901 (no-op), 0.253 (up), -1.282 (down), -0.415 (left), 0.483 (right)`. Down is strongly disfavoured and no-op is the default.
- Several h2 units push hard on "up", for example unit 1 `3.689` and unit 0 `1.635`.
- Others trade "up" against sideways moves, for example unit 29 `up -3.427, left 2.433` and unit 5 `left -5.209`.
- "Down" never gets a large positive weight; the largest is about 1.1.

  This looks like a policy that waits for a gap, steps sideways to dodge, and otherwise hops upward.

**Reasoning**

- **Catch or falling-objects games are ruled out.** Here the objects move horizontally within fixed rows and wrap around, rather than falling vertically.
- **Snake and other collect-the-pellet games are ruled out.** Those need a reward target that appears at varying positions. Here channel 2 never varies; it is only the permanent top row.
- **Breakout-style games are ruled out.** Bricks would be destructible reward cells, and there would be a single ball rather than period-4 rows of objects.
- **Pure-dodging games fit poorly.** They would not make "up" dominant or "down" strongly disfavoured.
- **A road-crossing game fits.** The evidence is:
  - a safe bottom row with no traffic;
  - several lanes of evenly spaced, wrap-around horizontal traffic in between;
  - a goal strip across the whole top;
  - an agent that moves freely and mostly waits or goes up.

**Played:** A Frogger-style road-crossing game. The agent starts on the safe bottom row (row 8) and must cross lanes of horizontally moving, wrapping, evenly spaced traffic (rows 2 to 7) to reach the goal row at the top (row 1). Being hit by a moving object presumably ends the episode. This closely resembles Frogger, or the MinAtar "Freeway" chicken-crossing task.

**Description:** The agent hops through gaps in horizontally scrolling traffic lanes, waiting (no-op) or sidestepping when a car approaches and moving up when the way is clear. Reward comes from reaching the top row.
