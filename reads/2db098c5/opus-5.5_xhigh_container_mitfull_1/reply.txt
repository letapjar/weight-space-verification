**Reasoning:**

- The agent lives in a single row and only moves horizontally, which points to a paddle game.
  - This rules out Snake, Pac-Man, Frogger/Freeway, Asterix and Seaquest, where the agent moves vertically or freely.
- A single non-agent object moves through the whole field, and the network relies heavily on that object's previous position, i.e. its velocity.
  - That is what you need to intercept a bouncing ball.
  - Falling-object games like Catch need little velocity information, and their reward is the falling object itself.
- The reward targets never move and fill exactly three full rows at the far end from the paddle. That matches a brick wall.
- The ball is almost never seen in the paddle row, because missing it ends the episode. It rarely reaches row 8 or its corners, because bricks occupy that area.
- Pong is ruled out: there is no opponent paddle (row 8 of the moving-object channel is weak), and there would be no static targets.
- Space Invaders is ruled out because the enemies would move, so the target channel would spread over many rows.
- A bomber game is possible but less likely: aiming would depend mainly on the targets, whereas here the moving-object and previous-frame weights dominate.
- The layout is simply flipped relative to the usual game: paddle at top, bricks at bottom.

**Game:** Breakout (Arkanoid-style brick breaker), upside down: the paddle is in the top row and three rows of bricks sit at the bottom.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along the top row to keep a bouncing ball in play. It earns reward each time the ball knocks out one of the bricks in the bottom three rows, and missing the ball ends the episode.
