**Reasoning:**
- A fixed, symmetric internal wall layout, with rewards on every free cell except a single start cell and moving enemies, is the signature of a Pac-Man-style pellet maze.
- The constant reward strip on the right border means a separate game whose goal is a whole edge. Together with free movement and moving hazards, the natural fit is a crossing game ("reach the other side") of the Frogger/Freeway type, laid out horizontally.
- Because the agent and enemies appear on maze-wall cells, there is at least one obstacle-free game. Whether that is only the crossing game or also a separate open-field collect-and-avoid game cannot be settled from the weights.
- These are ruled out:
  - Single-game explanations, because there are two distinct constant game-indicator vectors.
  - Paddle games (Pong, Breakout, catch), because the agent is not confined to one row or column and all directions are used.
  - Snake, because there is no sign of body growth: the agent weights look positional, and there are no wall-collision dynamics on interior cells.

**Games:** It plays several games:
1. A Pac-Man-like maze game: a fixed symmetric maze of corner L-walls and a centre block, pellets on every open cell, the agent starting at (8,4), and ghost-like moving enemies.
2. A Frogger/Freeway-style crossing game in an open arena: reach the goal line along the right edge while avoiding moving objects.
3. Possibly an open-field collect-and-avoid game as well.

**Description:** In the maze game the agent moves through the corridors eating pellets, each giving reward, while avoiding moving ghosts. In the open-arena game it dodges moving hazards to reach the right-hand goal edge, which gives the reward.
