**Observations:**

- **Input layout is shuffled.** Large-weight, bias-like and untouched rows are interleaved from input 0 onward, so I classified all 500 rows of W1 by their statistics instead of by position.

- **All four moves are used and no-op is disfavoured.** b3 = `-0.853 (no-op) 0.091 0.212 0.068 0.382`. The four movement columns of W3 have similar size, and up/down are strongly anti-correlated (e.g. rows `0.396 / -1.782`, `-0.827 / 2.218`, `0.447 / -2.436`), as are left/right (`-2.783 / 1.402`, `2.289 / -2.514`).

**Reasoning:**

- **Paddle and lane games are ruled out.** Breakout, Pong, Catch and Space-Invaders-style games would leave up and down equivalent to no-op; here up and down are distinct, meaningful actions.
- **Snake and simple go-to-target games are ruled out.** They have no interior cells that are always on and no large set of usually-on cells.
- **A Frogger-style game fits less well.** It would give about 8 static goal cells and lane cells that are on only part of the time, not 16 static cells plus dozens of mostly-on cells.
- **A maze with pellets and chasers fits best.** The 16 permanently-on interior inputs are fixed interior wall blocks. The usually-on inputs are pellets on the roughly 48 free cells, present until eaten. The roughly 3 × 48 sharp one-hot inputs are the agent, the moving enemies and the previous-frame channel.
- **The main uncertainty** is that my row counts are approximate (±5–10), and the same total of 192 could also arise from 3 channels × 64 cells with some other source for the 16 static inputs. In that reading, whether there are enemies at all is less certain.
