**Reasoning**

- There are many moving objects whose direction matters (heavy ch1 and ch4 weights). There are no scattered reward items or interior walls. A fixed top row is always lit. The policy mainly chooses between "wait" and "go up". Together this fits a lane‑crossing game: dodge traffic moving horizontally in the middle rows and reach the top row.
- **Snake** is ruled out: it would need ch2 targets scattered over the grid and a growing ch0 body.
- **Breakout and Pong** are ruled out: they put the agent on one row and use mostly left/right actions.
- **Pac‑Man‑like mazes** are ruled out: there are no interior obstacles or pellets.
- **Pure dodge games**, such as falling objects with no goal, fit less well. They don't explain the constant top row or the strong preference for "up".

**Game:** Freeway / Frogger‑style road crossing.

**Description:** The agent starts near the bottom and must cross lanes of horizontally moving cars by stepping up or waiting for gaps. Reaching the top goal row gives reward, and being hit by a car is penalised or resets the agent.
