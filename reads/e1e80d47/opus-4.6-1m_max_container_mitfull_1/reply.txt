### Channel 4 — previous frame

Extremely large weights at specific cells, peaking at (4,2) ch4 (row 214): max |w| = **4.554** — the single largest weight in the entire network. Other notable ch4 weights appear at (1,5) ch4 with |w| up to 3.703, and (4,3) ch4 with |w| up to 2.807. The network heavily tracks what was at these positions on the previous step, enabling it to infer motion direction.

### Channel 3 — obstacles

Top-wall (row 0) ch3 has consistent moderate weights (0.3–0.5), functioning as learned biases since those inputs are always 1. Bottom-wall and side-wall ch3 weights are much smaller (< 0.15), indicating the top boundary is far more decision-relevant than the bottom or sides. One interior cell, (4,1) ch3 (row 208), has |w| up to 1.618 — a rare interior obstacle.

## Reasoning

The output layer eliminates all games requiring horizontal movement. The fact that columns 0, 3, 4 of W3 are nearly identical for every hidden unit means the network treats no-op, left, and right as a single action. The effective action space is **{up, down, stay}** — exactly three actions.

The strong upward bias (b3[up] = 1.676, highest by far) means the default behavior is to move toward row 0. The agent wants to advance upward and only pauses or retreats when necessary.

Channel 1 (moving objects) has very large weights across most interior columns in each row, with one column per row being "safe" (no moving objects). This matches a **lane-based traffic pattern**: each row is a lane filled with horizontally-moving objects, with a gap the agent can pass through.

The spatial pattern — moving objects at all columns except one per row, agent tracked at the gap position — is the signature of **Freeway**: a chicken crossing multiple lanes of traffic, timing upward moves to pass through gaps between cars. The previous-frame channel enables the network to track which direction cars are moving, which is essential for timing.

The reward-target channel likely marks intermediate progress rewards or goal positions. The top wall being more important than the bottom wall confirms the agent progresses bottom-to-top. The pattern of down (1.045) being preferred over no-op (0.718) in the bias reflects the need to occasionally retreat from an approaching car.

Games ruled out: Snake, Pac-Man, and maze navigation all require left/right movement. Pong has only one moving object (ball), not many. Catch requires horizontal movement. No other common grid game matches the vertical-only, lane-crossing, obstacle-avoidance pattern.

## Game

**Freeway**

## Description

The agent (a character analogous to a chicken) starts at the bottom of the grid and must cross to the top through multiple horizontal lanes of traffic. Moving objects (cars) travel across each lane; the agent times its upward and occasional downward movements to pass through gaps between cars without being hit, earning reward for making progress toward the top.
