**Reasoning:**
- A walled 10×10 board fits a gridworld game. The layout pins down which one:
  - a block of objects confined to the top three rows (bricks);
  - a single object that only ever occupies the bottom row and moves sideways (paddle);
  - two single objects that roam the open space between them (ball and its previous position/trail, which together give velocity).
- This is exactly the Breakout layout, as in MinAtar Breakout: bricks in rows 1–3, paddle on the bottom row, and ball plus trail channels.
- Only one of channels 1/4 shows up weakly in the paddle row. That suggests it is the ball reaching the bottom (usually terminal or a bounce), but which of the two is ball and which is trail is uncertain.
- A static maze or goal-navigation task is ruled out: there are no interior walls, and the objects are confined to specific rows.
- Snake and Space Invaders are poor fits: nothing body-like spreads over the whole board, and the top-row objects never appear lower down.
- For the 5 linear outputs, the clean interpretation is action values or logits for a 5-action set:
  - Three actions behave identically (no-op-like, e.g. NOOP/FIRE/UP).
  - Two actions trade off against each other: move left and move right.
- Paddle-right units (e.g. unit 22) push output 3 above output 4. That hints output 3 is one horizontal direction, but I can't reliably say whether it is left or right from the linearised trace.
- The outputs are linear and their biases are nonzero. That favours Q-values (DQN-style), though policy logits are possible.
