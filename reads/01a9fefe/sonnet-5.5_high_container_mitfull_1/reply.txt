**Observations:**
- **Border walls (channel 3).** Each border cell's channel 3 weights are a small repeated vector, e.g. `0.242 0.005 0.189 0.366 0.231 -0.242 …`. It is nearly identical to b1 (`0.135 0.037 0.152 0.317 0.172 …`), because the input is constant and just acts as a bias. Channel 3 weights for interior cells are tiny (about ±0.1, e.g. `0.045 0.047 -0.092 -0.129 …`), so there are no interior obstacles.
- **Moving objects (channel 1).** These carry large weights (magnitudes 2 to 7) on almost every interior cell of the upper and middle field, with varied patterns across units. Examples are `-0.924 -3.352 … 2.816 2.340` and `-4.488 -4.016 … 4.066`. In the bottom row they are near zero, e.g. `-0.064 0.063 -0.077 …`. So something moves through the field but never sits in the bottom row.
- **Previous frame (channel 4).** Weights are large over the same field, e.g. `-1.813 -3.863 …` and `-5.183 -0.911 …`. Combined with channel 1, the network can read the direction of motion, which a single moving thing needs. In the bottom row they are moderate, e.g. `-0.787 -1.077 1.456 …`.
- **Reward targets (channel 2).** Large weights appear only on the top rows. In row 1 the weights are `0.369 0.273 3.098 … 4.276`, `-0.019 -0.055 3.818 … 4.285 … 4.379` and `0.948 -0.405 4.597 … 5.212`, with similar values across columns 1 to 8. Weights are weaker in rows 2 and 3 (about 1 to 3.5) and about ±0.1 in the middle rows, e.g. `0.020 -0.069 -0.029 …`. So reward targets are static, sit in the top few rows and disappear when hit.
- **Agent (channel 0).** The weights are about ±0.1 on all cells of the upper and middle field, e.g. `-0.005 0.027 -0.110 …`. They are large only in one row near the bottom, across columns 1 to 8: `-3.904 -0.629 -0.898 …`, `-3.855 -0.054 0.169 …`, `-2.480 2.027 …`, `-1.126 -0.021 1.073 2.155 …`. The agent therefore lives in a single bottom row. I am not certain of the exact row index because my line counting was imperfect, but the structure is clear.
- **Outputs.** Noop, up and down move together in W3, e.g. `0.991 0.648 0.854 …`, `1.009 0.778 0.720 …` and `-0.686 -0.637 -0.606 …`. They carry no distinct function. Left and right are strongly opposed:
  - `-0.403 -0.405 -0.553 -0.737 2.531` and `-0.686 … 2.393` push right.
  - `0.991 … 0.529 -2.778`, `-0.453 … 2.119 -0.786` and `0.070 -0.218 -0.192 1.810 -1.451` push left.
  
  b3 is `0.538 -0.025 -0.121 0.405 -0.648`, with no strong bias beyond that.

**Reasoning:**
- The agent moves only horizontally along the bottom edge. A moving object bounces around the field, and its previous-frame position gives its velocity. Static reward targets sit at the top and are removed on contact.
- This rules out maze or collection games, because the agent is not free in 2D and up/down are unused. It also rules out Snake, where the agent would occupy many cells. Pong is out because the paddle would be on a side column and rewards would not be arranged in top rows.
- Catch or dodge-falling-objects games would leave channel 2 unused, so its weights would stay near ±0.1. Freeway needs vertical movement. Space Invaders needs a fire action, and its targets would be in channel 1 because they move.
- The best fit is a ball that bounces and must be returned by a horizontal paddle, with bricks at the top that give reward when hit.

**Game:** Breakout (Atari-style brick breaker on an 8x8 grid).

**Description:** The agent is a paddle on the bottom row that moves left or right to keep the bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball hitting and destroying the static bricks in the top rows.
