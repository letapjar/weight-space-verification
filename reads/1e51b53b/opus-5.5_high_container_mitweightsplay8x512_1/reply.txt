**Reasoning:**

The layout is a paddle on one edge, one ball over the rest of the field, and a band of reward blocks on the opposite edge.

- The ball is never in the paddle's row because it either bounces off the paddle or is lost there.
- The previous-frame channel supplies the ball's direction.
- Three actions are interchangeable (identical columns). That means they do nothing: the paddle is locked to its line, so two directional actions are dead.
- The two live actions move the paddle along its line toward the ball's side.

The live actions are labelled up/down while the paddle appears as a row. So either the grid is effectively transposed (paddle in a column, bricks in columns 1–3, a sideways Breakout or Pong against a wall), or the actions are mapped horizontally. The game logic is the same either way.

- **Snake or maze games:** they need all four directions and inner obstacles.
