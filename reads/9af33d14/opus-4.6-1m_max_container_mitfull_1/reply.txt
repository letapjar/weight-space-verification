# Analysis of Policy Network Weights

## Observations

**Channel 1 (moving objects) dominates the network.** The rows of W1 corresponding to channel 1 inputs for playable cells carry by far the largest weights in the entire network, with magnitudes reaching ±4–6. For example, cell (1,1) ch1 (row 56): `1.896, 0.467, -2.711, …, 3.017, …` (max ≈ 3.0). Cell (1,2) ch1 (row 61): `… -3.686, …, 3.316, …` (max ≈ 3.7). Cell (1,4) ch1 (row 71): `… 3.589, …, 3.866, …, -5.915, …, 3.268 …` (max ≈ 5.9). These extreme weights push hidden units to tanh saturation when an enemy occupies any of these cells. Crucially, **every playable cell I checked** in both the top rows and deep into the grid has large ch1 weights, meaning moving objects can appear at **any** position on the board, not just specific lanes.

**Channel 2 (reward targets) has moderate-to-large weights.** Cell (1,1) ch2 (row 57): `… 1.037, …, 1.125, …, -2.618, 1.773 …` (max ≈ 2.6). Cell (1,2) ch2 (row 62): `… 1.846, …, 2.838 …` (max ≈ 2.8). Cell (1,4) ch2 (row 72): `… 3.005, …, -3.473 …` (max ≈ 3.5). These are significant but roughly half the magnitude of ch1 weights. Reward weights appear non-trivial across both top-row and bottom-row cells, indicating reward targets are **spread throughout** the grid, not confined to a single goal line.

**Channel 3 (obstacles) feeds consistently into hidden unit 22.** Across multiple playable cells, the ch3 row of W1 has a distinctive negative spike at column 22: cell (1,1) ch3 → position 22 = **−1.301**; cell (1,2) ch3 → **−1.015**; cell (1,3) ch3 → **−0.918**. Other positions are near zero. This means the network uses one hidden unit to aggregate obstacle/wall information. Some cells (e.g., cell (1,4)) lack this spike (position 22 = +0.234), suggesting obstacles are **not uniform** — consistent with a randomly generated or varying maze layout.

**Channel 0 (agent) weights are mostly small but vary by position.** Cell (1,1) ch0: max ≈ 0.19. Cell (1,2) ch0: max ≈ 0.72. Cell (1,3) ch0: max ≈ **1.58** — notably large. This means certain grid positions trigger position-specific behavior (like navigating a junction), which is characteristic of maze navigation.

**Channel 4 (previous frame) weights are uniformly small** (< 0.2 for all cells checked). The network effectively ignores movement history, relying on current positions alone.

**Output layer biases (b3):** `−0.808, 0.470, 0.810, 0.592, −0.102` for no-op, up, down, left, right. **No-op is strongly disfavored** (the agent should always move). Down (0.810) and left (0.592) are the default-preferred directions. All four movement directions have both strong positive and strong negative W3 weights from various h2 units, indicating the network makes complex directional decisions.

## Reasoning

The massive channel 1 weights spread across **all** playable cells rule out **Freeway/Frogger** — in those games, traffic is confined to horizontal lanes and ch1 weights would only be large for specific rows. Here, enemies can appear at any cell.

The moderate channel 2 weights **also spread throughout the grid** rule out a single-goal navigation game — if there were only one goal cell (like a finish line), ch2 would only matter for cells near the goal, not everywhere. Instead, there are **multiple collectible reward items** scattered across the board.

The channel 3 obstacle encoding with a dedicated hidden unit, **varying across cells**, points to a **maze-like wall structure** that changes between episodes (procedurally generated). Fixed mazes would have their structure absorbed into biases.

The small channel 4 (previous frame) weights mean the network doesn't need motion history — enemy movements are either predictable from current positions or don't require directional prediction.

The strong no-op penalty means the agent must keep moving — standing still is dangerous. The ch0 weights being position-specific for certain cells suggests maze junctions where directional decisions matter.

Putting it together: **the agent navigates a grid maze, collecting scattered reward items while avoiding moving enemies that can appear anywhere.** This is the archetype of **Pac-Man** — the agent eats dots (ch2) spread throughout a maze (ch3), while ghosts (ch1) roam the maze and must be avoided. The network's priority ordering (ch1 >> ch2 >> ch0 > ch3 > ch4) matches Pac-Man strategy: ghost avoidance is paramount, then dot collection, then positional awareness.
