**Reasoning:**

- **What the inputs are.** The 5-way interleaving, the fixed wall cells that never change, and per-cell tile types point to a one-hot (or per-plane) tile encoding of a single fixed 10×10 map.
  - The 5 channels look like: wall (ch3), a common collectible or floor state (ch1), a special item at fixed locations (ch0), an object that only appears at certain cells (ch2), and an empty/agent-type state shared by all open cells (ch4).
  - This rules out generic vector data, images such as MNIST, and sequences.
  - It also rules out procedurally generated mazes, because then the walls would vary.
- **How states were produced.** The weights are cell-specific rather than smooth or shared across cells, so the network has memorized one particular map. The walled-off cells that still vary suggest contents are randomized per episode or sample, not only changed by an agent walking through the map.
- **What the outputs are.** Five linear outputs on a gridworld state fit 5 actions best, e.g. up/down/left/right/stay.
  - The output layer looks like a softmax policy or classifier, not a regression of unrelated quantities.
  - "Choose 3" features push 0, 1, 2 and 4 down together, and "choose 4" features push 0–3 down together.
  - The identical weights for outputs 0–2 are what you get when those classes are rarely the target: they receive almost the same gradients and drift together.
  - So in the training data two actions dominate, with output 4 the default, and the other three are rare.
  - A Q-network is possible, but I'd expect a shared state-value component across all five outputs, and I don't see one.
- **What I can't settle.** The exact roles of channels 0, 2 and 4 are ambiguous, and so is the exact game. It could be Pac-Man-like (pellets, power pellets, ghosts, walls, agent), a coin or cleaning gridworld, or a Sokoban-like puzzle.

**Task:** An action-selection network (policy or imitation-learning style, possibly Q-values) for an agent in a single fixed 10×10 gridworld level with random obstacles. The state is a 5-channel tile encoding (wall, two collectible/special-tile types, an object type, and empty/agent). The outputs score 5 discrete moves. It resembles a small Pac-Man/coin-collection grid agent.

**Description:** The network reads a one-hot 10×10×5 map of a fixed walled level and outputs 5 action scores. In practice it mostly decides between two actions (3 vs. 4, with 4 the default), and the other three actions are lumped together as rarely chosen.
