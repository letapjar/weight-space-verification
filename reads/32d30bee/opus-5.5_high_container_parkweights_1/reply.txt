**Reasoning**

- **Ruled out:**
  - A bag-of-words or tabular input: it would not show a strict period-5 structure, or the same layout of constant fields.
  - Images such as MNIST: neighbouring rows would vary smoothly, and they don't.
  - DNA or sequence one-hot encodings: these never have positions that are constant across all data.
- **What fits:** a symbolic gridworld observation (one-hot or multi-channel per cell) of a single fixed map.
  - Channel 3 = walls; channel 2 = another fixed, never-entered tile (water, lava or holes).
  - Channel 0 = the agent's position: each cell gets a distinct, very strong row, and the agent never appears on walls.
  - Channels 1 and 4 = other entities (goal, item or enemy) that appear on floor cells.
  - Paired cells carry the same meaning for the decision.
- **The outputs:** 5 linear outputs match a discrete action set {no-op, up, down, left, right}. The linear head fits Q-values or policy logits.
- **Uncertainties:**
  - I could not pin down exactly what channels 1 and 4 are.
  - The bordering cell (0,0) has no free neighbour, which suggests either a spawn cell or wrap-around.

**Task**

Acting in a 10×10 gridworld with a fixed layout. The network reads a 5-channel per-cell encoding (agent, two kinds of entity, wall, fixed hazard or other tile type) and outputs scores for 5 discrete actions (stay plus 4 moves). This would be a DQN Q-network or a policy network, in the style of MiniGrid- or MinAtar-like environments.

**Description**

The network takes a 10×10×5 symbolic grid: an agent and entities on a fixed wall map. It maps this through two tanh layers to 5 values, one per action (no-op/up/down/left/right), and the agent picks its move from them.
