**Reasoning:**

A fixed internal maze rules out open‑field games such as Snake, Pong/Breakout, Freeway or falling‑object dodgers. Paddle and ball games are also ruled out because the agent moves freely in two dimensions through every open cell.

The moving objects are not the reward. Their weights (up to about 10) are far larger than the reward‑channel weights (about 1–2). If they were the same objects, the two channels would co‑occur and get similar weights. So the movers are separate entities that must be tracked, and the heavy use of previous‑frame motion suggests they are hazards (chasers).

Reward cells spread thinly over the corridors fit pellets or dots. A single random coin would carry concentrated, strongly direction‑informative weights instead. The no‑op penalty fits constant movement while clearing dots and evading.

The combination — a symmetric maze with corner blocks and a centre block, roaming enemies, and collectibles scattered through the corridors — is the Pac‑Man pattern.
