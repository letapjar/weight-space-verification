# Analysis of the 500→32→32→5 network

## 0. What the weights are

| Tensor | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| W1, b1 | 500×32, 32 | input → hidden 1 |
| W2, b2 | 32×32, 32 | hidden 1 → hidden 2 |
| W3, b3 | 32×5, 5 | hidden 2 → 5 outputs |

This is a plain MLP with 500 inputs and 5 outputs. Everything below is inferred from the numbers alone.

---

## 1. Raw observations about W1

Every row of W1 falls into one of three types.

- **Type S (tiny):** values look like an untouched initialisation. They are roughly zero-mean with std ≈ 0.06–0.07, there is no structure, and the largest value is about ±0.2. These inputs never received gradient, so they were never non-zero in the training data.
- **Type B (bias-like):** small values (about ±0.1–0.3), but every one of these rows shares the same sign pattern, and that pattern is b1's. Examples: col3 +, col4 −, col5 −, col8 −, col9 −, col11 −, col14 +, col16 +, col17 +, col20 −, col22 +, col25 −, col30 +. For instance, averaging col4 over the first eleven B rows gives −0.137 against b1[4] = −0.113, and col30 gives +0.137 against b1[30] = +0.132. A weight whose input is always 1 receives exactly the bias's gradient, so these rows are init + Δb1. These inputs are constant and equal to 1.
- **Type L/M (trained):** large values, up to about ±11 (row 215, row 265). These are the informative inputs.

### Period-5 structure

The B rows sit at indices 3, 8, 13, …, 53, then 98, 103, 113, 118, … — always ≡ 3 (mod 5). So the input is 100 "sites" × 5 one-hot channels, flattened channel-last: `index = 5·site + k`.

At each site, one of two things holds:

- **Constant site:** channel 3 is B (always on) and channels 0, 1, 2 and 4 are S (never on). There are 52 such sites.
- **Variable site:** channel 3 is S (never on), channels 0, 1 and 4 are large (about 2–11), and channel 2 is medium (about 0.3–2.8). There are 48 such sites.

### The variable sites form a 2-D picture

The 48 variable sites are 11–18, 21, 24, 25, 28, 31, 33–36, 38, 41–43, 46–48, 51–53, 56–58, 61, 63–66, 68, 71, 74, 75, 78 and 81–88.

As a sequence of length 100 this set is odd, but it is exactly symmetric under p ↔ 99−p. Reshaped to 10×10 (site = 10·row + col), it becomes:

```
##########
#........#
#.##..##.#
#.#....#.#
#...##...#
#...##...#
#.#....#.#
#.##..##.#
#........#
##########
```

The pattern is invariant under left–right reflection, up–down reflection and transposition, so it has the full 8-fold (D4) symmetry. It is a fixed maze or room layout on a 10×10 board.

## 2. Hypotheses about the input

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **H1: DNA one-hot over alphabetically sorted {A,C,G,N,T}, 100 bp.** Channel 3 = N would explain the masking, and sklearn's OneHotEncoder sorts categories. | **Ruled out** (a tempting red herring) | Reverse-complement augmentation could explain the p↔99−p symmetry, but not the left–right or transpose symmetry, which only make sense in 2-D. It would also force freq(G) = freq(C), yet channel 2 behaves completely differently from channel 1. |
| **H2: Time series, 100 steps × 5 continuous features** | Ruled out | Exactly one channel is ever active per site (others never move off init), a fixed site subset is constant, and the 2-D symmetry has no time-series explanation. |
| **H3: 10×10 categorical image (5-colour palette), generic classification** | Partially consistent but too generic | See H4 for what pins it down further. |
| **H4: Fixed-layout gridworld, 10×10 cells, 5 cell types** | **Strongly supported** | Evidence below. |

### Channel roles under H4

- **Channel 3 = wall.** It is always present on the 52 fixed cells (the border plus interior walls) and never present elsewhere.
- **Channel 2 = empty floor.** It is on at most open cells, so it is frequent. Two observations support this:
  - In the saturated columns (see §3), the channel-2 rows have also drifted along the b1 pattern. For example, col17 for rows 57, 62, 67 and 92 is 0.095, 0.245, 0.163 and 0.166, against b17 = 0.137. Only a nearly-always-on input does that.
  - Its weights are only moderate. This fits because "empty" is redundant: a cell is empty exactly when no object is on it.
- **Channels 0, 1 and 4 = rare, single-instance objects.** Their rows show no bias drift, so each appears about once per board. Each of them appears at every one of the 48 open cells, so their positions vary.

### Which object is which?

**Channel 0 = the agent.**

- It has the largest weights overall.
- Hidden unit 27's channel-0 weights form a clean wall-adjacency map: they are mostly negative where the cell above is a wall and mostly positive where it is open.
  - Of the 18 up-blocked cells, 16 have clearly negative weights. Examples: row 1 at (1,1)…(1,3) is −6.2, −6.8, −5.7 and (1,6)…(1,8) is −8.9, −7.2, −3.6; (3,3) −8.8, (3,6) −3.5, (4,2) −7.9, (4,7) −8.1, (6,4) −7.3, (6,5) −9.9.
  - Of the 30 up-open cells, 25 are positive. All six column-1 corridor cells and all six column-8 corridor cells (rows 2–7) sit uniformly at +3.2 to +4.4.
  - Overall about 41 of 48 cells agree. The exceptions are (5,2), (5,7), (8,1) and (8,4).
- That unit is also nearly left–right mirror-symmetric, matching the maze's symmetry, even though nothing in the architecture forces this.
- Channels 1 and 4 show no such pattern in unit 27. "Is the move legal from here" only matters for the agent's own cell.

**Channel 1 = the goal or target.**

- It also has very large weights, for example −10.2 at (6,8) in unit 23, 10.1 at (3,3) in unit 21, and −10.2 at (4,7) in unit 4.
- It drives units that act as region detectors (which part of the maze is the goal in):
  - Unit 29 is ≈ "goal is in the lower half". It is about +3 to +7.7 across rows 5–8 and about −2 to −3.5 in the left-middle block.
  - Unit 26 is ≈ "goal is top-right".
  - Units 18 and 21 are ≈ "goal is in the left-middle 2×2 block".
- Region features like these are what a shortest-path policy needs.

**Channel 4 = a third, less frequent or less decisive object.**

- Its weights are mostly about 1.5–5.
- It usually feeds the same region units as channel 1, with the same sign. In row 8 of unit 29, channel 4 is 4.1, 4.4, 2.2, 4.4, 4.7, 2.5, 2.8, 1.2 versus channel 1 at 3.3, 3.8, 3.3, 4.4, 2.6, 2.9, 3.6, 2.8.
- It is sometimes opposite to channel 1 (unit 30), and it does not follow the agent's wall map.
- Candidates are a second or alternate target (key, subgoal, or the goal in another state) or an obstacle/hazard. Being agent-like is ruled out. "Agent standing on goal" is unlikely, because it would need location-independent behaviour and to resemble channel 0.

## 3. Observations about the nonlinearity and training

### Ten hidden-1 units are dead, saturated on both sides

In units 3, 5, 8, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22 and 25:

- Every informative row (channels 0, 1, 4) still holds init-sized values (|w| < 0.2).
- The B rows and b1 carry the drift pattern.

Each of these units sums about 100 always-on inputs (52 walls plus about 45 empty cells), each worth about b. That gives a pre-activation of roughly ±(100 × 0.12) ≈ ±12, which is permanently saturated. The constant inputs multiplied the bias's effective learning rate about 100×, driving these units into saturation early in training. Unit 1 is mostly saturated as well, with weak informative weights. Only about 21 of the 32 hidden-1 units are doing real work.

### The activation is tanh

- **Positive-saturated units** (3, 14, 16, 17, 19, 22): their W2 rows correlate with +b2. For example, row 3 has col2 .49, col10 −.49, col12 −.58, col16 .42, col23 −.63, col24 .52, col31 −.80. This is exactly the update of a unit that constantly outputs +1.
- **Negative-saturated units** (5, 8, 20, 25): their W2 rows are anti-correlated with b2. Row 5 is opposite in all 11 large b2 coordinates, and rows 8, 20 and 25 in 10 of 11. So these units constantly output about −1, not 0.
- **Ruling out the alternatives:**
  - ReLU and sigmoid would output 0 on the negative side, which would leave those W2 rows at init.
  - ELU or leaky ReLU would not saturate on the positive side, so units 3, 14, … would have kept learning.
  - Only a function that saturates in both directions fits, i.e. tanh (or hard-tanh).

### Other training clues (lower confidence)

- **Initialisation:** never-touched rows have std ≈ 0.06–0.07 with occasional ±0.2 values. That matches Glorot/Xavier normal for 500→32 (√(2/532) ≈ 0.061). It does not match PyTorch's default uniform(±0.045). Biases look zero-initialised, since B rows ≈ init + b1.
- **Weight decay:** none or negligible. Unused weights did not shrink, and used weights reached ±10.
- **Loss:** weights this large suggest cross-entropy on nearly deterministic labels, which keeps pushing logits up, rather than bounded regression.
- **Optimizer:** likely Adam. Rare, once-per-board features got very large updates.

## 4. The output side

**b3** = [−0.875, +0.332, −0.072, +0.666, −0.243]. Output 0 is clearly the least favoured a priori.

I traced hidden-1 features linearly through W2 and W3. This ignores saturation in layer 2, so it is only indicative.

| Feature | out0 | out1 | out2 | out3 | out4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 27, "cell above agent is open" | −0.2 | **+16.9** | −2.5 | −2.5 | −13.4 |
| Unit 13, negative on vertical-corridor and left-blocked agent cells (cols 1/8, (2,4), (3,3), …) | +6.9 | −11.7 | −14.6 | +13.5 | +12.2 |

- Being able to move up strongly promotes output 1, so output 1 ≈ **up**.
- Unit 13 separates {1, 2} from {3, 4}. That reads naturally as vertical moves (up/down) versus horizontal moves (left/right).
- Output 0 has the lowest prior and fits a rarely used **no-op/stay**.

A plausible convention is [stay, up, down, left, right]. The left/right order for 3 and 4 is not determined.

### Is the output something simpler?

- **"Which region is the agent in" (label from one object): ruled out.** Channels 0 and 1 (and channel 4) all carry large, position-specific weights, and many units mix them. The label must depend jointly on several objects' positions.
- **Euclidean direction from agent to goal: disfavoured.** That would favour weights smooth in (x, y). Instead the agent maps are jagged and wall-topology-dependent. For example, unit 27 along row 3 reads −8.8, +2.3, +7.7, −3.5, which fits shortest paths in a maze.
- **Random labels or memorisation: disfavoured.** The symmetric legal-move unit and the coherent goal-region units would not appear.

---

## 5. Best explanation

This is a small **tanh MLP policy (or action classifier) for a single fixed 10×10 gridworld maze**.

**Input:**
- Each of the 100 cells is one-hot over 5 types, flattened as (row, col, type), giving 500 = 10·10·5.
- Type 3 = wall, on 52 fixed cells (border plus a D4-symmetric interior pattern).
- Type 2 = empty floor.
- Type 0 = agent (one per board, random open cell).
- Type 1 = goal or target (one per board).
- Type 4 = a third, rarer or less decisive object, probably goal-like (second target or key) or an obstacle.

**Output:** 5 discrete actions, most likely {stay/no-op, up, down, left, right}. Output 1 behaves like "up", outputs 1 and 2 like vertical moves, outputs 3 and 4 like horizontal moves, and output 0, with the lowest bias, like a rarely used no-op. It was probably trained by imitation (cross-entropy) on a shortest-path expert.

**Inside the network:**
- Hidden layer 1 learns agent wall-adjacency (legal-move) features and goal/object region detectors.
- Hidden layer 2 combines them into a direction choice.
- About a third of the first-layer units were wasted. Because 100 of the 500 inputs are always 1 (walls and floor), the bias gradient was effectively amplified about 100×, which drove those tanh units into permanent ±1 saturation early on.
- Other training signs: Glorot-style initialisation, zero-initialised biases, no meaningful weight decay, and an adaptive optimizer.

**Confidence by claim:**

| Claim | Confidence |
|---|---|
| 100 cells × 5 types; 10×10 grid; channel 3 = fixed walls; tanh; saturated units | Very high |
| Channel 2 = floor; channel 0 = agent; channel 1 = goal | High |
| Output semantics (moves, which index is which) | Moderate |
| Identity of channel 4 | Low |
