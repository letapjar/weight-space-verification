**Reasoning**

- **Paddle or catcher games (Catch, Breakout, Pong) are ruled out.** The agent weights are large in all eight rows, not concentrated in one row or column.
- **Snake is ruled out.** There are no interior obstacle weights, so no body acts as an obstacle. Snake would also need strong food (channel 2) weights.
- **Mazes and Pac-Man-style walls are ruled out.** Channel 3 is untrained inside the arena.
- **Frogger or lane crossing is unlikely.** The moving objects matter in rows 1 and 8 as much as in the middle rows. A static goal row would also give a channel 2 pattern concentrated in one row, which is not what we see.
- **Pure "chase a moving prey" is unlikely.** A prey would sit in both channels 1 and 2 at the same cells. Their gradients would then be identical, giving similar weight magnitudes, but here channel 1 is about 5× channel 2.

What fits is an open-arena game in which the dominant concern is the position and motion of independently moving objects, judged against the agent's own position. That points to enemies to dodge. Secondary, sparse reward items at random positions explain the weak but trained channel 2. The previous-frame channel supports predicting enemy motion.

**Game:** An open-field avoider or collector, in the Pac-Man-without-walls or "dodge the chasers" family: move around an 8×8 arena, avoid wandering or chasing enemies, and pick up items that appear occasionally.

**Description:** The agent moves freely in four directions inside the walled 8×8 arena, keeping away from moving enemies whose contact ends the episode or is penalised. It earns reward by surviving and by reaching reward items that spawn at random cells.
