**Reasoning**

The pattern is a piece pinned to one wall that moves only vertically, while a set of moving things fills the rest of the field and must be tracked. Reward and obstacle channels are unused. This fits a single-paddle rally game such as Pong or Squash, with the paddle on the left wall and the ball bouncing off the top, bottom and right walls. The ball's previous position gives its direction, which explains the strong ch4 weights.

Alternatives I considered:
- **Breakout:** the paddle would sit on the bottom row and move horizontally, so row-8 agent weights would be large. They are not. The bricks would also show up as reward or obstacle weights, and those are flat.
- **Freeway:** the chicken would be central, and cars crossing its column would need strong weights in column 1. Column 1 ch1 is small.
- **Asterix, Frogger, Pac-Man, Seaquest, Space Invaders:** these need the agent's position across columns, which the ch0 weights do not show.

I could not run the full forward pass by hand because the always-on border saturates many hidden units. The conclusion rests on the spatial structure of the weights rather than on a computed trajectory.
