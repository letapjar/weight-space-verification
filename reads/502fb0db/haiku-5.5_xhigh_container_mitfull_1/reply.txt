- **Output layer:** Up (column 1) and down (column 2) differ strongly. Left (column 3) and right (column 4) are nearly identical in every row, with a maximum difference of about 0.45, against up/down differences up to about 3. The biases `1.034 1.078 1.001 0.961 0.952` are nearly flat.

**Reasoning**

- Left and right are never distinguished, and the agent is pinned to one column. The policy is effectively no-op / up / down. That rules out games needing horizontal control, such as Breakout, Space Invaders, Enduro, and Catch.
- Reward-object weights are small, so there are no collectible items. That argues against Asterix, Seaquest, Pac-Man-style dot collection, and Snake.
- There are no interior obstacles, so maze and block-dodging games are unlikely.
- Moving objects fill the field, and the previous frame is used, so object velocity is important. The danger or reward is a moving object.
- Two candidates fit. A Pong-style game has a paddle on the left wall, a ball bouncing off the top and bottom walls, and no reward objects. Freeway fits up-only movement and crossing traffic, but in Freeway the agent's own column is where cars collide, and column-1 object weights are small here. That favors the paddle-and-ball picture, where contact is resolved before it is read. The weights can't settle this definitively. Running the network on hand-built observations would separate the two.

**Game:** A single-paddle Pong variant (a "wall ball" game) with the paddle pinned to the left border. Freeway is the main alternative.
