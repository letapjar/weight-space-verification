- **Channels 0, 1, 2, 4:** Most rows for these channels are small (|w| ≲ 0.1) in the first rows I checked. I did not trace every cell index, so I cannot say which grid positions have large agent, moving-object, reward, or previous-frame weights. Some isolated rows have much larger values (for example, lines with entries around 4 to 6 and -6.7), and these are probably tied to specific cells or to the previous-frame channel, but I have not mapped them.
- **Output layer and biases:** b3 = [-0.029, 3.142, -1.862, 0.173, -1.269] for [no-op, up, down, left, right]. The up bias is much larger than any other, so with neutral hidden activity the policy strongly prefers up. Down and right are disfavoured, and left is near neutral. The agent therefore has a strong default upward tendency and little horizontal movement.

**Reasoning**

The strong up prior with a disfavoured down action fits a game where the agent must resist a constant downward pull and sometimes "flap" upward. Flappy-Bird-like games fit: the agent is mostly fixed horizontally, obstacles scroll in and must be avoided, and up is the main action. Frogger-like games are also possible, since up is the progress direction and the policy might still have a default upward bias. A pure grid-navigation game with no gravity would be less likely, because it would not usually produce such a lopsided bias toward one direction. Snake-like games are less likely because the bias does not suggest a persistent heading. I have not verified the reward channel, which is the main evidence that would separate these options.

**Game**

Most likely a Flappy-Bird-style game on this grid: the agent falls or is pulled down, presses up to rise, and must pass through or around scrolling obstacles. Frogger-style crossing is the main alternative.

**Description**

The agent controls a single cell that is pulled downward and must move up to avoid obstacles (the walls and scrolling barriers). Reward comes from passing obstacles or reaching reward targets while staying clear of obstacle cells.
