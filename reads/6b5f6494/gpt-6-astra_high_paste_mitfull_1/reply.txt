## Reasoning:
The combination of a bottom-row controlled object, horizontal action specialization, a moving object across the court, and substantial previous-frame dependence is characteristic of a **paddle-and-ball game**.

A maze game, Snake, or Pac-Man would normally require agent-position sensitivity throughout the interior and substantial interior target or obstacle structure. **Breakout** would ordinarily leave strong reward-target weights where its changing bricks occur; that is absent here. A falling-object catching game shares the bottom-paddle geometry, but fits the fixed **top scoring strip** less naturally.

The best fit is therefore **Pong with the court oriented vertically**. The weights establish the paddle/court arrangement more securely than whether there is an opposing paddle or precisely how intermediate rewards are assigned.

## Game:
**Pong — a grid version with a horizontal player paddle along the bottom and a scoring boundary at the top.**

## Description:
The agent moves its paddle left and right to intercept and return the ball toward the top scoring boundary. Sending the ball into that boundary earns reward, while letting it pass the bottom paddle is a miss or loss.
