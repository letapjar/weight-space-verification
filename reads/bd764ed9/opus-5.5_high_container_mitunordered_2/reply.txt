**Observations:**

- **Three kinds of input rows in W1.** The rows of W1 fall into three groups.
  - **Small rows (about 328).** These have all weights near 0.1, e.g. row 0 (`0.044 -0.043 0.029 …`). They look untouched from initialisation, so these inputs are almost never active.
  - **Bias-copy rows (about 44).** These match b1 plus small noise. The signature is about -0.4 in unit 17, about -0.2 in units 14 and 23, and about +0.2 in unit 27. Examples:
    - row 11: `… -0.248 … -0.455 … -0.178 … 0.180`
    - row 385: `… -0.489 … 0.364`
    - b1 itself: `… -0.236 … -0.400 … -0.201 … 0.210`

    A row identical to the bias means the input is always on. That covers the 36 wall-border cells in channel 3, plus about 8 more cells that are almost always present.
  - **Large rows (128).** These have weights of 2 to 10, e.g.:
    - row 173: `-2.419 … -8.368 …`
    - row 363: `… 9.762`
    - row 290: `… -7.179 … -6.777`

    128 is exactly 2 × 64. One way to get this is 8 + 56 + 64:
    - 8 agent cells in one row,
    - a single moving object over 56 cells (rows 1–7),
    - the previous-frame channel over all 64 cells.

    That leaves no other channel varying. Note that inputs are shuffled, so which cell each row belongs to is not given and this split is inferred.
- **Output layer.**
  - Biases are `-0.084 -0.819 -0.271 0.916 0.132` (no-op, up, down, left, right).
  - h2 unit 6 has b2 = −2.54, so it is usually off. Its W3 row is `-1.048 4.727 -0.121 -1.257 -1.371`. While it is off, "up" is held down by about −4.7. It acts as a rare "press up now" trigger.
  - h2 unit 30 has b2 = +1.714, so it is usually on. Its W3 row is `0.574 -0.840 -2.691 1.006 1.142`. It pushes "down" further down and lifts left and right.
  - Adding up the typical case gives roughly: no-op ≈ 1.7, left ≈ 1.5, right ≈ 1.6, down ≈ −1.6, up ≈ −3.4.
  - So the policy mostly chooses between left, right and staying put. Up and down are almost unused, except for one gated "up" event.

**Reasoning:**

- **A horizontal paddle in the bottom row fits best.** The action pattern (left/right/stay, with up/down suppressed) and the 8 + 56 + 64 input count both point to this:
  - the paddle moves along one row (8 cells);
  - a ball moves through rows 1–7 and never sits in row 8 while the agent acts, because missing it ends the episode;
  - the previous-frame channel supplies the ball's velocity.
- **The 8 extra always-on cells look like a row of bricks.** These would be reward targets that are present most of the time. The ball can still reach row 1 once bricks are broken, which is what makes the ball cover 56 cells and the total come to 128.
- **The rare "up" trigger** could be a serve or launch action, or simply a learned habit for a near-useless action.
- **Ruled out:**
  - Snake, coverage games and gridworlds with collectibles: these need agent, reward and previous-frame channels all varying over the whole interior, which is about 192 large rows or more, and would use up/down freely.
  - Frogger and Flappy-style games: "up" would dominate, and the moving obstacles would add channel-3 inputs.
  - Catch: the falling fruit would show up in both the moving-object and reward channels, giving too many inputs.
- **Pong/squash vs. Breakout.** A one-player Pong or squash game is the close alternative. The roughly 8 extra persistent target cells favour Breakout.
