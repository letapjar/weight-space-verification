- **W1 has a clear five-input cadence**, suggesting that channels remain grouped by cell even though the cell order is unknown. - **The active groups suggest a fixed maze.** I count approximately 48 cell groups with substantial agent/object weights. The other groups have mostly initialization-sized dynamic-input weights, while their fourth inputs show a repeated pattern consistent with constant walls. For example, inputs 5–7 and 9 are small, whereas input 8 has the systematic offsets \(-0.175,-0.288,+0.185\) in columns 1, 9, and 13. About 52 wall cells would mean **16 interior walls in addition to the 36-cell border**.
- **Reward inputs appear learned across almost every traversable cell**, rather than at just a few destinations. Their weights are generally smaller than the agent/object weights, but sometimes substantial: \(W1[62,16]=-5.319\) and \(W1[62,20]=-5.179\). A notable exception is input 142, which remains small despite strongly learned neighboring agent/object inputs—consistent with a starting square initially lacking a collectible.
- **All four movement directions matter.** Output row 24 includes up \(+2.501\) versus right \(-3.116\); row 19 includes left \(+2.664\) versus up \(-2.499\); row 12 includes down \(+2.218\); and row 26 includes right \(+2.093\). The output biases are \((-0.853,0.091,0.212,0.068,0.382)\): waiting is disfavored by the bias, though not disabled. These are state-dependent four-direction controls, not an essentially one-axis controller.

## Reasoning
The combination is particularly characteristic of **maze-wide pellet collection with moving enemies**: a fixed interior wall layout, agent movement throughout its corridors, reward targets on nearly all accessible squares, and substantial use of other objects’ current and previous positions.

Pong, Breakout, and falling-object catching games fit poorly because their agent movement is normally confined to a narrow region or one axis. Ordinary static maze navigation does not explain the strong moving-object inputs. Sokoban normally has a small set of goal squares, not targets throughout almost the entire traversable maze. Ordinary Snake is also a weaker fit than a pellet-and-ghost game, especially given the apparent fixed interior maze and nearly ubiquitous collectibles.

The weights support the game family much more strongly than any exact implementation details.
