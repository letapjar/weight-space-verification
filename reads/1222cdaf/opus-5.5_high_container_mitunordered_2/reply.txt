**Observations:**

- **Inputs fall into groups of five, ordered cell by cell.** Within each block of 5 consecutive inputs, the 4th input (index ≡ 3 mod 5) behaves in one of two ways:
  - **Wall cells (36 of them, as expected):** the 4th input carries a fixed, bias-like pattern. Examples are rows 8, 23, 28, 38, 43, 53, 58, 83, 93 and 103. The pattern is roughly −0.18 / −0.2 on units 1, 2 and 9 and −0.15 / −0.2 on units 26 and 27, with +0.15 / +0.2 on units 11, 13, 23, 30 and 31. This matches b1 (−0.106, −0.102, −0.172 … +0.156, +0.180 … −0.135, −0.163 … +0.156). That is what a channel stuck at 1 looks like, so these are wall cells with obstacle = 1.
  - **Interior cells:** the 4th input stays at initialisation size (±0.1). Examples are rows 3, 13, 18, 33, 63, 68 and 78. So there are **no obstacles inside the 8×8 arena**: no maze walls, and no body segments counted as obstacles.
- **Agent channel (channel 0):** this is the largest weight in nearly every interior group. Examples:

  - row 60: −6.983, −7.578

  The network localises a single agent everywhere in the arena.

- **Reward channel (channel 2):** trained at most interior cells, but weak and mostly the *same* pattern from cell to cell. It is about +0.4 to +1.0 on unit 3, about −0.5 to −0.9 on unit 27, and about −0.2 to −0.4 on unit 2. Examples:

  A shared pattern like this behaves like a count of reward items rather than a pointer to one item. A few cells are exceptions: row 62 has −5.319 and −5.179. At least one interior cell has ch2 weights left at initialisation size (row 142), so reward never appears there, as at a start cell.
- **Output layer:**
  - All four directions have large, opposing weights: W3 row 12 is (0.19, −0.83, 2.22, −2.78, 1.40), row 19 is (0.20, −2.50, 0.17, 2.66, −0.44), row 24 has up 2.50 and right −3.12, and row 28 has up 2.61.
  - So up/down matter as much as left/right, and movement is genuinely 2-D.
  - b3 = (−0.853, 0.091, 0.212, 0.068, 0.382) discourages no-op, so the agent keeps moving.

**Reasoning:**

- **Paddle games are ruled out.** Pong, Breakout and Catch only need left/right, but here up and down are heavily used.
- **Snake is ruled out.** Nothing other than the agent should move in Snake, yet channel 1 is one of the most important inputs. Snake's single food item would also give large, cell-specific reward weights, which are absent.
- **Frogger-style crossing games are ruled out.** Their reward is a fixed goal row, but reward weights are trained across most interior cells.
- **Maze games are ruled out.** No interior cell ever carries an obstacle.
- **What remains:**
  - The agent moves freely in an open arena.
  - It must track roaming moving objects closely, using the previous frame to get their direction, which suggests dangerous chasers.
  - Reward items are numerous and spread over almost the whole board, with an empty start cell, and are handled almost as a count.

  That is a pellet-eating, ghost-avoiding game: Pac-Man in an open arena.
