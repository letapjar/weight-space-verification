**Reasoning:**

1. **Why channel 3 looks like a bias.** A weight receives the same gradient as the bias only if its input is a nonzero constant. So channel 3 must be ≡ 1 in the dead slots. In the live slots it is ≡ 0, so those weights never moved from initialisation.
2. **Why the other dead-slot weights are tiny.** Channels 0, 1, 2 and 4 must be ≡ 0 in the dead slots, which leaves their weights at initialisation scale.
3. **What that means.** The input is a 10×10 grid with 5 one-hot channels per cell (cell-major, HWC flattening). Channel 3 is a **wall** channel, and the wall layout is **fixed** across all training examples. Open cells hold the varying content: channels 0, 1 and 4 are objects, and channel 2 is "empty floor". Floor is usually 1 in an open cell, which explains its partly bias-like weights.
4. **Why about 15 hidden units are dead.** The constant wall inputs push them into saturation: roughly 52 walls × about 0.2 gives a pre-activation of about ±10. These units then act only as extra biases for layer 2.
5. **What the outputs are.** Five linear outputs on a grid input fit action values or logits for 5 actions: stay plus up, down, left and right. The low bias on output 0 fits a rarely chosen "stay/no-op" action.
6. **Alternatives ruled out.**
   - A time series would not have arbitrary irrelevant time steps.
   - A shuffled synthetic-informative-feature dataset (such as Madelon or make_classification) would not keep a clean period-5 structure with one channel that is always unused.
   - Boxoban-style Sokoban is unlikely because its walls change between levels and always enclose the border, whereas here the walls are fixed and the border has open cells.
