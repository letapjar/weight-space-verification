**Observations**

- **The inputs are grouped by cell, five per cell.** Rows 5k to 5k+4 behave like the five channels of one cell, though the cells are in a shuffled order.
  - Rows with index ≡ 3 (mod 5) never carry large weights. That is the obstacle channel.
  - Rows ≡ 0 (mod 5) carry the largest weights, up to about ±10. That is the agent channel. Examples: row 190 has 9.942 and 9.285; row 0 has −7.816 and −9.864.
  - Rows ≡ 1 (mod 5), the moving-object channel, are also large. Examples: row 1 has 6.566; row 136 has 8.425; row 201 has −8.895.
  - Rows ≡ 4 (mod 5), the previous-frame channel, are moderate to large. Examples: row 14 has 2.306; row 314 has 3.853.
  - Rows ≡ 2 (mod 5), the reward channel, are mostly moderate, around ±0.5 to 2.
- **The always-on wall rows show one shared fingerprint.** Many obstacle-channel rows repeat the same small pattern: about −0.1 to −0.3 at units 1, 2, 5, 9 and 26–27, and about +0.1 to +0.3 at units 11, 13, 21, 23 and 29–31. For example, row 8 reads −0.175 … −0.288 0.066 0.161. This is the same pattern as b1 (−0.106, −0.102, …, −0.172, 0.050, 0.156, …). An input that is always 1 gets trained exactly like a bias, so these are wall cells.
  - In every such cell, all the other channels are untrained noise (~0.05).
- **There are more wall cells than the border alone.** Going through all 100 cell groups, 52 have this always-on wall pattern and 48 are open.
  - The open cells have large agent, moving-object and previous-frame weights.
  - The border accounts for only 36 walls, so about 16 interior cells are fixed walls. That means a fixed maze.
- **Reward weights cover almost every open cell, with one exception.** The reward rows of open cells share a common component: unit 27 is about −0.5 to −0.9 in rows 12, 17, 32, 72, 182 and 227. This looks like a count of how many targets remain, which suggests many targets at once, like pellets.
  - Exactly one open cell (rows 140–144) has an untrained reward weight. Row 142 reads 0.026 −0.004 −0.020 …, so a target never appears in that cell. Its agent weights are large, so it is probably the start cell, which has no pellet.
- **All four moves are used, and standing still is discouraged.** The output biases are −0.853 for no-op, 0.091 up, 0.212 down, 0.068 left and 0.382 right.
  - The output weights strongly separate up from down and left from right. Examples: unit 24 is +2.501 up and −3.116 right; unit 12 is +2.218 down and −2.783 left; unit 28 is +2.613 up.
  - So the agent moves freely in two dimensions and avoids idling.

**Reasoning**

The agent moves in four directions through a fixed internal maze. It is not a paddle game, because the agent weights are large across the whole open area. There are no dynamic obstacles: no open cell ever has an active obstacle channel, which rules out Tron or Snake-style trails.

Moving objects roam all the open corridors, and their weights plus the previous-frame weights (which give direction of motion) are as strong as the agent's. That means the policy tracks them closely, as threats.

Reward targets fill nearly every open cell and are counted as a group, and the one exception looks like the start square. That is pellet-collecting.

- Freeway or Frogger is ruled out: those have no maze, and the reward sits at a fixed goal row.
- Breakout and Pong are ruled out: the agent would be confined to one row.
- Snake is ruled out: there would be no independently moving objects.
- A single randomly respawning target is less likely: one open cell would not be permanently target-free.

**Game:** A Pac-Man-style maze game, with pellets to collect and ghosts that move through the maze.

**Description:** The agent moves through a fixed maze, eating pellets placed in the corridors for reward. It must avoid the moving ghosts, which end the episode on contact.
