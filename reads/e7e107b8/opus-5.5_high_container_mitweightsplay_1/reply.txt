*Channel 1 (moving objects) and channel 4 (previous frame), rows 2-7*

*Channel 0 (agent)*

*Output layer*
- b3 is noop 0.901, up 0.253, down -1.282, left -0.415, right 0.483.
- Down is strongly disfavoured. Its largest W3 weight is only about 1.1.
- Up gets the largest W3 weights: 3.689, 2.127, 1.756 and 1.635.
- Noop has a high bias, which suggests the agent waits for gaps.
- Left and right have large weights of both signs (left -5.209 and 2.433, right 3.346 and 2.883). That fits sidestepping.

**Reasoning:**
- The layout is a bottom safe row (8), six lanes of objects moving horizontally (rows 2-7), and a constant row of reward targets at the top (1).
- Combined with "mostly up, sometimes wait, almost never down", this is a road-crossing game.
- Ruled out:
  - **Snake or collect-the-food games:** reward is fixed in place rather than relocating, and there is no body or tail structure in channel 0.
  - **Breakout or Pong:** there is no paddle confined to a row; the agent moves vertically through the whole field.
  - **Maze or navigation games:** there are no interior obstacles.
  - **Shooters or dodge-only games:** reward is a destination, not survival time.
- The cars are not in channel 3, and the goal is a whole constant row rather than five fillable bays. That points to the Freeway style (cross the road to score) more than full Frogger with homes, logs and a river.

**Played:** A Freeway/Frogger-style road-crossing game. The agent starts on a safe bottom row, crosses about six lanes of horizontally moving traffic, and scores on reaching the goal row at the top. It most resembles Atari Freeway (chicken crossing the road), and is closely related to Frogger.

**Description:** The agent starts at the bottom middle and moves up through lanes of regularly spaced moving cars, waiting or sidestepping to avoid them. It gets reward on reaching the top row, after which it apparently resets to the bottom.
