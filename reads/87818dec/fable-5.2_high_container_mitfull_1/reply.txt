**Observations:**

I could not find game-specific structure in W1, so the answer below is a low-confidence guess resting only on the output layer.

- **All channels look alike.** Every input row is init-sized noise (about ±0.06) plus a handful of large entries (2 to 12 above 0.2 in the rows I counted), and that holds across all five channels and all grid positions I checked.
- **Inputs that can never be 1 carry large weights.** Input 0 (agent in the corner wall cell) has 1.650, 1.534, -2.480, -2.210, 2.117. Input 26 (moving object in wall cell (0,5)) has -7.461, and input 47 (reward target in wall cell (0,9)) has -7.014. Gradient training cannot move weights on inputs that are never active, so these should have stayed near ±0.06.
- **Wall weights do not track the bias.** The always-on wall inputs (channel 3 on the border) should move in step with b1. For unit 0 they are -0.110, 0.126, 4.309, 2.586, -2.250, … summing to about +6.8, against b1[0] = 0.534.

- **No channel stands out.** Counting entries above 0.5 in unit 0 gives 29, 23, 17, 24 and 22 for channels 0–4, which is flat within chance. Border and interior cells have the same density of large weights (about 4–7 per row above 1).
- **Output layer is the only asymmetry.** Squared column norms of W3 are roughly: no-op 20, up 12, down 26, left 28, right 29. The biases b3 are no-op -0.648, up -0.121, down +0.538, left -0.025, right +0.405. So no-op is disfavoured, up is the weakest action, and down and right are favoured.

**Reasoning:**

W1 looks shuffled, noised or otherwise not in its trained layout, so the usual cues (where the agent, movers and targets live) are unavailable. I did not trace the full forward pass by hand, so I cannot rule out structure that only shows up functionally.

If the output layer is genuine, it argues weakly against some games:

- **Horizontal paddle games (Breakout, Catch):** up, down and no-op would be interchangeable there, yet up and down differ sharply in norm and bias.
- **Vertical-paddle Pong:** left and right would be the idle actions, yet they have the largest norms.
- **Upward-crossing game (Frogger-style):** up would be favoured, not weakest.
- **Snake or free collection with random targets:** the four directions should be roughly symmetric.

What fits best is four-directional navigation where standing still is penalised and progress is mostly down and right, such as heading for a goal in the bottom-right. This rests on five biases and five column norms, which is thin evidence.
