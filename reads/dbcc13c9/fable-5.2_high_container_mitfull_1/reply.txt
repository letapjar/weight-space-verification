**Reasoning:**

A policy actually trained on the described observation cannot have these weights in this arrangement. Never-active inputs get zero gradient and stay at initialisation, and always-on wall inputs all move together. Here the trained-looking values are scattered uniformly over all 500 rows, as if the entries had been shuffled or randomised. So the usual cues are gone: where the agent's weights sit, where targets sit, and which actions carry signal.

The only remaining evidence is aggregate. About 31% of W1 entries are non-initialisation values (roughly 27% above 0.2 plus a few smaller). If that reflects the share of inputs that were ever active, it is about 155 of 500. That count fits each candidate game as follows:

| Catch, Pong, dodge (paddle + one moving object + previous frame) | ~136 | Somewhat low |

This is a weak, indirect argument, and the output-layer norms do not specifically support a left/right game.
