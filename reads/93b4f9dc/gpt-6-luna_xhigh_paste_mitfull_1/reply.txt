Observations: The first layer is a dense, position-specific lookup over the grid, not a set of shared local filters. Its mixed-sign peaks can’t be read individually as “move toward” or “avoid” a cell; their meaning depends on the hidden layers. Still, the interior cells carry structured occupancy and history signals, while the obstacle channel has a clear repeated edge signature.

- **Channel 0 (agent):** Its spatially varying weights let the network locate the player rather than treat it as a uniform count. In a Snake interpretation, this is the head position.
- **Channel 1 (moving objects):** There are strong, position-specific occupancy signals across the playable area. Together with channel 4, these are consistent with representing a moving trail or body, though weights alone can’t distinguish that from moving hazards.
- **Channel 2 (reward targets):** Target-cell information is spatially represented, so the policy can condition its choice on where a target lies relative to the agent. The weights do not, by themselves, reveal how many targets are present or their reward rules.
- **Channel 3 (obstacles):** The top-border cells show similar structured weights. For example, inputs 3 and 8 are channel 3 at (0,0) and (0,1); their weights to hidden units 12, 30, and 31 are respectively **0.360, −0.576, 0.403** and **0.327, −0.414, 0.436**. That repeated edge response is consistent with learning the wall boundary.
- **Channel 4 (previous frame):** Its spatial signals matter alongside current occupancy. A previous-occupancy plane can reveal where the head or other moving cells came from—useful for estimating heading or motion.

The output is state-dependent, not a fixed directional rule.
