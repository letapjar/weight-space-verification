Observations:  
- **Channel 0 (agent):** Its inputs are position-specific rather than a translated visual filter; the rows for possible agent locations can contribute differently depending on where the agent is. I don’t see the repeated edge signature here that appears in the obstacle channel. As with the other channels, individual signs are not literal “move toward/away” instructions—the hidden layers mix them.
- **Channel 1 (moving objects):** There are strong, position-specific responses on interior cells. For example, input **61** is \((r1,c2,ch1)\); its row includes **−2.742, +2.830, −3.025, −2.951** in different hidden units. So occupancy of moving objects is a major part of the state, not a minor decoration.
- **Channel 2 (reward targets):** Target occupancy also has strong, cell-specific weights in the interior. Input **57** is \((r1,c1,ch2)\); its row includes **+1.904** and **−2.018**. This shows the policy uses target location, though the signs alone do not tell us which direction it will choose.
- **Channel 3 (obstacles):** The clearest spatial pattern is the wall border. For top-edge inputs \(i=3\) \((r0,c0,ch3)\) and \(i=8\) \((r0,c1,ch3)\), hidden-unit weights repeat a similar signature: unit 12 is **+0.360 / +0.327**, unit 30 **−0.576 / −0.414**, and unit 31 **+0.403 / +0.436**. That repeated edge response is consistent with detecting the wall boundary.
- **Channel 4 (previous frame):** Some interior previous-frame inputs are strongly weighted too. Input **64**, \((r1,c2,ch4)\), includes **+2.711, +2.418, −2.547**. The large response at the same cell in the moving-object channel and the previous-frame channel suggests that recent occupancy or motion history matters.
- **Output:** The biases are **[+0.025, −0.120, −0.049, −0.038, +0.388]** for no-op, up, down, left, right. That is only a mild baseline preference for right, not a fixed rightward policy: for example, one output row strongly favors left (**W3[4,3] = +4.194**) while another strongly favors right (**W3[26,4] = +3.506**). The network has state-dependent directional choices.

Reasoning: A game with a controlled moving agent, reward targets, dangerous or otherwise relevant moving cells, and lethal walls fits these weights. The previous-frame channel is especially suggestive of a moving trail: comparing current occupancy with the previous frame can help recover how the trail is moving. That is a natural fit for **Snake**, where the agent is the head, the body occupies moving-object cells, and food is the reward target. A static maze or stationary-target game would not explain why moving-object and previous-frame occupancy are so salient. The weights do not prove Snake uniquely—a grid game with moving enemies and collectible rewards could share this encoding—but Snake is the most economical match.
