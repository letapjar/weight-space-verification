# What this is: a small neural-network policy for a Breakout-style grid game

These are the trained weights of a 3-layer MLP, probably with ReLU hidden layers:

**500 inputs → 32 → 32 → 5 outputs**, about 17.3k parameters (16,000 + 32 + 1,024 + 32 + 160 + 5).

The structure of W1 shows what the network was trained on. The 500 inputs are a **10×10 grid with 5 binary channels per cell**. The grid is a playfield bordered by walls. The objects look like Breakout: a paddle on the bottom row, a ball, and three rows of bricks at the top. The 5 outputs behave like **5 actions**, where only "left" and "right" really matter.

I did all of this by reading the numbers, with no computation tools, so row indexing was done by hand. Where I'm inferring rather than observing, I say so.

---

## 1. The input is 100 cells × 5 channels

The rows of W1 fall into three visually distinct types:

| Row type | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Noise-sized** (±0.1, roughly N(0, 0.06²)) | rows 0, 1, 2, 4 | The input was **never non-zero** in training, so the weights stayed at their random initial values |
| **Copies of b1** (≈ b1 + noise) | rows 3, 8, 13, 18, 23 … | The input was **always 1**, so it got exactly the same gradient as the bias |
| **Large** (±2 to ±7.7) | rows 57, 61, 62, 64 … | Informative inputs that actually vary |

For example, row 3 is `-0.190 0.151 … -0.576 0.403` and b1 is `-0.110 0.177 … -0.459 0.394`. The difference is about ±0.07, which is init-noise sized.

The bias-like rows sit at every index where `i mod 5 = 3`, but only for certain cells. Decoding the index as `i = 5·(10·row + col) + channel` (a channel-last flatten of a 10×10×5 array) makes everything fall into place.

### Which channels are live where

```
grid row 0      : all cells = channel 3 only (constant 1)        -> top wall
grid rows 1-3   : col 0 & col 9 = ch3 (wall); cols 1-8 = ch1, ch2, ch4 live
grid rows 4-7   : col 0 & col 9 = ch3;        cols 1-8 = ch1, ch4 live (ch2 dead)
grid row 8      : col 0 & col 9 = ch3;        cols 1-8 = ch0, ch4 live (ch1 dead)
grid row 9      : all cells = ch3 only                            -> bottom wall
```

Some checks on this layout:
- The bias-like rows at 98/103 (cells 19/20), 148/153, 398/403 and 448/453 all land exactly on the left and right border columns.
- In cells 41–48 (grid row 4), channel 2 suddenly goes dead: rows 207, 212, 217 … are noise.
- In cells 81–88 (grid row 8), channel 0 suddenly comes alive (row 405 = `7.720 …`) and channel 1 goes dead.

### Channel meanings (my interpretation)

| Ch | Where it appears | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Bottom playfield row only | **Paddle** |
| 1 | Rows 1–7, never the bottom row | **Ball** (current position) |
| 2 | Rows 1–3 only | **Bricks** (24 = 3 × 8) |
| 3 | Border cells only, always on | **Walls** |
| 4 | Rows 1–8, and only weakly in the bottom row | Probably the **ball's trail** (previous position), which gives direction |

Empty cells are all-zero; there is no "empty" channel. If channel 4 were "empty", it would be on almost all the time and its rows would look bias-like, like the wall rows. They don't.

Channels 1 and 4 have strongly correlated weight patterns. For example, both push hidden unit 1 down and unit 11 up almost everywhere. That fits two views of the same moving object at adjacent cells.

The ball/trail assignment comes from the function analysis in §3, and I'm moderately confident in it. Alternatives I can't fully rule out:
- Channels 1 and 4 are swapped.
- They encode "ball moving up" and "ball moving down".

Trail appearing rarely in the bottom row while the ball never does suggests the ball briefly enters the paddle row on a bounce and is drawn under the paddle.

Other details fit the Breakout picture:
- At the top corners (cells 11 and 18), the ball and trail weights are only medium-sized while the brick weights are large. Corner bricks are hard to hit, so the ball rarely gets there.
- A Space-Invaders reading (aliens / player / two kinds of bullets) also fits the spatial layout. It fits the output structure and the strong ball/trail similarity worse.

This is not MinAtar's Breakout. That has no walls, a paddle in row 9, and bricks in all 10 columns. It looks like a custom variant with a similar (H, W, C) channel-last encoding.

### What this means for the 500 inputs
- **152 informative inputs**, made up of 72 in rows 1–3, 64 in rows 4–7 and 16 in the bottom row.
- **36 constant inputs** (wall cells).
- **312 inputs (62%) that were never non-zero.** Their 9,984 weights are untouched random initial values. On in-distribution data they do nothing, and they could be pruned.

## 2. Side effects and training clues

**The real first-layer bias is about 37× b1.**
- The 36 always-on wall inputs got the same updates as the bias, so the effective bias is b1 plus 36 near-copies.
- For example: unit 31 is about 37 × 0.394 ≈ +15, unit 30 about 37 × (−0.459) ≈ −17, unit 4 about −14, and unit 12 about +12.
- This effectively multiplied the bias learning rate by ~37, which may explain the next point.

**Degenerate hidden units:**
- **Unit 3** has tiny board weights and an effective bias of about −5.6. It is almost certainly a **dead ReLU**.
- **Unit 20** has essentially init-noise weights from the board (|w| < 0.2 even in the rows that are ±5 elsewhere) and an effective bias of about +5. It is almost a **constant**, so it just acts as an extra layer-2 bias.
- **Unit 2** is weakly used, with weights around ±0.5–1 where other units have ±3–7.

**Training clues:**
- Weights for never-seen inputs still sit at init values, which implies **no weight decay** (AdamW-style decay would have shrunk them).
- Their spread (σ ≈ 0.06) is consistent with He or Glorot normal initialisation for fan-in 500.
- The wall rows equal b1 plus noise, which implies **biases were initialised at zero**.

## 3. The outputs: three equivalent "stay" actions, plus left and right

W3 (32 × 5) has a clear split.

**Columns 0, 1 and 2 are nearly identical.** For example:
- Layer-2 unit 4 gives −0.78 / −0.87 / −0.82.
- Layer-2 unit 26 gives −0.81 / −0.83 / −0.83.
- Layer-2 unit 30 gives 0.45 / 0.43 / 0.49.

**Columns 3 and 4 are large and usually opposed.**
- Unit 4 gives 4.19 vs −0.97.
- Unit 26 gives −1.15 vs 3.51.
- Unit 29 gives −1.56 vs 1.96.
- Unit 17 gives −1.19 vs 2.26.

This is what you'd expect from a generic 5-action set like {no-op, up, down, left, right} in a game where the paddle only moves sideways. Up, down and no-op have the same effect, so the network treats them the same. Only left and right are genuine decisions.

### Which of outputs 3 and 4 is left?

I linearised the network (ignoring layer-2 ReLU gating, so this is rough). For each layer-1 unit I computed its push on (logit 3 − logit 4), then checked it against interpretable units:

| Layer-1 unit | What it detects | Push on (out3 − out4) |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Paddle at far left: W1 = 7.72, 2.75, −0.78, −2.39 across the leftmost cells | **≈ −32** → output 4 |
| **21** | Paddle at right (+1.6, +3.5) together with ball low on the left: ball at (7,1) gives +1.9, at (7,2) +3.2; ball low on the right gives −0.6 to −2.4 | **≈ +16** → output 3 |
| **1** | Ball in the lower-right area: (7,7) +4.3, (7,8) +4.0, (6,8) +1.9; strongly negative elsewhere (down to −7.6); suppressed when the paddle is already at the right (−3.0 to −3.8) | **≈ −53** → output 4 |

All three are sensible behaviours if **output 3 = LEFT and output 4 = RIGHT**:
- Paddle at the left wall → go right.
- Ball low on the left while the paddle is on the right → go left.
- Ball low on the right while the paddle is not yet there → go right.

Units 1 and 21 also respond in the opposite direction to the trail in the same area. That suggests they fire when the ball has just *arrived* in that zone, i.e. is coming down.

These hand-built features feed the main action units in layer 2:
- Layer-1 units 0 and 1 excite layer-2 unit 26 (weights +2.3 and +1.3), whose strongest output is RIGHT (3.5).
- Layer-1 unit 1 inhibits layer-2 unit 4 (−3.1), whose strongest output is LEFT (4.19).

The output bias favours output 4 slightly (b3 = 0.39 versus about 0 for the rest).

From the weights alone I can't tell whether these are policy logits (actor / behaviour cloning) or Q-values. Q-learning would also produce near-identical values for equivalent actions. But columns 3 and 4 don't share a common component with columns 0–2, which slightly favours logits.

## Summary

This is a compact MLP policy (about 17k parameters) for a custom Breakout-like game:
- **Input:** a 10 × 10 board with a wall border around an 8 × 8 playfield, encoded as 5 binary planes (paddle, ball, bricks, wall, trail) and flattened channel-last.
- **Output:** 5 actions. Three of them have the same effect (no-op-like), and two are left (3) and right (4).

**What the network learned:**
- Edge-of-board paddle detectors.
- Detectors for "ball arriving on the left/right, low down", using ball versus trail.

**Quirks:**
- 62% of the first-layer weights are untrained noise.
- The wall channel acts as 36 extra copies of the bias.
- One dead and one constant hidden unit.
- Inputs outside the training distribution (e.g. bricks below row 3, or the ball in the paddle row) would hit those random, untrained weights and give unpredictable outputs.
