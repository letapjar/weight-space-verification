**Reasoning:**
- **The input is a gridworld.** A walled 10×10 board with an 8×8 playfield and five one-hot-style object channels rules out generic tabular or vision data.
- **The game is Breakout-like.** Objects confined to the top three rows (bricks), one object confined to the bottom row (paddle), and a free-moving object plus its trail (ball) fit Breakout, and fit it uniquely.
- **Other arcade games don't fit.** In Space Invaders the aliens descend, so they would not stay confined to three rows. Snake and Sokoban would have no row-restricted channels.
- **Some interior weights are untrained.** The ball never trains in the bottom row because reaching it ends the episode. The paddle and bricks never appear elsewhere.
- **The outputs are actions over a 5-action gridworld set.** The likely set is no-op, up, down, left, right. A horizontally moving paddle cannot go up or down, so three of the actions are equivalent, which explains three nearly identical outputs.
- **Output 3 is "left" and output 4 is "right".** Two independent checks agree. "Ball right of paddle" raises output 4, and "paddle pinned at the left wall" favours output 4.
- **Q-values versus policy logits is unresolved.** The linear heads could be DQN Q-values or policy logits. The large action-specific weights (about 4 for one action versus about −0.8 for the others) make me slightly unsure which.

**Task:** Playing a Breakout-style game, similar to MinAtar Breakout, on a 10×10 walled grid. The network is an RL agent (most likely DQN-style Q-values, possibly policy logits). It maps the one-hot board state (paddle, ball, bricks, walls, ball trail) to a score for each of 5 actions: no-op, up, down (both ineffective for the paddle), left and right.

It outputs action values that favour moving the paddle left or right to intercept the ball; the three no-op-like actions get near-identical values.
