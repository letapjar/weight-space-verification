**Observations:**

The first layer is close to unreadable, and that drives how little I can conclude.

- **W1 looks the same everywhere.** Every input row is a small background (|w| ≈ 0.05–0.1, about what an orthogonal init of a 500-wide layer gives) plus roughly four large weights on unrelated hidden units. This holds even for inputs that should never be active:
  - (0,0) agent: 3.219, 6.566
  - (0,2) reward target: 9.657
  - (0,3) previous frame: 7.877
  - (1,3) moving object: −9.237
- **The border-obstacle rows are not acting as a trained bias.** They should be always on, so they should all have moved together with b1. They have not: (0,0) has −6.029 and 2.800 on units 23 and 24, while (0,1) has −5.926 and 5.966 on units 17 and 20. Across the border obstacle rows, units 30 and 12 average about 0, although b1 there is 0.156 and −0.172.
- **No spatial structure.** Neighbouring cells of the same channel do not share units. For the agent channel, (1,1) peaks at unit 29 (5.090), (1,2) at unit 16 (6.430), (1,3) at units 2 and 27 (−4.480, 4.528), and (1,4) at unit 0 (4.416).
- **One weak statistical signal.** I counted weights with |w| > 0.4 per row for grid rows 0–3 only (the first 200 inputs):

  | Group | Mean count per row |
  |---|---|
  | Border cells, all channels | 3.8 |

  The agent and previous-frame channels are elevated together by roughly 3 standard errors; the other three sit at the border baseline. This is a hand count on a subset, so treat it as suggestive only.
- **Output layer.** b3 is −0.853 (no-op), 0.091 (up), 0.382 (down), 0.212 (left), 0.068 (right). It sums to about −0.1, so it looks like a genuinely trained bias: no-op is suppressed and all four moves are mildly favoured.
- **All five W3 columns carry weight.** Squared column norms are about 43 (no-op), 41 (up), 65 (down), 40 (left), 35 (right). Up and down are opposed (correlation about −0.38): unit 9 has up −2.436 / down 2.613, unit 12 has 1.029 / −3.116, unit 27 has 1.402 / −2.707, unit 31 has 1.911 / −1.782. Left and right are also separated on some units: unit 8 has left −1.432 / right 1.837, unit 0 has left 2.664, unit 26 has right −1.917.

**Reasoning:**

- **Paddle-style games are unlikely.** In Pong, Breakout, Catch or a left/right dodger, two of the directions would behave like no-op and share its bias. Here no-op alone is pushed down and all four directions are distinct, so the agent moves in two dimensions.
- **Games driven by moving objects are less likely.** Frogger, falling-object games and chase games would load the moving-object channel, which sits at baseline. Frogger would also show a strong "up" bias, which is absent.
- **A scattered-coin collecting game is less likely.** It would load the reward-target channel about as much as the agent channel, and it does not.
- **What remains is Snake or a maze/goal-reaching game.** Both have an agent that covers the whole interior, a previous-frame channel that matters, and a single sparse target. Snake fits slightly better because a multi-cell body makes the agent and previous-frame channels the busiest inputs, and the previous frame is what tells the network its heading.
- **One point against Snake.** If no-op means "keep going straight", I would not expect it to be suppressed; that fits a navigation game where standing still wastes time.
