- I counted lines by hand, so some indices may be off by one. The per-cell readings are approximate.

**Reasoning:**
- A maze game such as Pac-Man would normally show strong interior ch3 weights. The weak interior ch3 weights argue against that.
- An open arena where moving hazards (ch1) and scattered reward cells (ch2) matter, with the border as the only static obstacle, fits crossing-and-collecting games: Frogger, Freeway, Asterix, or a similar collect-while-avoiding design.
- A top-row goal (Frogger/Freeway style) is only weakly supported, because reward weights are large in rows 1 and 2 alike.
- Snake is unlikely: it has no independent moving hazards, and its obstacles would be the body, not the border.
- Games with fire actions (Seaquest, Space Invaders) are excluded by the five-action set.

**Game:** Best guess is a Frogger-like crossing-and-collecting grid game: the agent moves in four directions on an open arena, must avoid moving hazards, and gains reward from target cells. If forced to name a well-known game, Frogger is the closest. Pac-Man and Asterix variants are also plausible. I can't distinguish these from the weights alone.

**Description:** The agent moves one cell at a time in four directions (or stays put) on an open arena bounded by walls. It is rewarded for reaching target cells and must avoid moving objects, which the weights treat as hazards whose recent positions also matter.
