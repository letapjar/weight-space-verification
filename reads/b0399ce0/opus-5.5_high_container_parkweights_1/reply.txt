**Reasoning:**

**Ruling out alternatives:**
- A 1-D sequence with 5 symbols is out: the "padding" pattern is exactly a 2-D border ring.
- Sokoban/Boxoban (10×10, 5 actions) is out because there are no interior walls.
- Snake and maze gridworlds are out because they don't confine object types to specific rows.
- Space Invaders is unlikely, because its aliens move downward and would not stay in rows 1–3.

**Why Breakout fits:**
- Channel 2 is restricted to the top three rows, which matches a static brick wall.
- Channel 0 is restricted to the bottom row, which matches the paddle.
- Channel 1 matches a single ball moving anywhere above the paddle row:
  - its huge per-cell weights act as a one-hot position code;
  - it never sits in the paddle row, because the ball bounces off there or the game ends.
- Channel 4 matches the ball's trail (previous position). It is nearly co-located with the ball, which explains why its weights resemble channel 1's.
- This is the MinAtar-Breakout channel set (paddle, ball, trail, brick) plus an explicit wall channel.

**Why the outputs look like action values:**
- Five outputs with three near-identical ones is what you'd expect from a 5-action set where three actions have the same effect in Breakout (noop/up/down or fire). Only left and right change the outcome.
- Outputs 3 and 4 are anti-correlated, as a left/right preference would be. They are also anti-correlated with the three "stay" outputs.

**Task:** A reinforcement-learning agent (most likely a DQN-style Q-network, possibly policy logits) for a Breakout-like game on a walled 10×10 grid, MinAtar-Breakout style.
- Inputs are a 10×10×5 one-hot state: paddle, ball, brick, wall, ball-trail.
- Outputs are values for 5 actions. Three of them are effectively "don't move" (outputs 0–2); the other two are left/right (outputs 3 and 4, order uncertain).

**Description:** The network reads a Breakout-style game board (bricks in the top rows, ball and its trail, paddle on the bottom row, border walls). It outputs a score per action, chiefly deciding whether to move the paddle left, right, or stay.
