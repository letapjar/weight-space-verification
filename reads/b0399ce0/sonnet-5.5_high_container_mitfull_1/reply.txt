**Reasoning:**

- A paddle-like agent confined to the bottom row, controlled left and right, fits the output layer.
- A single fast-moving object that must be tracked across the whole field fits the strong ch1 and ch4 weights.
- Static reward objects confined to the top rows fit the ch2 weights.
- The up/down actions are essentially unused, which rules out maze or free-movement games (Pac-Man, Snake, Freeway-type crossing, grid navigation).
- It also makes a shooter (Space Invaders-like) less likely. There the targets would be moving objects (ch1), and there would be a strong action to fire.
- Catch-style games have no top-row reward block.
- A pure avoidance game would use ch3 or negative ch1 weights, which I don't see.
- The combination of paddle at the bottom, bouncing ball and bricks in the top rows is Breakout.
