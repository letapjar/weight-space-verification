**Reasoning:**  
A bottom-confined agent, predominantly horizontal action selection, and a moving object tracked across successive frames strongly indicate a paddle-and-ball game. The upper reward-target band is the distinguishing clue: it fits bricks that the ball must hit.

Pong explains the paddle and ball but not the upper brick-like reward region. A falling-object catching game would more naturally have reward objects represented down their falling paths, rather than only in the upper rows. Snake, Pac-Man, and ordinary maze navigation are poor fits because their controlled characters occupy positions throughout the board, unlike the bottom-only agent signature here.

The weights identify the game family much more clearly than they identify exact scoring, paddle width, or life rules.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle left and right along the bottom to keep a bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball hitting and destroying bricks in the upper part of the grid, while the paddle prevents it from escaping below.
