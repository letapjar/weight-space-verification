**Reasoning**

Weights for inputs that never become non-zero during training stay near their random initial values (roughly ±0.06). The data show this for interior channel 3, all of channel 2, channel 0 in columns 1-7, and channel 1 in column 8. Trained weights (channel 1 in columns 1-7, channel 0 in column 8, channel 4 everywhere in the interior) are large. So in training the agent stays in the right-hand column and moves up and down. Left and right are effectively no-ops, which fits the similar noop/left/right biases. Moving objects roam the left seven columns and never occupy the agent's column, so anything reaching column 8 must end the episode. No interior obstacles exist, and reward-target cells are never used.

This fits a one-sided Pong or Squash-type game: a paddle on the right edge that moves vertically, a ball in the court bouncing off the top and bottom walls and the far wall, reward for returning the ball, and an episode that ends when the ball gets past the paddle. The large previous-frame weights match the need to estimate a ball's velocity. Breakout is ruled out because it would need interior bricks (obstacle or reward channels). Freeway is unlikely because its cars would cross the chicken's column, so column 8 would be trained. Snake, maze and pellet games need obstacles or reward targets, which are absent. Catch and falling-object games would need the agent to move horizontally.
