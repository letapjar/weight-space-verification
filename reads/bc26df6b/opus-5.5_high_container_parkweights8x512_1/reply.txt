**Reasoning:**
- The grid is 10×10 with an unused 1-cell border. Its object layout fits a Breakout-style game: a single paddle row at the bottom (row 8), bricks only in the top three rows, and a ball that appears everywhere above the paddle. The ball reaches the paddle row only rarely (miss states), so c4 has only moderate weights there.
- 5 linear outputs where three columns are interchangeable fit an action-scoring head over a generic action set: {no-op, up, down, left, right}. In Breakout, up and down do nothing, so they are equivalent to no-op and their output columns converge. Only left and right get distinct weights.
- The direction of the paddle effect identifies the two distinct outputs. A paddle on the right raises output 3, so output 3 is "left" and output 4 is "right". This matches the conventional ordering (stay, up, down, left, right).
- The outputs move against each other rather than sharing a common value component. That favours policy logits (an actor from PPO/A2C or behaviour cloning) over DQN Q-values, but I can't rule out Q-values.
- Ruled out:
  - Image classification: the inputs are sparse one-hot object channels with rigid row restrictions.
  - Regression to coordinates: three outputs are duplicates.
  - Sequence data: the periodic gaps exactly match grid borders.
