**Reasoning:**

- Between them are 6 horizontal lanes (rows 2–7). Each lane holds periodically spaced objects that move one cell per step and wrap around.
- The moving objects are not marked as obstacles, so they are hazards you dodge, not walls.
- The policy waits a lot (high no-op bias) and pushes toward the goal (down).

These features describe a lane-crossing game.

- Snake or food collection would put random reward targets across the grid. Here ch2 is a fixed row.

- Maze games would have inner ch3 obstacles. There are none.
- Pac-Man-style chasers would not move in rigid periodic lane patterns.

The objects are best read as cars rather than river logs, because every lane is treated the same way and the goal is simply reaching the far side.
