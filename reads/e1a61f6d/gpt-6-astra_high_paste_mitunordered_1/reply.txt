**Reasoning:**  
The combination fits a crossing game: moving objects that are also dangerous, a likely fixed destination across one edge, upward advancement, and waiting or sidestepping to avoid collisions. That is characteristic of **Frogger**.

A static maze does not naturally explain the duplicated moving-hazard inputs. Snake or ordinary food collection would more naturally require changing reward locations rather than an always-present destination strip. Standard paddle-and-ball games fit the directional output structure less well, while a simple fixed-column Flappy Bird game would not need meaningful sideways control. These alternatives are disfavored, not mathematically excluded: weights alone do not uniquely determine an environment.

**Game:** **Frogger — a small-grid road-crossing game.**

**Description:** The agent travels from the bottom toward the top, timing its movements and using safe gaps to avoid moving traffic. Reaching the opposite side or goal strip gives reward; colliding with traffic is penalized or ends the attempt.
