**Reasoning:**

- **What the layout shows.** A fixed internal maze that is identical in every episode rules out games with random or no interior obstacles: catch, pong/breakout, snake, a plain dodger, or an open-field chaser. Every channel is dead in the same 16 cells, so the agent, the movers and the rewards all share this one layout. That points to a single game rather than a mix.
- **Why it looks like Pac-Man.** The game has a controllable agent in a corridor maze, several roaming movers that the policy watches closely, and diffuse, many-cell rewards. Motion is inferred from the previous-frame channel, and no-op is penalised. This is the Pac-Man pattern: eat dots spread along the corridors while ghosts chase you.
- **The alternative.** A "catch the moving prey in a maze" game would put the reward on the mover itself, giving strong channel-2 weights that line up with channel 1. That is not what the weights show.
