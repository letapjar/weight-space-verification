*Dead cells and always-on cells.* An input that never changes gets either no training signal (always 0) or the same signal as the bias (always 1), so both are easy to spot.

- The border cells have all five channels at noise level (|w| ≲ 0.2), and their channel-3 rows mirror b1. For example, input 3 (cell (0,0), ch3) starts `-0.055 -0.161 -0.028 -0.195 …` against b1 `0.026 -0.135 -0.046 -0.105 …`.

**Reasoning**

- Paddle and shooter games (Pong, Breakout, Catch, Space Invaders) are ruled out: the agent would be confined to one row or column, and there would be no internal walls.
- Lane-crossing and dodging games (Freeway, Frogger) are ruled out: movers would sit in lanes, and there would be no static maze or targets spread over the grid.
- Snake is ruled out: there are fixed internal walls, there are independent movers, and targets sit in nearly every cell rather than one food item.
- Box-pushing (Sokoban) or a single-goal maze fits poorly: targets occupy almost every corridor cell with weak per-cell weights, and there are several strongly tracked movers that look like chasers.
- What remains is a fixed symmetric maze, collectibles on every corridor square except the start, and enemies roaming the corridors whose position and heading dominate the policy. That is a Pac-Man-style game.
