# What these weights are

**Best explanation:** this is the policy network (500 → 32 → 32 → 5, tanh) of an agent playing a small Pac-Man-style maze game. The input is a 10×10 grid with 5 binary channels per cell, flattened cell by cell. The 5 outputs are action logits: no-op, up, down, left, right. The agent eats pellets and avoids two enemy channels.

Everything below is read off the numbers by hand. The magnitudes I quote for output effects are first-order products (W2·W3) that ignore tanh saturation, so only their signs and rough sizes are meaningful.

## 1. Key observations

**The 500 inputs are 100 cells × 5 channels.** Rows of W1 repeat with period 5. Within each group of five, the rows are either all near-noise (σ ≈ 0.065) except one, or follow a fixed magnitude profile:

| Channel (row mod 5) | Typical weight size in "active" cells | Behaviour in "inactive" cells |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | huge (5–12) | noise |
| 1 | large (1–8) | noise |
| 2 | small (0.1–1.5) | noise |
| 3 | noise | small but identical pattern in every cell |
| 4 | medium (0.5–4.5) | noise |

**Channel 3 in inactive cells is a copy of the bias.** Its weights have the same sign pattern as b1 in all 52 such cells (e.g. unit 1 ≈ −0.17 vs b1 = −0.135; unit 9 ≈ +0.13 vs +0.132). That only happens if the input is the constant 1 in every sample, so it receives the bias gradient. It also implies b1 started at zero.

**The active/inactive cells form a maze.** Laying the 100 groups out as 10×10 (`#` = constant channel-3 cell, `.` = active cell):

```
##########
#........#
#.##..##.#
#.#....#.#
#...##...#
#...##...#
#.#....#.#
#.##..##.#
#...S....#
##########
```

This is a border wall plus 16 interior wall cells with 4-fold symmetry: an outer ring corridor and an inner ring around a central 2×2 block. There are 48 free cells and 52 walls. So channel 3 is "wall", and the layout never changes between samples.

**One free cell is special.** At cell (8,4), marked `S`, the channel-2 row is pure noise, while channels 0, 1 and 4 are trained there. Channel 2 is never on at that cell.

**Channel 2 is "usually on".** Its rows in free cells carry a faint copy of the bias pattern on top of small task weights, so it is present in most free cells most of the time.

**About 13 first-layer units are constants.** The 52 always-on wall inputs act as a ~53× bias. Units 5, 18, 19, 29, 30 (and weakly 26) are pinned positive; units 1, 3, 16, 20 (mostly also 2, 6, 31) are pinned negative. Their W1 columns have no large feature weights and their W2 rows are small. Roughly 19 units do the work: 0, 4, 7–15, 17, 21–25, 27, 28.

**The activation is tanh-like.** W2 rows of the positive-pinned units copy b2 (+0.49, −0.56, …); rows of the negative-pinned units are its mirror image (≈ −0.8, +0.7, …). Dead ReLUs would get no gradient; a unit stuck at −1 gets minus the bias gradient. ELU can't be fully excluded.

**The output layer looks softmax-trained.** W3 entries have RMS ≈ 1 but row sums have σ ≈ 0.4 (independent entries would give ≈ 2.2), and b3 sums to −0.07. Updates that sum to zero across outputs are the signature of a softmax loss.

**Output 0 is weak.** Its W3 column is the smallest and its bias is the lowest (−0.70).

## 2. Decoding the actions and channels

**Outputs 1/2 are one axis, 3/4 the other.** Several units are clean switches:

| Hidden unit | Effect on outputs (up, down, left, right) | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | −29, +28, ·, · | vertical switch |
| 28 | ·, ·, −18, +28 | horizontal switch |
| 7 | ·, ·, −30, +8 | "don't go left" |
| 0 | +8, +25, −2, −28 | vertical vs right |
| 15 | +6, −6, −9, +6 | up-right vs down-left |

**Channel 0 is the agent's position.** Unit 0's channel-0 weights are positive in the vertical-only corridor cells (columns 1 and 8: +4.6, +6.3, +2.7, +5.3, +4.3, +3.1, …) and strongly negative in the horizontal-only cells of the top corridor (−7.9, −6.0, −4.3, −7.9). The weights encode which moves are possible from the cell that channel 0 marks.

**Direction signs come from wall legality.** The strongest channel-0 weights never push into a wall under one assignment only:
- Unit 4: (1,5) +9.9, (3,6) +10.6, (1,8) +6.6 favour output 2, and in all three only "down" is open; (8,5) −6.6 favours output 1, where only "up" is open.
- Unit 28: of 13 cells where exactly one horizontal move is legal, 11 agree with output 4 = right, output 3 = left (e.g. (1,1) +6.3, (2,4) +6.2, (4,1) +5.9, (2,5) −8.2); 2 disagree.

So the order is 0 = stay, 1 = up, 2 = down, 3 = left, 4 = right. This assumes index÷10 is the row, counted downward.

**Channels 1 and 4 are things to avoid.**
- Unit 4 gets positive weights when channel 1 or channel 4 is in the upper rows (up to +4.8) and negative in the lower rows (down to −3.8 for channel 1, −4.5 for channel 4). Entity above → go down.
- Unit 7 gets large positive weights when either is on the left or bottom-left (channel 1 up to +6.8, channel 4 up to +3.8). Entity on the left → suppress "left".

The two channels have very similar spatial maps on units 4, 7 and 22, so they are the same kind of object. Channel 1 weights are about twice as large. Both are trained in every free cell, so both roam the whole maze.

**Channel 2 is a collectible.** Unit 15's channel-2 weights are positive for the top-right corner cells (+0.61 to +0.84) and negative for the top-middle cells (−0.3 to −0.8), and the unit pushes up and right. Remaining items attract the agent. Small weights fit many pellets being present at once.

**`S` is the agent's start.** A pellet can never be observed under the agent's starting cell, which explains the untrained channel-2 row at (8,4). The neighbouring cell (8,5), and (7,5) just beyond it, have unusually large pellet weights, plausibly because they are eaten in the first moves.

## 3. Hypotheses and verdicts

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Generic tabular or image classifier | Out | Strict period-5 structure, constant inputs, 2-D symmetric sparsity |
| 100-token one-hot sequence (text, DNA) | Out | Active positions form a 10×10 maze, not a 1-D pattern |
| Board-game move predictor | Out | Fixed walls, a single "self" marker, only 5 outputs |
| Single-goal maze navigation | Out | The attractive channel is on in most cells at once; two repulsive channels exist |
| Sokoban, Snake, Bomberman-like | Unlikely | No pushable or growing object signature; 5 outputs with a no-op |
| Q-network (DQN) | Unlikely | Zero-sum output rows and biases point to softmax logits, not values |
| ReLU network | Unlikely | Mirror-image outgoing weights from negatively pinned units |
| Policy for pellet collection with enemy avoidance | In | Everything above |
| Trained by policy gradient (PPO/A2C style) | Plausible | tanh, zero bias init, Gaussian init with σ ≈ √(2/500) = 0.063 matches orthogonal gain-√2 init |
| Behaviour cloning of an expert | Cannot exclude | Same softmax signature; weights alone do not separate it from RL |

## 4. What I cannot determine

- Whether channels 1 and 4 are two individual ghosts, two ghost types, or one always present and one occasional. The 2× weight difference suggests they differ in threat or frequency, but that is a guess.
- The exact algorithm and reward, and whether a separate value network exists.
- The up/down/left/right names depend on the row-major, top-down reading of the grid; the maze itself is symmetric under transposition.
- The linearised path strengths overstate real effects; I did not run forward passes on actual states.
