**Reasoning:**

- **Not an image or text classifier.** The cell-major structure with mostly constant cells rules out natural images, and text embeddings or bag-of-words would not show this spatial symmetry.
- **The inactive cells are walls.** Their inputs never change, so their weights stay at initialisation. That points to one hand-designed maze shared by all episodes.
- **The 5 features are one-hot object channels per cell.** Feature 0 is most likely the agent's own position, since it has the largest weights and drives the bottleneck. Features 1, 2 and 4 are probably other objects, such as an enemy, collectibles or a goal. Feature 3 is never non-zero on floor cells; it is probably a wall or unused channel.
- **The outputs look like movement actions.** Two opposite pairs plus one distinct output matches {no-op, up, down, left, right}: opposite moves get opposite preferences from the same hidden features. The linear output layer fits Q-values (as in DQN) or policy logits.
- **Supervised regression of coordinates is ruled out.** That would give 2–3 outputs, not 5 arranged as opposite pairs.

**Task:**

Reinforcement learning, most likely DQN-style Q-learning, in a small Pac-Man-like gridworld:
- a fixed symmetric 8×8 maze inside a 10×10 grid with a wall border;
- the state is a 5-channel one-hot occupancy map per cell (agent, plus objects such as enemies or pellets);
- the 5 outputs are values for the actions stay, up, down, left and right.

**Description:**

The network reads a flattened 10×10×5 one-hot gridworld observation, compresses it to an 8-dimensional code (mainly where the agent and other objects are), and outputs a score or Q-value for each of 5 discrete moves. This tells the agent which way to move through the maze.
