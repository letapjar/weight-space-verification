**Reasoning:**  
Together these indicate a vertically controlled paddle on the right, a moving ball to its left, and a three-column reward-bearing target field on the opposite side. That is the geometry of **sideways Breakout**.

Pong explains the paddle, ball, and vertical actions, but not nearly as naturally the broad, spatially variable reward-target field in columns 1–3. Ordinary falling-object catching games would instead suggest a bottom-row agent and left/right control. Snake, maze navigation, and general collection games fit poorly because the agent representation is confined to one column and the output layer emphasizes vertical control.

**Game:** **Breakout / brick breaker, rotated sideways**, with a vertical paddle on the right and bricks on the left.

**Description:** The agent moves its paddle up and down to bounce a ball into the brick field. Reward comes from hitting or destroying bricks while preventing the ball from escaping past the paddle.
