**Reasoning:**

The network shows four things:
- A controllable body confined to column 1.
- Movement only along the vertical axis, since left and right are functionally no-ops.
- A single moving object tracked over columns 2–8, with its velocity read from the previous-frame channel.
- No reward-target or obstacle cues.

That is the structure of a Pong-style paddle game. The paddle must move up and down to line up with an incoming ball.

Several alternatives are ruled out:
- **Snake or collection games.** These would need a heavy channel 2 and four-way movement.
- **Freeway or Frogger-style crossing.** The agent would move across rows and columns and watch rows of cars.
- **Maze or avoidance games.** These would put weight on the obstacles channel.
- **Breakout.** That would need a horizontal paddle at the bottom using left and right, plus bricks.
- **Catch with falling objects.** Objects would fall vertically onto a horizontally moving bottom paddle.

The moderate weights in column 8 (e.g. W1[91] `-0.286 -0.127 0.309 …`) might reflect an opponent paddle or just the ball's far wall. I cannot cleanly tell Pong against an opponent from solo Pong/squash against a back wall.
