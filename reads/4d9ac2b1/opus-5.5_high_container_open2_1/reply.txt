# What this is: a small trained neural network

This is the complete parameter set of a small trained MLP classifier or decision head. It has a narrow bottleneck. Its input is very likely a flattened 10×10 grid with 5 channels per cell. Only the inner 8×8 region and 2–3 of the channels were actually learned from. The network reduces all of that to roughly one or two decision variables. Its 5 outputs behave like only 3 distinct outputs.

All indices below are 0-based.

---

## 1. Architecture

| Layer | Shape | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| W1, b1 | 500 → 8 | 4,000 + 8 |
| W2, b2 | 8 → 512 | 4,096 + 512 |
| W3, b3 | 512 → 5 | 2,560 + 5 |
| **Total** | | **11,181** |

The shape is unusual: 500 inputs are squeezed to 8, expanded to 512, then reduced to 5. The 8-unit layer is a hard bottleneck. Everything the network knows about the input must pass through those 8 numbers. The wide 512 layer then acts like a piecewise-linear lookup table over that tiny code.

No activation functions are given. The weights strongly suggest ReLU hidden layers and a softmax output, for reasons explained in sections 3 and 5.

---

## 2. Layer 1 (W1): the input has a hidden grid structure

### Noise floor vs. learned weights

About 75% of W1's rows look like pure random initialisation. Their values are about N(0, 0.06) and never exceed about ±0.23. That is what you'd expect from roughly 3,000 draws of He-normal or Xavier-normal init for fan-in 500 (σ = √(2/500) ≈ 0.063).

The other rows have weights up to ±1.8. They are clearly learned.

The learned rows are not scattered randomly. They follow a strict pattern:

- Rows **0–54** and **442–499** are all noise.
- Between those, the active rows come in a period-5 pattern with gaps of 2, 3, 2, 3. For example: 59, 61, 64, 66, 69, 71, … 89, 91.
- After each block of 50 rows there is a dead gap of about 13 rows.

### Decoding the pattern: 500 = 10 × 10 × 5

If you read the input as a 10×10 grid with 5 channels per cell, flattened channel-last (index = row·50 + col·5 + channel), everything lines up:

- **Grid rows 0 and 9** (inputs 0–49 and 450–499) are entirely dead.
- **Grid columns 0 and 9** (offsets 0–4 and 45–49 in every 50-block) are dead.
- So **only the inner 8×8 region was learned from**. This looks like an 8×8 board or arena surrounded by a 1-cell border of padding or walls that never changes. Those inputs were constant, almost certainly 0, so their weights never moved from init.

Within the 8×8 interior, only some channels are used:

| Channel | Where it carries learned weights |
|---|---|
| ch 0 | only column 1 (all 8 rows; weaker in rows 4–5) |
| ch 1 | columns 2–8 (column 8 weak in most rows) |
| ch 2 | nowhere (noise everywhere) |
| ch 3 | nowhere (noise everywhere) |
| ch 4 | columns 1–7 (column 8 only occasionally) |

That is about 120 informative inputs out of 500.

The column offsets have a left/right asymmetry. ch 1 is never informative in the leftmost interior column, and ch 4 is never informative in the rightmost one. So the channels have something horizontal or directional in their meaning. I checked whether ch 4 at (r,c) and ch 1 at (r,c+1) are duplicate encodings of the same thing, and they are not: their weights differ. They also aren't strict one-hot complements of each other.

### Spatial pattern of the main weights

The strongest weights in **channel 1** form a quadrant pattern:

- **Upper-left** (rows 1–4, cols 2–4) is strongly **positive**. For example, input 61 (r1,c2) and inputs 111, 116, 161 and 166 all have their main units at about +0.5 to +1.2.
- **Lower-left** (rows 5–8, cols 2–4) is strongly **negative**. For example, inputs 261, 266, 311, 316, 361, 366 and 411 are at about −0.7 to −1.5.
- **Lower-right** (rows 5–8, cols 5–7) is mostly **positive**.
- **Upper-right** is weakly negative.

Channel 4 shows a similar top/bottom flip on the right side. Input 79 (r1,c5) is about −1.3, and input 429 (r8,c5) is about +1.5.

So the main learned feature is roughly "content in the upper-left or lower-right" minus "content in the lower-left or upper-right". That is a diagonal or quadrant-XOR-like detector, with a strong top-vs-bottom component on the left half. This is the most data-driven reading I can make. The channel meanings themselves are not recoverable from the weights alone.

## 3. The 8-unit bottleneck is highly redundant

Hidden units **0, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7** have nearly identical input weights. Here are two examples:

```
input 79:  -1.31 | -0.22 -0.30 | -1.46 -1.46 -1.31 -1.40 -1.14
input 429: +1.36 | -1.49 +0.68 | +1.47 +1.67 +1.49 +1.61 +1.53
           u0      u1    u2      u3    u4    u5    u6    u7
```

So six of the eight units carry essentially the same linear score s = w·x. Their biases are all close to 0.

**Unit 1** is roughly the negative of that score on the strong positive-evidence inputs: −1.83 at input 61, −1.49 at 429 and −1.1 at 111/116. It also has its own independent positive features: +1.05 at 264, +0.97 at 424, +0.94 at 374, +0.89 at 186 and +0.74 at 271.

This pairing is a classic ReLU signature. A one-sided activation can only pass a signed scalar if you keep a ReLU(+s) copy and a ReLU(−s) copy. A tanh network wouldn't need this.

**Unit 2** has large input weights: +1.06 at 269, +1.02 at 166, +0.99 at 281, −0.82 at 184 and −0.80 at 179. However, its outgoing weights (row 2 of W2) are the smallest in the network, nearly all below 0.06 in magnitude. It barely affects anything downstream. It is probably a dead or near-dead ReLU, or its information is redundant.

**Effective dimensionality:** the network uses roughly 2 to 3 real directions out of its 8 units. The "8" is mostly copies of one thing.

## 4. Layer 2 (W2, 8 → 512): a wide lookup over the bottleneck code

- W2 rows 4, 5 and 6 are the largest and are strongly correlated. They are the "main score" units.
- W2 row 1 often has the opposite sign, as expected given unit 1 is the anti-copy. For example, column 3 is +0.263 in row 1 versus −0.260 in row 4, and column 15 is −0.351 in row 1 versus +0.27 in row 4.
- The 512 units effectively tile a 2–3-dimensional space with thresholds. Many of them are near-useless. Only a few dozen have output weights above about 0.4 in magnitude (see section 5).

Tracing a few concrete units shows the logic:

| L2 unit | Fires when | Effect on outputs |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | score high (rows 4–6 positive, unit 1 negative) | out1 **+0.75** |
| 141 | (strongest out1 driver) | out1 **+1.02** |
| 3 | score low (unit 1 +0.26, rows 4–6 negative) | out1 **−0.51** |
| 16 | score low (rows 0, 3, 5 negative) | out2 **+0.70** |
| 27 | score high (rows 3–5 positive) | out2 **−0.95** |

## 5. Output layer (W3, b3): 5 outputs, but only 3 distinct behaviours

This is the most striking feature of the model.

**Outputs 0, 3 and 4 are nearly identical** in every one of the 512 rows, including rows with tiny weights:

```
row 16:  -0.112 | 0.040  0.699 | -0.152 -0.124
row 27:   0.128 | 0.001 -0.946 |  0.172  0.160
row 141: -0.125 | 1.023 -0.010 | -0.123 -0.130
row 112: -0.012 |-0.010 -0.036 | -0.011 -0.011
```

- **Outputs 1 and 2** are the only ones that carry real signal. The large driver units are *specialised*: each pushes one of them hard and leaves the other near 0. Smaller units often push them in opposite directions (e.g., row 0 is −0.104 vs +0.106; row 8 is +0.195 vs −0.182).
- **The tied outputs** get small weights of the opposite sign, about −0.13 to −0.18 times (out1 + out2). That is what you get from softmax cross-entropy, where whatever probability goes to classes 1 and 2 is taken from the others.
- **Biases** are b3 = [+0.225, −0.292, −0.295, +0.113, +0.142]. The default, no-evidence prediction is therefore one of the tied classes. Classes 1 and 2 have to be "earned" through the score.

**Interpretation:** the input information only distinguishes three cases.

- Score strongly positive → **class 1**
- Score strongly negative → **class 2**
- Otherwise → **classes 0, 3 and 4**, at their base rates

Their bias differences imply class 0 is roughly 10–12% more frequent than classes 3 and 4.

Three classes receiving identical gradients throughout training means the model never learned to tell them apart. Either they really are indistinguishable from this input, or the 8-unit bottleneck and training failed to separate them.

## 6. Clues about how it was trained

- **Initialisation:** W1 dead rows match N(0, ≈0.063), which is He- or Xavier-normal. They don't fit the PyTorch default uniform init (bounded at ±0.045) or Keras glorot-uniform (bounded at ±0.109).
- **Little or no weight decay on W1:** the dead weights kept their full init variance instead of shrinking toward 0.
- **The last layer probably started at or near zero, or was decayed:** with a normal random init, columns 0, 3 and 4 would differ by about 0.04–0.06 on small rows. They differ by only about 0.01–0.03.
- **Inputs are probably binary (one-hot or multi-hot):** learned weights reach about ±1.5, which makes sense when each active cell adds one weight.

---

## 7. What it most plausibly is

The most likely reading is a **small classifier or policy over an 8×8 board or gridworld**. The board is padded to 10×10 and encoded with 5 feature planes per cell. The network learned to rely on about 2 of those planes. It compresses the board into essentially one or two scalar decision variables, then makes a 3-way decision exposed through 5 outputs.

Two variants fit equally well:

1. **5-class softmax classifier** in which classes 0, 3 and 4 are never separated. This could be a sign of underfitting or bottleneck collapse.
2. **RL policy or Q-head with 5 actions** (e.g., noop/up/down/left/right), where three actions are equivalent in the environment and only two directional actions are informed by the state. The top-vs-bottom structure in channel 1 is loosely consistent with outputs 1 and 2 being an up/down-style pair, but that is speculative.
