**Observations:**

**Input layout.** The 500 inputs are arranged cell by cell, five channels per cell: index = 5·(10·row + col) + channel. Two kinds of row stand out against the background of small (~±0.1) initialisation-scale weights.

- **Rows that copy the bias pattern.** Rows 3, 8, 13, …, 53 are the channel-3 inputs of the top wall and the left wall. Their 32 weights mirror b1:
  - unit 17 is about −0.40 (b1[17] = −0.400)
  - unit 14 is about −0.2 to −0.3 (b1[14] = −0.236)
  - unit 27 is about +0.2 (b1[27] = 0.210)

  This is what happens to an input that is always 1: it receives the same gradient as the bias. On the right wall (column 9), two consecutive rows per cell have this pattern: 97/98 for cell (1,9) and 147/148 for cell (2,9). So both channel 2 (reward) and channel 3 (obstacle) are constantly 1 there. The right wall is permanently a reward target. The left wall (row 52, cell (1,0) channel 2) and the top-right corner (row 47) have small weights in channel 2, so they are not reward targets.
- **Rows with large weights (magnitudes 1–7).** These are inputs that actually vary during play.
  - Column 1 (cells 11, 21, 31, …): only channel 0 (agent) and channel 4 (previous frame) are large. For example, row 55 has −0.841 −4.414 −1.964 4.186 …, and row 105 has 0.254 −2.235 −2.502 …. Channel 1 in column 1 is tiny (row 56: −0.045 −0.070 …).
  - Columns 2–8 (for example rows 61, 66, 71, 111, 116, …): only channel 1 (moving object) and channel 4 are large. The agent channel there stays at init size (row 60: −0.002 −0.049 …; row 110: 0.180 0.046 …).
  - Interior channel 2 and channel 3 are tiny everywhere (rows 62, 63, 112, 113 …). There are no collectible items and no hazards inside the field.
  - Column 9 channel 4 is tiny (row 99: 0.033 0.002 …). The moving object never sits on the right wall.

  So the agent lives only in the leftmost playable column, moving objects only in columns 2–8, and the only reward marker is the whole right wall.

**Output layer.** The large output weights are almost all in the up/down columns:
- unit 6: up +4.727
- unit 30: down −2.691
- unit 10: up −1.603, down +1.526
- unit 16: up +1.302, down −1.235
- unit 2: up −1.068, down +1.262

The left and right columns are mostly small. b3 = [−0.084, −0.819, −0.271, 0.916, 0.132]. The "left" bias is the highest, and in column 1 a left move pushes into the wall, so it effectively acts as "stay". The policy is a vertical up/down controller.

**Reasoning:**
- **A paddle game.** The agent is confined to one column and only steers vertically. The thing it reacts to moves freely through the middle of the field. That object is neither a reward nor an obstacle, which fits a ball.
- **Reward from sending the ball across.** Reward is attached only to the far (right) wall. So reward comes from getting the ball to the opposite side, which means keeping it in play and returning it.
- **Ruled out:**
  - Snake and collect-the-item games: there are no varying reward cells inside the field, and the agent never leaves column 1.
  - Breakout: there are no varying bricks in channel 2.
  - Dodge games and Frogger/Freeway-style crossing: the moving objects are not in the obstacle channel, and the agent never moves rightward.
  - Catch games: the falling items would appear in channel 2.
- **Left unresolved:** a second moving object (an opponent paddle) in column 8 cannot be excluded from the weights.

**Game:** Pong, a single-player Pong / squash-style variant. The agent is a paddle on the left edge, and the right wall is the scoring goal.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle up and down along the left column to intercept a bouncing ball and send it back across the field. Reward is earned when the ball reaches the right wall.
