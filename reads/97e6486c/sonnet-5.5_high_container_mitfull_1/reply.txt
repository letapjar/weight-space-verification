**Observations:**

- **Wall border.** The obstacle channel (channel 3) has a distinctive weight pattern on every border cell. Examples are input 3 (cell (0,0)), 8, 13, 18, 23 and so on. The same pattern shows up in the hidden-unit columns 3, 9, 11, 13, 19, 23, 27, 29 and 31. The agent, moving and reward channels on border cells are all small, about ±0.1. So the agent never stands on the border.
- **Fixed interior obstacles.** On 16 interior cells, the agent, moving, reward and previous-frame weights are tiny, but the channel-3 weights carry the wall-like pattern. Those cells are (2,2), (2,3), (3,2), (2,6), (2,7), (3,7), (6,2), (7,2), (7,3), (6,7), (7,6), (7,7), and the centre block (4,4), (4,5), (5,4), (5,5). I found them by counting rows of W1, so a miscount is possible.
  - Together they form four L-shaped pieces in the corners plus a central 2x2 block. The layout is mirror-symmetric, so it is a fixed hand-designed maze, not random clutter.
  - On the remaining 48 free cells, channel 3 is near zero, for example input 58 at (1,1) has first value -0.039.
- **Agent channel.** Weights on the free cells are very large, for example input 55 at (1,1): 1.703, 6.706 (unit 7) and -4.323 (unit 25). Input 215 at (4,3) has 6.563. Input 305 at (6,1) has 6.378. Input 415 at (8,1) has 5.59. Input 375 at (7,4) is about -7. So the network is strongly position-dependent. It is a single-position or few-cell agent that navigates the maze.
- **Moving-object and previous-frame channels.** These are also large on every free cell. Input 56 is up to -2.25. Input 76 has 7.385. Input 91 has 4.62. The previous-frame weights track the moving-object weights, which suggests the network uses them to infer the direction of motion of other mobile entities.
- **Reward channel.** Weights are moderate, about ±1 to 2, with a spatial gradient. Reward on the left of the grid pushes hidden unit 14 negative, for example inputs 57, 67 and 157. W3 row 14 is [-0.20, 0.17, 1.28, -2.79, 1.03], so that pushes left and suppresses down and right. Reward on the right pushes it positive, for example input 72 at 1.868. So the policy is attracted toward rewards.
- **Output layer.** The no-op bias is -0.853 and the other four biases are positive, so the policy prefers to keep moving.
  - Unit 6 and unit 24 drive "up": W3 [-0.58, 1.87, ...] and [0.19, 2.50, 0.50, 0.22, -3.12].
  - Units 12 and 14 drive "down" and "right" and suppress "left".
  - Unit 7 and unit 25 drive "left".
  - These units have large weights on the agent channel, which is consistent with position-dependent steering through the maze.

**Reasoning:**

- The fixed, symmetric interior walls with an open corridor-like layout point to a maze game, not an open arena. That rules out Snake-style games and random-obstacle games.
- Mobile entities other than the agent carry heavy weights everywhere, and the previous-frame channel supports tracking their motion. Strong avoidance or pursuit reactions fit enemies that chase.
- Rewards are spread across the free cells and are gradient-like. I could not tell whether they are many static pickups or a few targets.
- The main alternative is a maze-based "collect the target" game with wandering objects. Pac-Man-style gameplay fits all of the above best, but I cannot distinguish it from a similar maze game with certainty.
