**Reasoning:**
- The agent appears everywhere in the 8×8 area, including the top row. That rules out paddle or shooter games where the agent stays on one row or column: Catch, Breakout, Pong, Space Invaders.
- The interior obstacle channel is untrained. That rules out mazes, gridworlds with walls, Pac-Man with a maze, and Snake-like games where the body becomes an obstacle.
- Channel 1 is heavily used, together with the previous frame. That rules out static navigation and Snake, which has no separate movers.
- Reward targets appear in every row. That rules out Frogger and Freeway, where the reward sits in a goal row at the top.
- The ch1 and ch2 weights are very different in size, so the moving objects are not themselves the reward targets. This argues against a pure chase-the-prey game.
- What remains is an open arena:
  - The agent moves in four directions.
  - Moving hazards wander or chase, and the agent must track their motion.
  - A collectible target respawns at random interior locations.
- Two details fit this picture. Avoiding the hazards dominates the representation, which matches the huge ch0/ch1/ch4 weights. Heading toward the target is a secondary signal, which matches the moderate ch2 weights coded mostly by column and presence.

**Game:** An open-arena collect-and-dodge game. It is like Pac-Man with no maze: the agent grabs a coin or pellet while avoiding roaming enemies, a "ghosts and coins" style game.

**Description:** The agent moves freely around the walled 8×8 arena. It gets reward by reaching a randomly placed target (coin/pellet), which then respawns, and it must avoid the moving enemies, since touching one is bad or ends the episode.
