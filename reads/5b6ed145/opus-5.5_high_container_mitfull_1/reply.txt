**Reasoning:**
- **Internal maze walls.** The fixed maze is the decisive clue. It rules out open-arena games (Catch, Pong, Breakout, Dodge, Freeway/Frogger) and Snake, which has no other moving objects and whose body is not a fixed pattern.
- **Moving objects throughout the maze.** Channel 1 is heavily used at every open cell, so there are enemies that roam the corridors.
- **Rewards everywhere.** Many small reward targets spread over all corridor cells, with weights acting like a count, fit pellets rather than a single goal. A goal-only game such as Frogger would only train the top-row weights.
- **Motion matters.** The strong previous-frame weights mean enemy direction matters. That suits avoiding chasers.
- **Always moving.** The negative no-op bias fits a game where standing still is useless or dangerous.

Together, a symmetric corridor maze, pellets everywhere, roaming enemies and constant motion is the Pac-Man pattern.
