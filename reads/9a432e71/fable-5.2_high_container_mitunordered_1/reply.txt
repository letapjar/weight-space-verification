**Reasoning:**
- **Ruled out by the action structure.** Left and right behaving as no-ops means the agent only moves vertically along one column. That excludes Snake, mazes, Breakout, Catch and Space-Invaders-style games, which need horizontal or 2-D movement.
- **Pong fits best.** Symmetric, heavy use of both up and down is what tracking a ball with a side paddle looks like. The input count (8 paddle cells, one object over the rest of the board, plus previous-frame cells for direction) matches a single ball.
- **Freeway is the runner-up.** A road-crossing game would explain the 8 constant cells as a goal row. But it would be dominated by "up versus wait", with down nearly unused, and a 6-lane version would give only about 105 varying inputs rather than about 128.
- **Unexplained by Pong.** The 8 extra constant cells do not have a natural Pong role; my best guess is a fixed goal or target line. This is the main reason my confidence is moderate.
