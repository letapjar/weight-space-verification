**Reasoning:**

- **What the encoding most likely is:** 100 positions, each with 5 categorical or binary channels. The most natural reading is a 10×10 board with 5 cell‑type channels.
- **Why channel 3 looks like a wall layer:** a fixed set of about 52 cells is permanently in state 3, so channel 3 behaves like a static wall or obstacle layer. The remaining about 48 cells never take state 3 and are the only cells whose contents vary.
- **What channels 0, 1 and 4 probably are:** their very large per‑cell weights suit channels that are "on" in only one or a few cells, such as an agent or a target. A single active input must be able to drive the tanh by itself.
- **What channel 2 probably is:** its smaller weights fit a channel that is on in many cells at once, such as items or floor.
- **What the lookup‑table weights imply:** the output depends on *which specific cell* something occupies, not on smooth coordinates. That is what a value function or policy over a fixed obstacle map looks like.
- **What the outputs probably are:** five linear outputs fit Q‑values or action logits for {up, down, left, right, stay}.
- **Ruled out:**
  - Raw time series or images: every slot would be used, and the weights would be smooth across neighbouring positions.
  - DNA one‑hot: all 4–5 channels would be used at every position.
  - Border walls only: corner cell 0 is active.
- **Weak points in my interpretation:**
  - The free cells do not form a 4‑connected maze. Cells (0,0), (0,6), (0,9) and (2,1) are isolated, so agents may be placed at random rather than having to walk there.
  - A generic table of 100 five‑level categorical variables, about half of which are constant, cannot be excluded.
