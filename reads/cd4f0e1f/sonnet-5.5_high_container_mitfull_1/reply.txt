**Observations:**

- **Input layout.**
  - Rows of W1 follow the layout (input = cell·5 + channel). The border cells' obstacle inputs (e.g. inputs 3, 8, 13, …, 53, 98, 448) carry the same ±0.2–0.5 pattern as b1. They are always on, so they act as extra bias.
  - Obstacle inputs inside the playable area are essentially noise (|w| < 0.15). Nothing in the interior is an obstacle.
  - All of row 9 is small, so the bottom wall row does not matter.

- **Output layer.**
  - Up (action 1) and down (action 2) have large, opposed weights from the same hidden units. Hidden unit 16 gives +2.90 up and -1.00 down. Hidden unit 22 gives +1.35 up and -2.77 down. Hidden units 12 and 21 push down by 1.99 and 2.31.
  - Left and right weights are small and nearly identical, mostly under 1.
  - The biases (0.099, 0.027, -0.058, 0.276, -0.125) are modest.
  - The policy is therefore essentially a vertical move/stay controller. Horizontal movement is irrelevant.

**Reasoning:**

- **Why vertical tracking.** The agent lives in one edge column and only slides up or down. Its decision depends on the position and direction of fast-moving things in the rest of the board. That points to a paddle-and-ball game.
- **Why not Freeway, Frogger or an avoidance game.**
  - Cars would need to cross the agent's column, so column-8 ch 1 would carry weight. It carries none.
  - There would be no dense reward block on one side.
- **Why not a maze or collection game.** The agent doesn't move in 2D, and there are no interior obstacles.
- **Why not Pong.** Pong has no static reward cells. Here reward cells fill columns 1–3 across all rows, forming a wall of bricks opposite the paddle.
- **Why not Space Invaders-style shooters.** There is no fire action. Previous-frame weights as large as the current-frame ones fit a ball whose velocity matters, not a slowly marching formation.
- **Why not Catch.** Falling objects would be reward-bearing moving objects spread across the board, not a fixed block in columns 1–3.
- **Fit.** A ball (ch 1) bouncing around columns 1–7 fits the large weights there. Its previous position (ch 4) gives velocity. Bricks (ch 2) sit in columns 1–3. The paddle (ch 0) is on the right edge.
  - Column 1 ball weights are weaker, perhaps because the ball usually bounces off the bricks before reaching it.

**Game:** Breakout, in a sideways variant. The paddle is a vertical bar on the right edge, the bricks are on the left (columns 1–3), and the ball bounces between them.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle up and down the right-hand column to keep a bouncing ball in play (letting it get past ends the episode). It gets reward each time the ball knocks out a brick in the left-side wall.

**Confidence:** 60%
