**Reasoning:**

- An agent confined to one edge column, moving only up and down and tracking a single moving object and its trail, is a paddle-and-ball game.
- It is not plain Pong, because there is a static block of reward targets three cells deep on the far side from the paddle. That is a brick wall, so the game is Breakout turned on its side.
- Ball weights also appear inside columns 1–3, which fits a ball entering the brick region as bricks are cleared.
- Games that do not fit:
  - Dodging or collecting games (Freeway, Frogger, Asterix): these need interior hazards on channel 3, or an agent that moves across the grid.
  - Snake or maze collection: the agent would appear everywhere, and the targets would be spread over the grid.
  - Space Invaders-style shooters: the targets would themselves move, and a fire action would matter.

**Game:** Breakout (MinAtar-style), rotated 90° — the paddle moves vertically along the right edge (column 8) and the bricks fill columns 1–3 on the left.

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle up and down the right-hand column to bounce a ball back toward a wall of bricks on the left. It is rewarded for each brick the ball breaks, and the episode ends if the ball gets past the paddle.
