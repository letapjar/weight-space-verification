**Reasoning**

A goal strip along one edge, the agent starting at the opposite edge, six wrapping lanes of paired moving objects in between, and a policy biased toward the goal with waiting and sidestepping is a road-crossing game.

Other game types do not fit:
- Collecting or catching games would have reward targets that vary in position; here they are a constant row.
- Shooters and paddle games would pin the agent to one row; here it occupies rows 1–7.
- Snake or maze games would show a growing body or interior walls; channel 3 is empty inside the border.

Because the agent uses left and right across many columns, it is closer to Frogger than to Atari Freeway, where the chicken only moves up and down. There is no river or log structure, only the road part.

**Game:** A Frogger-style road-crossing game (Freeway-like), played top to bottom: start in row 1, cross six traffic lanes in rows 2–7, reach the goal row 8.

**Description:** The agent starts near the top middle and moves down through six lanes of horizontally moving, wrapping cars (two per lane, four cells apart), waiting or sidestepping to avoid them. Reward comes from reaching the bottom goal row; hitting a car presumably ends the episode or costs reward.
