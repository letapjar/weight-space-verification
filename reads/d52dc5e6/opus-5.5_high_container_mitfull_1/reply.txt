  - Down is strongly suppressed.

- **The output weights confirm up and no-op are steerable.** Several layer-2 units push up hard against down, for example unit 6 (up +2.332) and unit 19 (up +2.004). Others push down or no-op, for example unit 15 (down +1.783, up −1.838) and unit 22 (no-op +2.068, right −5.826). So the policy can switch between "advance up" and "wait", and only occasionally moves sideways.

**Reasoning:**

- **What the biases imply.** Waiting is the default action and moving down is almost never useful. That points to a game where the agent starts at the bottom and makes progress by moving up, but often has to hold still to avoid danger.
- **Road-crossing game (best fit).** A Freeway or Frogger-style game fits this well:
  - The agent starts on the bottom row, so down is useless or harmful.
  - Cars move across lanes; these would be the moving objects in channel 1.
  - Reward comes from reaching the top; that would be the reward target in channel 2.
  - No-op is the safe "wait for a gap" action, and up is the scoring action.
- **Ruled out or weaker:**
  - Snake and food-collection games need all four directions used fairly evenly, and they rarely favour no-op.
  - A paddle or catcher game would rely mostly on left and right, with up and down suppressed. Here, up is not suppressed while down is.
  - A Flappy-Bird-style game (gravity plus flap) is a possible alternative, since it also has a no-op/up dominance. But its obstacles would scroll, and the biases fit a road crossing slightly better.
- **Limits of this answer.** W1 gave me no usable spatial evidence by hand, so this conclusion rests mostly on the action biases and output weights.
