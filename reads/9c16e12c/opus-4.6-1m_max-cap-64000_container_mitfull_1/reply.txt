## Reasoning

The combined evidence points to a game with these properties:

1. **The agent is restricted to specific rows** (ch0 small at top) — a paddle fixed near the bottom.
2. **One object moves freely across the grid** (ch1 large everywhere, same h1 pattern at every position) — a bouncing ball.
3. **Static reward targets occupy the top rows** (ch2 large at rows 1–2, completely different h1 units from ch1) — destructible bricks.
4. **Nothing in the playable area is deadly** (ch3 uniformly small) — the ball bounces off the paddle harmlessly.
5. **Trajectory prediction is paramount** (ch4 has the absolute largest weights, mirroring ch1's pattern) — the paddle must predict where the ball is going from its previous and current positions.
6. **Dominant horizontal actions** (many W3 units with large left/right weights) — the paddle moves left and right.
7. **Edge cells matter less** (ch1 smaller at columns 1 and 8) — the ball bounces off walls quickly and spends more time in central columns.

Ruled out:
- **Space Invaders**: alien bullets would be deadly (ch3 should be large).
- **Freeway/Frogger**: the agent primarily moves up/down, not left/right.
- **Pong**: the paddle moves up/down.
- **Catch/Catcher**: the falling objects ARE the rewards, so ch1 and ch2 would be active at the same cells — but here they activate disjoint h1 units, proving they represent different objects at different locations.
- **Snake**: no independently moving objects (ch1 would be small).

The only common game where a horizontally-moving paddle tracks a bouncing ball to destroy static reward targets, with no lethal obstacles, is Breakout.

## Game

**Breakout**

## Description

The agent controls a paddle at the bottom of the grid, moving left and right to intercept a bouncing ball and deflect it into rows of bricks at the top; each brick destroyed gives reward, and the network relies most heavily on the ball's previous-frame position to predict its trajectory and position the paddle accordingly.
