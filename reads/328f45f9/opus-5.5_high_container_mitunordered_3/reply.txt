Observations:
- **The inputs are shuffled.** No contiguous block lines up with the wall border. In the standard layouts the first rows of W1 would be border cells, and border cells should carry no information. Yet rows 1, 2 and 9 are strongly weighted:

  - row 2: −8.192 on unit 0, 7.046 on unit 7
  
  So I had to classify rows by their statistics instead of their position.
- **Four kinds of rows.**
  1. About 140 rows are strongly informative, with weights of several units. Examples: −10.535 / 10.714; 4.628 / 10.607; 10.005 and 10.358 on unit 28; −12.391 and −12.332 on unit 21.
  2. About 40–50 rows are moderately informative, in small recurring groups. One group loads only on units 9 and 22: (−0.776, −1.124), (0.992, 1.525), (−1.619, 1.368), (1.838, 1.651), (−1.086, 2.048). Another loads on units 10, 14, 15 and 23, e.g. (−0.823, −0.839) and (0.647, 0.814).
  3. About 45–50 rows share one "constant-input" signature: about −0.15 to −0.27 on units 1, 3 and 16, and about +0.12 to +0.25 on units 5, 9, 18, 19, 22, 23, 29 and 30. Examples are rows like "−0.060 −0.268 −0.275 … 0.190 … 0.168 0.159 … 0.195 0.131 0.188" and "−0.022 −0.215 … 0.258". These inputs act as an extra bias, so they are (nearly) always on. That is more than the 36 wall-border cells, which points to fixed interior walls and/or inputs that are on most of the time.
  4. The rest (about 250 rows) stay at initialization size (about ±0.1). These inputs are never active.
- **Dead units.** Hidden units 1, 3, 5, 16, 18, 19, 20, 29 and 30 receive almost nothing except the constant-input signature.
- **Output layer.** All four movement directions are used in a structured way:
  - one unit has up +2.509 against down −2.341
  - one has left +2.779 against right −0.414
  - one has right +2.045 against left −1.057 and down −1.5
  - one has up −2.579 against noop +1.043 and right +1.105
  
  Biases are noop −0.697, up −0.024, down +0.073, left +0.195, right +0.385. No-op is the most disfavoured action, but it is still used in some situations (noop weights reach +1.09 and +1.13).

Reasoning:
- **Movement is two-dimensional.** Up and down are clearly meaningful and opposed. That rules out paddle-only games such as Breakout, Pong, Catch and Space Invaders.
- **Several channels carry information.** About 180 informative inputs is roughly three to four channels over the free inner cells: the agent position, previous-frame motion, and at least one more spatial channel. A plain goal-reaching task with a single target would not need this much.
- **Some cells are on most of the time.** There are more "always-on" inputs than border cells, plus groups of weakly informative inputs that are usually on. This fits fixed interior walls plus many reward items that are present most of the time and disappear when collected, i.e. pellets.
- **Moving hazards fit too.** A moving-object channel plus the previous-frame channel would explain strongly weighted inputs beyond the agent's own position.
- **Snake is the main alternative.** It would explain the multi-channel structure. It fits the excess of constant inputs and the multi-hot "mostly on" reward pattern less well.
