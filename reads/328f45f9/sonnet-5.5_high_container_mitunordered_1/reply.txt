  - Unit 17 has right +1.583 and left −1.661.
  - Unit 4 has right −2.059 and left +1.085.
  - Unit 18 has left +2.779.
  - Unit 12 has down +1.935.

  - This looks like "target is above/below/left/right, so go that way" and "danger is on a side, so go the other way". It is a 2D, four-direction control problem.

**Reasoning:**
- Up/down are used as heavily as left/right, so this is not a paddle-style game restricted to one axis such as Breakout or Pong. The agent moves freely in 2D.
- The no-op penalty means standing still is not rewarded, so it is not a pure dodging or survival game.
- The widespread, high-magnitude weights on many cells suggest rewards and other entities appear all over the grid, as in pellet collection or spawning targets.
- Antagonistic weights, with the same unit pushed positively by some inputs and strongly negatively by others (e.g. units 10 and 11 at about −5 to −8 for a family of rows), suggest both attraction and avoidance. That fits hazards or moving enemies plus a prev-frame channel used to read their velocity.
- I cannot distinguish this from a Snake-like game (multi-cell agent, food, self-collision) with the weights alone. A pure maze or navigation game with static obstacles seems less likely, given the heavy use of the dynamic channels.
