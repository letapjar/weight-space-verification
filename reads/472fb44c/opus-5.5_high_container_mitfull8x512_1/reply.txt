**Reasoning:**

The agent is a horizontal paddle in the bottom row and only left and right matter. That rules out navigation games such as Snake, maze games and collectors, which would show agent weights all over the grid and meaningful up/down actions.

A paddle tracking a moving object points to Catch, Pong or Breakout.

- **Plain Catch** has a ball that falls straight down, so the previous-frame channel would add nothing, yet here it is heavily used. Catch would also have no separate reward objects fixed in the top rows.
- **Pong** would put an opponent paddle in row 1 as a moving object. It would not explain reward targets confined to rows 1–3.
- **Breakout** fits every observation:
  - The ball bounces, so the network needs its velocity, which it reads from channel 4.
  - Bricks are reward targets that occupy the top rows (1–3) and disappear when hit, giving the channel-2 structure. Row 3, the lowest brick row, is broken most often, which matches it having the strongest weights.
  - The ball reaches row 1, and especially its corners, only after bricks are cleared, which gives the cone shape in the channel-1 weights.
  - The paddle never shares its row with the ball.

**Game:** Breakout (a mini Breakout / Arkanoid-style brick breaker on the 8×8 field).

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle left and right along the bottom row to keep a bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball hitting and destroying the bricks in the top rows, and the episode ends if the ball gets past the paddle.
