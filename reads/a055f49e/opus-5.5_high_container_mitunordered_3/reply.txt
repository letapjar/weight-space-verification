**Observations:**

- **Inputs are scrambled, so I grouped the 500 W1 rows by behaviour rather than by position.** About 330 rows are near-random noise (|w| < 0.2). These are inputs that never turn on.
- **About 44 rows are copies of the bias b1.** They show the same signature as b1:
  - b1 at units 5, 12, 23, 25 is −0.355, −0.246, −0.328, +0.366.
  - Row 14 has −0.308, −0.136, −0.329, +0.481 at those units.
  - Row 216 has −0.441, −0.227, −0.258, +0.332.
  - Rows 11, 28, 32, 70, 80, 93, 114, 139 to 151, 168, 178, 223 to 232, 347 to 350, 417 to 482 and others look the same.
  
  An input that is always 1 gets exactly the bias's gradient, so these are constant inputs. 36 of them are the wall border in channel 3. That leaves about 8 more constant cells, which is a full row's worth of inner cells (8 per row). This looks like a fixed line of cells, most likely a fixed goal row in the reward channel.
- **About 128 rows have large weights (|w| up to 7–10).** Examples are rows 1, 13, 36, 71, 183, 267 (−7.05), 270 (+7.08) and 453 (+9.89). These are the inputs that change during play: the agent, the moving objects, and the previous-frame channel. I found no further block of about 64 changing rows, so the reward targets are not randomly placed.
- **The output layer is lopsided.**
  - b3 = [noop 0.360, up −0.915, down −0.136, left 0.456, right 0.238].
  - The up column has the largest swings: +1.538, +1.515, +1.323, +1.077, and −1.885.
  - The down column is almost all strongly negative (−2.523, −2.075, −2.073, −1.177, −1.085); its largest positive weight is only 0.704.
  - Noop, left and right move together and stay small, for example rows 18 (0.891, 0.863, 0.730), 25 (0.615, 0.743, 0.731) and 31 (0.420, 0.527, 0.463).
  - So the policy's real choice is "go up" against "wait". Left and right act like waiting, and down is mostly suppressed.

**Reasoning:**

- **Ruled out: snake, collect-the-food and random-goal games.** Those need a reward channel whose position changes, and none is visible.
- **Ruled out: fixed-maze navigation.** It would have no moving objects, and it would not make horizontal moves useless.
- **Ruled out: Pong or other paddle games.** They would use up and down symmetrically, but here down is strongly suppressed while up is the active choice.
- **Ruled out: Breakout or Catch.** They depend on left and right, which this network treats like noop.
- **What fits:**
  - A single agent whose progress is purely upward.
  - Moving hazards that the network has to read through the current and previous frame. This explains the many strong, changing inputs.
  - A fixed goal row, which explains the extra row of constant cells.
  - "Wait" as the main alternative to "up", used to let hazards pass.
  
  This is a road-crossing game.

**Game:** A Freeway / Frogger-style road-crossing game.

**Description:** The agent starts near the bottom and moves upward across lanes of horizontally moving cars, waiting (noop, or equivalently left or right) to avoid being hit. It gets a reward when it reaches the fixed goal row at the top.

**Confidence:** 40%
