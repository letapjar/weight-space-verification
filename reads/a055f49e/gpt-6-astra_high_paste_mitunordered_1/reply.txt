- The output biases are \((0.360,-0.915,-0.136,0.456,0.238)\): they favor those neutral actions before state-dependent contributions. They do **not**, by themselves, establish actual action frequencies.

**Reasoning:**  
The strongest fit is a **vertically controlled paddle-and-ball game**. Restricted paddle positions, a ball moving through the arena, and previous-frame positions can explain the relatively small learned input support. The approximate support counts are also compatible with paddle positions along an edge and moving-object positions elsewhere. A fixed additional line could represent the opposite goal or boundary.

Standard **Breakout** and falling-object **Catch** would principally distinguish left/right, not up/down. Standard **Snake**, **Pac-Man**, or general maze navigation would normally distinguish all four directions. **Flappy Bird** usually needs flap versus idle, whereas this network gives down a substantial, independent role.

A different vertical-only game cannot be excluded from weights alone, but the combined input structure and three-effective-action output pattern make Pong the best explanation. Exact scoring and opponent mechanics are less identifiable.

**Game:** **Pong**, in a small-grid version with an up/down-controlled side paddle.

**Description:** The agent moves its paddle vertically to intercept and return a bouncing ball, preventing the ball from passing its own side. Reward most likely comes from successful returns and/or scoring against the opposite side; the precise reward convention is not recoverable confidently here.
