*How I mapped the inputs.* The rows don't follow `cell*5 + channel` in a simple spatial order, since cells appear shuffled. But within each block of 5 rows, the channel order (0–4) holds. So I classified each row by `i mod 5`.

**Channel 3 (obstacles).** About 36 rows with `i mod 5 = 3` have a "bias-copy" pattern that matches b1 closely. For example:
- Row 8: `0.275 -0.225 -0.112 0.179 … 0.363 (unit 10) … 0.139 (unit 21)`

An input that is always 1 receives the same gradient as the bias, so it ends up looking like the bias. These rows are therefore the 36 border cells, where the wall is always present. Channel 3 is small everywhere else, so there are no interior obstacles.

**Reasoning:**
- **The agent is confined to one column**, and left/right behave like no-op. That rules out free-roaming games such as Snake, Pac-Man-style collectors, and Frogger with sideways movement.
- **Reward sits on a fixed 8-cell edge**, not on spawning items, which points to a "reach the far side" objective. This rules out catch games and apple collectors, where channel 2 would vary across interior cells.
- **The field is full of moving objects** whose direction matters (heavy channel 1 + channel 4 weights everywhere). This fits horizontally moving traffic lanes.
- **The output units say "don't go up when danger is above, step down to dodge."** This is exactly the timing behaviour needed to cross traffic.
- Pong- or paddle-style games would also give a single-column agent. But they would not produce a constant reward edge, and they would emphasise one ball rather than objects in every lane.
