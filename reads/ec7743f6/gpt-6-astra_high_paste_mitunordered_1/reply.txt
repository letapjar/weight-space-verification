- The output biases are `(0.025, -0.120, -0.049, -0.038, 0.388)`. Right has a modest baseline advantage, but the state-dependent weights are much larger.

**Reasoning:**
This looks like **horizontal paddle control**, rather than ordinary Snake, Pac-Man, or maze navigation, where both movement axes matter. Standard vertically controlled Pong is also a poor fit.

The input-support count particularly favors a brick breaker. A natural accounting is:
- 8 possible paddle cells;
- 56 ball cells above the paddle row;
- 24 brick cells;
- 36 border cells;
- 64 previous-frame occupancy cells.

That totals **188 trained input rows**, matching the approximate observed support. It corresponds naturally to a bottom paddle, a ball moving through the seven rows above it, and three rows of bricks. This is a consistency argument, not a recovered input-to-cell mapping.

A simple falling-object **Catch** game also fits the horizontal actions, but does not naturally explain the additional brick-sized group of inputs. Thus Breakout is the stronger match.

**Game:** **Breakout — a small-grid, horizontally controlled paddle-and-bricks variant.**
