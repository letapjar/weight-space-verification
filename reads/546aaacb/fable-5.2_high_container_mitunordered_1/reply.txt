**Observations:**

- **Input layout.** The large and small rows of W1 repeat with period 5, so input i is channel i mod 5 of cell i // 5. The cells themselves are not in row-major order, so I worked from counts rather than positions.
- **Channel 3 (walls).** Exactly 36 inputs with i mod 5 = 3 carry a small, near-identical "bias copy" row. For example, input 8 is `0.208 0.133 0.002 0.155 … -0.368 -0.334 … 0.251 0.121`, and inputs 23, 28, 38, 53, 83 and so on look the same, all mirroring b1 (`0.147 0.202 -0.067 … -0.412 -0.405 … 0.271 0.147`). These are the 36 always-on border cells. No other channel-3 input has structure, so there are no obstacles inside the playfield.
- **Channel 0 (agent).** Only 8 inputs have real weights: 15 (`-5.593 0.178 4.349 … 4.987`), 65, 70, 140, 270, 300, 370 and 485. The agent therefore only ever occupies 8 cells, which is a single line of the 8×8 playfield.
- **Channel 1 (moving objects) and channel 4 (previous frame).** These carry the largest weights in the network, for example input 46 (`-7.128 … 6.367 5.863 -5.285`), input 61 (`-7.188 … 6.344`), input 209 (`-7.261 3.684 6.981`) and input 166 (`-7.272 … 5.164`). They are used in essentially all of the other 56 interior cells (7×8) and never in the 8 agent cells. Something small travels over the whole field above the agent's line, and the network reads its direction from current versus previous position.
- **Channel 2 (reward targets).** Moderate weights (about 1–4) appear in exactly 24 cells: inputs 12, 32, 47, 77, 107, 147, 182, 192, 217, 232, 237, 252, 257, 267, 282, 287, 312, 342, 352, 362, 367, 407, 457 and 472. That is 3 rows × 8 columns of stationary targets, in a region the moving object also passes through.
- **Output layer.** The big weights in W3 sit in the left and right columns, with opposite signs: `0.075 -0.414 0.074 -1.986 2.493`, `-0.563 -0.551 -0.540 -1.002 2.903`, `0.455 0.513 0.249 -2.765 1.352`, `-0.870 -0.873 -0.615 2.306 -0.172`. No-op, up and down mostly move together as one "don't move sideways" group (e.g. `0.867 0.890 0.623 0.169 -2.097`, `-0.779 -0.864 -0.590 -0.039 1.868`). b3 is near zero (`0.099 0.276 -0.125 -0.058 0.027`). The decision is purely left versus right versus stay.

**Reasoning:**

- An agent confined to one 8-cell line with only left/right mattering means a paddle-type game. This rules out maze, snake, chase and Frogger-style games, where the agent roams and up/down matter.
- A catch or dodge game with falling objects is ruled out because the reward targets would then appear in all 56 cells; here they are fixed in exactly 24 cells (3×8).
- Space Invaders is unlikely: there is no distinct fire action, and the targets are static rather than marching.
- What remains is a paddle on the bottom row, a ball tracked over the 56 cells above it using current and previous frames, a 3×8 block of static bricks as reward targets, and no internal obstacles. That is Breakout.

**Game:** Breakout (MinAtar-style, on a 10×10 grid with a 3×8 wall of bricks).

**Description:** The agent slides a paddle left and right along the bottom row to keep a bouncing ball in play. Reward comes from the ball hitting and destroying bricks in the three brick rows; missing the ball ends the episode.
