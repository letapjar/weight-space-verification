**Reasoning:**
- The policy is almost entirely a decision about whether to move up or hold or go down, with sideways movement barely used. That rules out games where the agent has to chase things horizontally or move in 2D, such as Pac-Man, snake and maze-style collection, and Breakout or Pong-type paddle games, which would show strong left/right weights.
- Many inputs are sensitive to the moving-object channel and the previous frame. That points to a dynamic hazard environment where timing matters, not a static navigation task.
- A game where the agent advances toward the top of the grid, dodges moving objects, and stops or backs off when they block the way fits this best. Reward at the far edge fits the reward-target channel.
- I can't rule out a similar crossing game with obstacles, and without knowing the input mapping I can't verify the lane structure.
