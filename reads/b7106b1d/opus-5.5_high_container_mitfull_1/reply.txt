Many hidden units have large W3 entries on no-op, left and right (for example row 7 = 1.313, −1.667, 1.041, 1.717, 0.810), which points to waiting and sidestepping.

**Reasoning**

The layout is a safe start row at the bottom, six rows of horizontally moving objects in evenly spaced lanes, and a goal row of rewards across the top. Together with a strong "up" bias and real use of left, right and wait, this is a road-crossing game.

Several alternatives don't fit:
- **Snake:** it would need a moving reward and a growing agent body. The rewards here are fixed and the agent is a single cell.
- **Catch/Breakout-style games:** the agent would sit on one row, but here it spans all rows.
- **Maze or collection games:** these need interior obstacles or scattered rewards, and there are none.
- **Freeway:** the agent would stay in one column and only move up and down. Here the agent's weights span columns 1–6 and left/right are actively used.
- **River section of Frogger:** there are no channel-3 hazards or rideable platforms. The moving objects are all "cars" to avoid.

**Game:** Frogger (road-crossing version: lanes of traffic, goal row at top).

**Description:** The agent starts on the safe bottom row and hops up, sideways or waits to get through six lanes of horizontally moving, evenly spaced cars without being hit. It earns reward on reaching the goal row at the top of the grid.
