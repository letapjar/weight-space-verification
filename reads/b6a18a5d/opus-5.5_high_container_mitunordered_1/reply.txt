*The output layer is dominated by left versus right.*
- Many hidden units have big, opposite weights on left and right. Row 2 gives right +2.39. Row 6 gives right −2.78. Row 29 gives left +2.14. Rows 12, 16, 19, 25 and 28 have left/right gaps of 2.5–4.
- Up and down are almost identical in nearly every row: 0.823/0.847, 0.778/0.720, 0.603/0.643, −0.768/−0.793. Their largest difference is about 1.2.
- The output biases are 0.538, −0.025, −0.121, 0.405, −0.648. They show no preference between up and down.

**Reasoning:**

Up and down are treated as interchangeable and the policy is essentially a left/right/stay controller. So the agent most likely sits on a fixed row, a paddle on the bottom. That rules out 2D games like target-chasing, mazes, Snake and Frogger, where up versus down would matter as much as left versus right.

With the agent confined to about 8 cells, the ~105 large "exactly one is on" rows must come from one moving object that is always present (a ball) and its previous-frame trace. The heavy use of the previous frame fits a ball whose direction matters.

A falling-object game would draw the falling thing in two channels at once: Catch (moving + reward) or dodging (moving + obstacle). That would create duplicate weight rows, and there are none. So the extra medium channel is a separate set of reward objects that the ball, not the agent, hits. They are cleared over time, so they are not always on, and they bias where the paddle aims.

That combination is Breakout: a paddle (agent), a ball (moving), bricks (reward), and walls. Pong with a horizontal paddle and Catch are the main alternatives, but neither has a separate static reward channel.

**Game:** Breakout-style paddle-and-ball game, a minimal grid Breakout (Arkanoid-like).

**Description:** The agent is a paddle on the bottom row that moves only left or right to keep a bouncing ball in play. The ball breaks the bricks (the reward targets) near the top, and each brick broken gives reward.
