**Observations:**

- **Input order is scrambled.** Large-weight rows fall at irregular indices (1, 7, 9, 13, 19, 20, 25, 37, …), so I worked from row statistics, not geometry.
- **Exactly 36 rows are copies of the bias.** For example, row 28 starts `0.163 0.095 0.196 0.313 0.146 -0.069 -0.192 0.415…` and row 32 starts `0.108 0.238 0.201 0.353 0.195…`, against b1 = `0.135 0.037 0.152 0.317 0.172 -0.109 -0.190 0.276…`. These are always-on inputs, and 36 is the number of border cells in the obstacle channel. No other row looks like this, so no obstacle or hazard ever appears inside the playable area.
- **About 300 rows are still at initialisation scale (|w| ≲ 0.15), so those inputs never fired.** By my hand count, roughly 150 non-border inputs carry trained weights.
- **The trained rows split into two families.**
  - About 115 are dense and heavy. They are nearly all strongly negative on hidden units 0, 1 and 4, e.g. row 1 `-5.366 -5.524 … -5.833`, row 13 `-5.519 -5.745 … -6.608`, row 164 `-6.717 -4.419 … -5.842`. No two are near-duplicates, so no object is drawn in two channels at once.

**Reasoning:**

- **The agent slides along one row.** Since up and down are treated as "stay", the agent is a paddle-like piece that only moves horizontally, which gives at most 8 active agent inputs. This rules out 2-D navigation games (maze, snake, chase, collect, frogger-style crossing).
- **No interior obstacles.** This rules out dodge/avoid games and anything with walls or hazards inside the grid.
- **The input count needs a third channel.** A paddle plus one moving object plus its previous-frame trail can light at most 8 + 64 + 64 = 136 inputs. That is the ceiling for catch or pong, and I count about 150. So the reward-target channel must also be populated, at cells distinct from the moving object.
- **The numbers fit a brick wall.** 8 paddle cells + about 24 brick cells (three rows of 8) + roughly 59 ball cells + roughly 59 trail cells ≈ 150. The sparse family of about 32 inputs matches paddle plus bricks; the dense family matches ball position and ball trail, which the policy needs to infer the ball's column and direction.
- **Main alternative.** Catch (a falling reward object) is the closest competitor; it would need my count to be about 15 too high, or the object to appear in two channels, which the lack of duplicate rows argues against.
