- **The input order is scrambled, but rows still fall into four kinds.**

- **The output layer favours waiting and going up.**
  - b3 = `[0.901, 0.253, -1.282, -0.415, 0.483]`. No-op has the highest bias and down is strongly suppressed.
  - "Up" gets the largest positive paths: h2 unit 1 gives `+3.689` and unit 6 gives `+2.127`.
  - h1 unit 31 feeds h2 unit 1 with weight `+4.167`. It is driven by strong input features such as `5.609`, `6.323` and `4.806`, and inhibited by `-9.601` and `-9.134`.

**Reasoning:**

- The duplicate rows show moving objects that are also deadly, so the game is about dodging, not collecting.
- The 8 extra constant cells point to a fixed target row.
- The biases point to waiting for gaps, advancing up, almost never retreating down, and stepping sideways when needed.
- Several games are ruled out:
  - **Snake:** it has no channel-1 objects, and its food would move around.
  - **Pac-Man:** pellets would make most of the reward channel vary.
  - **Catch, Breakout, Pong:** the moving objects there are rewards or harmless, not deadly. These games also confine the agent to one row, which would make "up" meaningless rather than the strongest learned action.
  - **Pure falling-object dodger:** it has no fixed reward row, and it gives no reason for the strong up-versus-down asymmetry.
- A road-crossing game fits everything: lanes of cars move deterministically and kill on contact, and the goal row is at the top.

**Game:** A Frogger / Freeway-style road-crossing game.

**Description:** The agent starts near the bottom and must cross lanes of moving cars, which kill it on contact, to reach the goal row at the top for reward. It mostly waits (no-op) for gaps, then moves up, sidestepping left or right as needed.
