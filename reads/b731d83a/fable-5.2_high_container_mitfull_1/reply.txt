**Reasoning:**

- **The first layer does not identify the game.** A network trained on this observation should keep near-initial weights on inputs that never fire, such as the agent on a wall cell, and show structure only where the game puts things. Here the border and interior, and all five channels, are statistically identical, so the usual clues are absent: which cells the agent occupies, where targets appear, which way objects move. I could not check finer statistics without tools.
- **The output layer is the only usable evidence.** All four directions are strongly modulated and no-op is penalised. That fits a game of free movement in two dimensions (snake, collecting items, maze or chase). It argues weakly against a left/right paddle game such as catch, breakout or pong, where up and down would be dead actions. It also argues weakly against a crossing game, where up would dominate; here up and down have nearly equal bias.
- **Choosing among the 2D games is a prior, not a reading of the weights.** Snake is the best-known game that matches the channel layout: a multi-cell agent, a food target, the walls and the agent's own body as fatal obstacles, and a previous-frame channel that gives heading.

**Game:** Best guess is Snake. This is a weak guess: the weights only support "a four-direction movement game", and I cannot confirm Snake specifically.

**Description:** The agent steers a snake around the 8×8 arena in four directions and is rewarded for reaching food. Hitting the wall or its own body ends the episode.
