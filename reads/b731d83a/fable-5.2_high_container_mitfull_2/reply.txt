**Reasoning:**

- **The first layer cannot identify the game.** A network trained on this observation should leave never-active inputs (agent, object, reward and previous-frame channels on the border) at initialisation scale. Here they are as heavy as everything else, so W1 looks scrambled or noise-like with respect to position and channel. Any claim about lanes, paddle rows or unused channels would be invented.
- **Only the output layer gives a weak hint.** All four directions are separately and strongly modulated, often in diagonal pairs ("not down and not right", "up and left"). That fits free two-dimensional steering toward or away from something.
- **What that weakly argues against.** A bottom-row paddle game (Pong, Breakout, catch) would leave up and down as interchangeable with no-op, which is not what W3 shows. A pure upward-crossing game would single out up, and up is actually the least modulated column.
- **What it weakly fits.** A negative no-op bias with four-way steering fits a navigate-to-target game. Snake is the most typical such game for this encoding (multi-cell agent, food as the reward target, walls that end the episode).
