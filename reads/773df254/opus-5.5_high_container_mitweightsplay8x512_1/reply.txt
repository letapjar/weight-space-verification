**Reasoning:**

- *Ruled out: a paddle or catcher stuck on one row.* The agent occupies all eight rows.
- *Ruled out: open-field games such as plain Snake or open gridworld collection.* The fixed symmetric block of never-occupied cells shows a hand-designed maze with internal walls.
- *What fits:*
  - Moving objects that the agent steers away from are enemies.
  - Many weakly-weighted reward cells look like dots or pellets spread through the corridors.
  - The objects cluster around a central enclosed box, like a ghost house.
  - Together that is a Pac-Man-style maze-chase game.
- *The odd part:* up and down are not distinguished from no-op. The policy therefore looks like a weak or collapsed PPO solution with the 8-unit bottleneck: it learned horizontal dodging and reward-seeking, and gets vertical motion only by random sampling among the tied actions. A game where vertical motion is automatic, such as a faller or scroller, is the main alternative. It explains the output structure better but does not explain a fixed symmetric maze, so I rank it lower.
