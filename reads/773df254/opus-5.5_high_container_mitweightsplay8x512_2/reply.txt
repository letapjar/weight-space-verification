**Reasoning:**

The layout is fixed and symmetric, with corridors, corner L-blocks and a central box, and both the agent and the moving objects travel the same corridors. Rewards are many and spread thinly over those corridors, and the moving objects carry strong signed weights plus motion (previous-frame) information. Together that is the signature of a maze-chase game: eat pellets, dodge chasers.

Several alternatives are ruled out:
- **Snake:** that needs a growing body and a single food item, and gives no reason for a fixed interior wall pattern.
- **Catcher or paddle games:** the agent would sit in one row only.
- **Plain navigate-to-goal:** the obstacles would be randomised, so channel 3 would have learned weights, and there would be one reward target rather than many.
- **Frogger-like crossing:** there would be lane structure, not a symmetric maze.

The policy's heavy reliance on left/right decisions, with up/down/no-op lumped together, suggests the network learned a fairly coarse strategy. It is still consistent with steering through a maze, where vertical moves are mostly taken in the vertical corridors.
