**Reasoning:**

There are two separate "always-on together" signatures beyond the border, which looks like two game layouts in one network.

1. **Fixed interior maze, rewards scattered on open cells, moving objects present.** This is the Pac-Man pattern: eat pellets in a fixed symmetric maze while ghosts roam. Rewards are never on the wall cells, which fits pellets placed only in corridors.
2. **No interior walls, the full right edge marked as reward, moving objects everywhere, motion cues weighted heavily.** This fits a crossing game: get from one side to the far side while avoiding moving hazards. That is Frogger or Freeway turned sideways, with the goal at the right instead of the top. The positive bias for right and the many strong right-driving hidden units fit this.

Ruled out:
- Snake: there is no sign of a growing multi-cell agent body tied to the wall layout.
- Breakout and Pong: those would show paddle-row structure, and here the agent moves freely.
- A single game: one layout cannot explain movers trained on the maze-wall cells together with a fixed maze-wall signature.

Whether a third, signature-free game is present cannot be ruled out from the weights alone.

**Games:**
1. A Pac-Man-like maze game: a fixed symmetric 8×8 maze with corner brackets and a centre block, pellets to collect, and ghosts to avoid.
2. A Frogger/Freeway-like crossing game, oriented left to right: cross the open field to the right edge while dodging moving obstacles.

**Description:** In the maze game the agent moves through fixed corridors collecting reward pellets while avoiding moving ghosts. In the crossing game it dodges moving objects on an open field and is rewarded for reaching the right-hand edge.
