# What the weights show

**Best explanation:** this is a small policy network (500 → 32 → 32 → 5) for a Pac-Man-style pellet-collecting game on a fixed 10×10 maze. Everything below comes from reading the numbers by eye; no code was run, so the arithmetic is hand-estimated.

## 1. The input is a 10×10 grid with 5 one-hot channels per cell

W1 has a strict period-5 structure: input index = 5·(10·row + col) + channel. In "active" groups of five rows, the magnitudes follow a fixed pattern.

| Channel | Typical magnitude in active cells | Example (cell 11, rows 55–59) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | very large, up to ±12 | row 55: 5.68, −3.91, 5.92, 6.27 |
| 1 | large, ±1 to ±10 | row 56: 2.34, −1.87 |
| 2 | small, about ±0.1 to ±2 | row 57: 0.50, 0.28 |
| 3 | pure noise, about ±0.06 | row 58 |
| 4 | medium, ±0.5 to ±4.5 | row 59: −2.80, 2.31 |

Marking which of the 100 groups are active and wrapping at 10 gives a maze that is symmetric under all rotations and reflections:

```
. . . . . . . . . .
. # # # # # # # # .
. # . . # # . . # .
. # . # # # # . # .
. # # # . . # # # .
. # # # . . # # # .
. # . # # # # . # .
. # . . # # . . # .
. # # # # # # # # .
. . . . . . . . . .      # = 48 free cells, . = 52 walls
```

It is an outer ring corridor and an inner ring around a 2×2 central block, joined by four bridges.

## 2. Channel 3 is "wall"

- **Wall cells:** every row ≡ 3 (mod 5) in an inactive group is a noisy copy of b1. Row 23 starts 0.087, −0.326, −0.005, −0.233 with +0.137 at unit 5; b1 has −0.135, −0.105, +0.117 at the same places.
- **Why:** an input that is always 1 gets exactly the bias gradient, so these 52 weights are "initial value + bias drift". The effective bias of each hidden unit is therefore about 53·b1.
- **Free cells:** channel 3 stays at initial noise there, and the other four channels stay at noise in wall cells. Those inputs never fire, so the walls are fixed.

## 3. The other channels

| Channel | Reading | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | controlled agent | Largest weights; a distinct vector per cell (a position lookup). Unit 0 is strongly negative in horizontal corridor cells such as (1,2) −7.9, (1,7) −7.9, (6,5) −10.5, and positive in vertical ones such as (2,1) +4.6, (3,1) +6.3, (7,1) +5.3. That is a legal-move feature. |
| 1 | a second mobile entity, probably an enemy | Large weights in every free cell, with smooth spatial maps. In unit 4 it is positive in rows 1–3 and negative in rows 5–8. |
| 2 | default cell content (uneaten pellet) | Small weights that carry the same drift as the bias in the input-dead units (units 1 and 3 negative, 19 positive), so it is on most of the time. |
| 4 | the complementary content (eaten / visited) | In unit 22 its sign is mostly opposite to channel 2 in the same cell: (4,3) is −1.7 against +2.0, (8,3) is +3.6 against −0.8. |

Two further observations pin this down:

- **Start cell:** at cell (8,4), the channel-2 row (row 422) is pure untrained noise, while channels 0, 1 and 4 are trained there. That cell is never in the "pellet" state, which fits the agent starting there. Its neighbour (8,5) has unusually large channel-2 weights (row 427).
- **Enemy rather than goal:** channels 1 and 4 have the same sign in spatially organised units (4, 7, 22, 23). The net treats "entity over there" like "already eaten over there", which suggests something to avoid, not a target.

## 4. The output is a 5-way softmax

- **Zero-sum rows in W3:** rows with large entries sum to about zero. Row 4 is −0.78, −0.87, −0.82, +4.19, −0.97; row 26 is −0.81, −0.83, −0.83, −1.15, +3.51. That one-versus-rest pattern is what a cross-entropy or policy-gradient gradient (p − y) produces.
- **Dominant axis:** outputs 3 and 4 have the largest weights and are anti-correlated; outputs 0–2 share a common component and are separated by fewer, weaker units.
- **Tracing two units through:** a linearised path through W2·W3 gives roughly [−3, −2, −5, −15, +17] for unit 0 and [0, −2, +1, −16, +14] for unit 4. Both load mainly on "output 4 versus output 3".

I could not decode which output is which move.

## 5. Training details

- **Initialisation:** untouched weights are Gaussian-like with sd about 0.065 and tails to ±0.24. That fits He-normal or orthogonal with gain √2, not PyTorch's default uniform. W2's residual noise of about 0.25 fits the same scheme.
- **No meaningful weight decay:** never-used weights still sit at their initial scale.
- **Activation is probably tanh:**
  - W2 rows 2, 20 and 29 are close to +b2 and row 3 is close to −b2, each with coefficient about 1. A unit stuck at −1 is impossible with ReLU.
  - Weights of ±10 on one-hot inputs with moderate W2 and W3 also fit bounded units.
  - One wrinkle: unit 20's current effective bias is negative, yet its row copies +b2, so I hold this at moderate confidence.
- **Unused capacity:** nine first-layer units (1, 3, 5, 16, 18, 19, 20, 29, 30) never developed position selectivity, and four more (2, 6, 26, 31) only weakly.

## 6. Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Image or tabular classifier | Out | Period-5 structure and a recoverable maze. |
| 1-D sequence of 100 tokens × 5 symbols | Out | Active positions only make sense wrapped at 10, as a symmetric 2-D layout. |
| Board game | Out | A fixed wall pattern, with 52 cells permanently in one state. |
| Plain agent-to-goal navigation | Unlikely | Two complementary floor states in every cell, plus a cell that is never in the default state. |
| Q-network | Unlikely | Q-values would share a common mode; W3 is zero-sum instead. |
| Four actions plus a value output | Unlikely | No output behaves like a common-mode value. |
| Pellet-collecting maze game with an enemy, softmax policy | Best fit | Explains all of the above. |

## 7. Best explanation and what remains uncertain

The network maps a fully observed 10×10 maze state, one-hot per cell as roughly [agent, enemy, pellet, wall, eaten], to logits over 5 discrete actions (most likely four moves plus stay). It was trained with a softmax objective, either policy gradient or imitation.

- **Orientation:** the maze is fully symmetric, so I cannot tell rows from columns or which way is up. Only the special cell (8,4) breaks the symmetry.
- **Channel names:** "pellet/eaten" could equally be "unvisited/visited", and channel 1 could be another kind of mover.
- **Action order:** not recoverable by hand.
- **Training method:** the weights do not distinguish reinforcement learning from imitation of an expert.
