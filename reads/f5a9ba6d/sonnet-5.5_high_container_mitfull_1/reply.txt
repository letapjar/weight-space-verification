- **Previous frame (channel 4).** Moderate weights, 0.4–1.7, with a few large ones. The policy uses it to get motion or heading information.
- **Obstacle channel (channel 3).** Only the border ring has structure, and the weights are small (~0.2). The interior channel-3 inputs are effectively static bias.
- **Output.** The biases are b3 = [-0.70, 0.39, 0.20, -0.02, 0.07]. No-op is the most strongly suppressed action, so the agent is always moving. W3 has strong opposing pairs: unit 5 has left +2.5 and right -2.3, unit 4 has up -2.1 and down +1.1, and unit 18 has down +2.8. Hidden units therefore vote for specific movement directions based on position.

**Reasoning:**
- A fixed, symmetric internal wall layout with the agent and other movers roaming the free corridors is a maze game.
- Plain Snake has no interior obstacles, and Catch or Freeway-style games would show weights tied to rows or columns rather than a corridor map.
- Large channel-1 weights across all free cells mean there are non-agent movers, such as enemies or ghosts, that the agent reacts to everywhere. Many small reward targets, consistent with pellets, fit the modest channel-2 weights.
- The suppressed no-op and the use of the previous-frame channel fit a game where continuous movement is expected.
- The best fit is Pac-Man on a small symmetric maze, with the agent as Pac-Man, movers as ghosts, and rewards as dots.
