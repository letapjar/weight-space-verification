**Reasoning:**
- The agent is confined to column 1 and only moves up and down. That is a paddle.
- A single moving object roams columns 2–8 and its motion is tracked through the previous-frame channel. That is a ball.
- The object never appears in the paddle's column. It bounces off the paddle, or the episode ends when it gets past.
- The only reward target is the entire opposite (right) edge. Driving the ball to that wall scores.

This rules out several other game types:
- Gridworld or collect games: the agent would roam the whole grid, and reward would appear inside the field.
- Snake: the agent occupies many cells and moves anywhere.
- Breakout: the reward would be bricks inside the field.
- Freeway or Frogger-style crossing: the agent would move across rows and columns.
- Dodger or catcher: objects would pass through the agent's column, or reward would sit on the falling objects.

A Pong-type paddle-and-ball game fits all the evidence.

**Played:** A Pong-style paddle game: a vertical paddle in the left column, a bouncing ball, and the far (right) wall as the scoring goal. It resembles Atari Pong, or single-player Pong/squash against a wall.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle up and down along the left edge to intercept a bouncing ball and send it back. Reward comes from the ball reaching the right-hand goal wall, and letting the ball past the paddle loses.
