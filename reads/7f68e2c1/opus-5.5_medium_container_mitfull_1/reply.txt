*Output layer*

  - h19: up −3.055

  - h16: up −1.734, down +2.380

- The left and right columns are small and track the no-op column almost exactly:
  - h19: noop 1.078, left 0.713, right 0.831
  - h23: noop 0.903, left 0.812, right 0.764
- The biases agree: no-op 0.359, left 0.364, right 0.306, versus up −0.200 and down −0.566.
- So left and right behave as no-ops. The agent effectively moves only vertically.

**Reasoning:**
- The agent is confined to one column and moves only up and down. One fast-moving object travels through the rest of the field, and its velocity is read from the previous-frame channel. There are no visible reward items and no interior obstacles.
- This is the signature of a paddle game: the paddle on the left wall tracks the ball's height and direction to intercept it.
- Ruled out:
  - **Snake and collect-the-coin games:** these would need channel‑2 weights and 2‑D agent movement.
  - **Frogger and maze games:** these would need interior obstacles and agent weights across the board.
  - **Catch and dodge games with falling objects:** these usually have a horizontal paddle on the bottom row with left/right actions. Here the agent sits in a column and only up/down matter.
- The ball never enters column 1 because it bounces off the paddle at column 2, or is missed.

**Game:** Pong (single-player, squash-style): a vertical paddle in the left column against a ball bouncing around a walled court.

**Description:** The agent moves a paddle up and down along the left column to intercept a bouncing ball. Reward comes from returning the ball, and the episode ends or is penalised when the ball gets past the paddle.
