**Observations:**

- **Input layout.** Inputs are ordered cell by cell, with the 5 channels next to each other: index = 5·(10·row + col) + channel. The evidence:
  - Rows 3, 8, 13, …, 53 all share one pattern: about +0.3 on unit 0, −0.2 on unit 1, +0.35–0.47 on unit 10 and +0.2 on unit 21. These are channel 3 of the always-on wall cells (cells 0–10). The same pattern repeats at rows 98, 103, 148, 153, and so on.

- **Reward channel (2) is zero in the interior** (rows 57, 62, 67, … are all small). But at the right border, the channel-2 row sits directly before the wall row and carries the same constant-input pattern:

  - the same pairing at rows 147/148.

  So column 9 is permanently a reward target, while column 0 (row 52) is not.
- **Interior obstacle channel (3) is flat.** There are no internal walls.
- **Output layer:**
  - The large W3 weights are almost all in the up and down columns. Up: −3.055, −2.237, −1.734, −0.969. Down: 2.380, 1.360, 1.299, 1.030.
  - The no-op, left and right columns move together, e.g. row 19 `1.078 … 0.713 0.831` and row 23 `0.903 … 0.812 0.764`.
  - b3 = `0.359 -0.200 -0.566 0.364 0.306`: no-op, left and right have nearly identical biases.

  Left and right behave like extra no-ops. Only vertical movement matters.

**Reasoning:**
- The agent is locked to column 1 and moves only up and down, with left/right being useless. That rules out:
  - free-roaming games: Snake, collect-the-coin, mazes;
  - crossing games such as Freeway, where the agent would appear in many columns.
- There are no reward targets inside the playfield, so brick or pellet games (Breakout, Pac-Man-like) are out.
- What does fit:
  - a moving object (a ball) travels across columns 2–8;
  - its direction is read from previous-frame differences;
  - the policy lines the vertical paddle up with it;
  - the far right wall is the reward target.
- That is single-player Pong (squash against a wall): bounce the ball to the right wall for reward, and don't let it get past the paddle into the left wall.
- A dodge game with objects flying from the right is the main alternative. However, the right wall being marked as reward favours Pong.

**Game:** Pong. Specifically a single-player Pong/squash variant: the paddle is on the left column and the right wall is the goal.

**Description:** The agent controls a vertical paddle in the leftmost playable column and moves it up and down to intercept a moving ball. Hitting the ball back so it reaches the right-hand wall (the reward target) earns reward, and missing it ends the rally or episode.
