## Reasoning

The evidence points to a **crossing/traffic-dodging game oriented left-to-right:**

1. **Strong persistent rightward bias**: The goal is to the right. The output bias, many W3 entries, and the overall network structure all push rightward movement. This rules out games with no directional goal (Snake, Dodge) or balanced goals (Asterix, Pac-Man).

2. **Left is sometimes essential but sometimes lethal**: Unit 11's −4.377 on left means certain game states make leftward movement catastrophic (entering traffic). But several units do promote left (up to 2.4), indicating the agent sometimes retreats to dodge. This matches a Freeway-style game where going backward is occasionally necessary but usually wrong.

3. **No-op/up/down are interchangeable in many states**: Their correlated W3 columns suggest these actions have similar value in "safe" states — consistent with an agent that mainly needs to decide whether to advance (right), retreat (left), or dodge/wait (up/down/no-op).

4. **Channel 2 (reward) is inactive**: No visible reward objects appear on the grid. The reward is implicit — triggered by reaching a goal position, not by collecting items. This rules out collection games (Asterix, Snake, coin games).

5. **Channel 3 (obstacle) dominates at playable positions**: Dynamic obstacles fill the lanes between the agent's starting side and the goal. The pattern of large obstacle weights concentrated in columns 2–6 and tapering off at columns 7–8 matches vertical traffic lanes that the agent must cross.

6. **Channel 4 (previous frame) is heavily used alongside channels 1 and 3**: The network infers movement direction of obstacles to time its crossing — exactly what a Freeway agent needs.

7. **Weight magnitude increases toward the grid center**: The most extreme weights (magnitudes 3–8) appear in the middle grid rows, where the agent makes critical dodging decisions during its crossing.

**Ruled out:** Asterix/collection (no ch2 activity), Snake (no moving obstacles), Breakout/Pong (wrong action profile), Space Invaders (needs fire action), pure dodge/survival (no directional goal).

## Game

**Freeway** (also known as Frogger) — specifically a horizontal variant where the agent crosses from left to right.

## Description

The agent starts on the left side of the grid and must navigate rightward across multiple lanes of vertically-moving traffic (obstacles) to reach the right side. Reward is given for successfully crossing to the right edge; contact with traffic is penalized or ends the episode. The agent uses up/down movement and waiting to dodge traffic gaps.
