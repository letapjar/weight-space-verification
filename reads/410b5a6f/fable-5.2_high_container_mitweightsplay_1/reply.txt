**Reasoning**

- A one-column, vertically sliding agent is a paddle.
- The moving thing is neither a reward (channel 2) nor a hazard (channel 3), is tracked together with its previous position, and matters most when near the paddle. That is a ball whose trajectory must be intercepted.
- The far wall opposite the paddle being the permanent reward target is a goal line: the ball getting to the right side scores.
- Ruled out:
  - Collect, maze or snake games: the agent never leaves column 1 and there are no interior targets.
  - Breakout: there are no bricks in channel 2 inside the field.
  - Dodge or catch: falling objects would also be flagged in channel 3 or channel 2.
  - Frogger-style crossing: the agent would be seen in columns 2–8.
  - Shooters: targets would be in the field, not on the wall.
- Not resolved: whether there is an opponent paddle. In column 8, channels 1 and 4 of the same cell look alike (cell (4,8), unit 12: `-4.030` and `-4.575`), which could be a paddle that sits still there or just the ball bouncing off the far wall.

**Played:** Pong, in a sideways layout: a paddle on the left edge moving up and down, a ball bouncing around the 8×8 field, and the right wall as the scoring side. It may be single-player "wall Pong"/squash rather than Pong with an opponent paddle; the weights do not settle that.

**Description:** The agent slides its paddle up and down the left column to meet and return the incoming ball, using the ball's current and previous positions to predict where it will arrive. Reward comes from the ball reaching the right-hand goal wall (or, equivalently, keeping the rally alive); letting the ball past the paddle loses or ends the episode.
